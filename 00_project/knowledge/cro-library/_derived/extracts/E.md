# Extract E (files 054-066)

Note: 055-059 and 066 are client testimonial videos for the agency ThrillX. They hold little CRO method. They are used for trust and testimonial rules only.

## Rules
### Hero / above the fold
- IF you plan effort for a page THEN spend 80-90% of it on the top section (above the fold). Why: it is the only part every visitor sees. Src: 054 "focus 80 to 90% of your effort on the topmost section"
- IF a visitor lands THEN state who you are, what you do and what is in it for them within 5 seconds. Why: visitors decide fast and the "what is in it for me" part is the one most pages miss. Src: 060 "who you are what you do and what's in it for me"
- IF you doubt that the hero matters THEN remember 60% never scroll past it, but 100% see it. Why: content below the hero reaches fewer than half of the visitors. Src: 065 "the only area that 100% of your users will see"
- IF the hero headline only names the service or the brand THEN rewrite it to give the emotional or practical benefit. Why: people already know what they clicked for; they need the reason to act. Src: 064 "tell me why Tell me the emotional benefit"
- IF a page has a hero THEN give it one headline, one sub headline that explains how, and one clear CTA. Why: one path reduces confusion. Src: 054 "have one clear call to action"
- IF the headline is catchy but vague THEN replace it with a plain benefit headline. Why: clear beats clever and most visitors read the headline only. Src: 061 "make it clear instead of clever"
- IF the headline can show a number THEN quantify the benefit or the speed. Why: numbers make the benefit concrete. HWG: quantify service facts (reply time, years, case count), never a healing result or a time to cure. Src: 062 "when you can quantify results and really communicate that end benefit"
- IF you write the sub headline THEN name the pain first and follow with the solution. Why: the pain-then-solution order keeps attention and shows you understand. Src: 062 "stop doing X no more Y or painoint follow up with the solution"
- IF the hero text has many lines and bullets THEN cut to one short paragraph or three bullets. Why: stacked text hides the social proof and the benefit. Src: 063 "I'd cut down the four bullet points to three"
- IF the mobile headline wraps to two lines without need THEN shorten it to one line. Why: it wastes the fold on the screen where most visitors are. Src: 063 "pushing down the average fold"
- IF you add a hero image or video THEN make it show the outcome or the emotion the headline promises. Why: the image must reinforce the value proposition. A lone founder photo or a dog eating food does not. Src: 064 "reinforce your core value prop"
- IF the traffic comes from search or ads for a known problem THEN put the lead form or contact action in the hero. Why: the visitor already knows the need; the page just has to remove steps. Src: 064 "putting a form above the fold"

### Headlines and copy
- IF a section headline is generic (Our services, What people say, Testimonials) THEN write the benefit in the headline. Why: most visitors read headlines only. Src: 065 "communicates the benefit in the headline itself"
- IF you write any section THEN assume the reader skims: only about 20% is read. Why: headline and bullets must carry the sale alone. Src: 063 "people just read 20% of the content"
- IF a headline repeats the brand name or the word "Contact" THEN replace it with a reason to act. Why: the visitor already knows where they are. Src: 065 "do not make your headline contact us or let's get started"
- IF you write feature text THEN pair each feature with its benefit. Why: the benefit tells why to care. Src: 054 "combine the feature which is what someone gets with the benefit"
- IF copy has jargon THEN remove it. Why: understanding speeds the decision. Src: 061 "how can you get rid of jargon"
- IF you do not know what the customer wants THEN ask real customers about pains and goals before writing. Why: guesses give weak messaging. Src: 062 "you would actually have to interview your customers"

### Pain, solution and objections
- IF you write a pain section THEN pair the pain with your solution and your unique benefit. Why: a pain list alone only says what the visitor hates. Src: 064 "tying in your unique benefit"
- IF the traffic has high intent (search, local maps) THEN put social proof before the pain section. Why: these visitors need trust more than education. Src: 064 "put the social proof above the painoint section"
- IF the traffic is cold or unaware (social, ads) THEN educate first and build trust and rapport before the ask. Why: they do not know the problem or you. Src: 065 "educating building trust and rapport quickly"
- IF visitors are anxious about the first step THEN list their fears (cost, trust, data safety, what happens next) and answer each near the CTA. Why: anxieties live in support tickets and sales calls. Src: 065 "it's in your support tickets"
- IF you write an FAQ THEN base the questions on fear and uncertainty (Is it free? What is the catch?). Why: the FAQ removes the last doubts. Src: 062 "based on fear and uncertainty"

### CTA
- IF the main CTA sits only at the bottom THEN move it above the fold. Why: the action must be visible at the first glance. Src: 064 "should just be moved up here"
- IF you write the CTA label THEN make it benefit-oriented and clear. Why: it should say what the visitor gets. Src: 065 "stands out on the page and is benefit oriented"
- IF the page has several sections THEN add a CTA button to each section. Why: visitors convert at any stage. Src: 065 "each of these sections should have a CTA button"
- IF a CTA sits at the critical action point THEN put fear-reducing lines right under it (reply time, cost, what happens next). Why: this is where doubts peak. Src: 064 "response in less than 10 minutes"
- IF an audience has two or more clear segments THEN split the CTA into one button per segment. Why: each visitor goes straight to their path. Src: 063 "make this a two-step CTA"
- IF the CTA is a calendar booking as first step THEN offer a simpler first step (short form, call, message). Why: booking is high friction at first contact. Src: 064 "adding so much friction by getting people to book an appointment"
- IF the closing section exists THEN make it a mini hero: reason to act today, three value points, CTA, social proof, image. Why: it is the last chance to convert. Src: 064 "almost like a mini above the fold"
- IF the mobile page has a primary action THEN keep a sticky CTA and phone number. Why: the action stays one tap away. Src: 054 "sticky call to action and phone number"

### Social proof and trust
- IF the hero has no proof THEN add social proof in the hero. Why: it gives a reason to scroll. Src: 060 "social proof above the fold"
- IF proof sits only near the page bottom THEN move it to the top. Why: scroll maps show most visitors never reach it. Src: 060 "testimonials are all the way down towards the bottom"
- IF you show proof in the hero THEN stack two or three types (rating, quote, press). Why: more up-front credibility raises conversion. Src: 065 "we call stacking social proof"
- IF your proof is a row of logos THEN add a short quote from the source. Why: repeated logos are overdone and give little credibility. Src: 063 "add in a quote from the publication"
- IF the proof is a generic quote THEN replace it with one that fits the offer. Why: a quote usable for any business proves nothing. Src: 064 "give me something specific to"
- IF the lead proof is a rating THEN place the rating at the very top. Why: it is seen first and builds trust before the value proposition. Src: 063 "the 1.5k Plus festar reviews it's right at the very top"
- IF you write a testimonial block THEN show stars, the review, a real photo, name and role, and the platform (e.g. Google). Why: text alone looks fakeable. Src: 062 "put a photo of the actual person"
- IF a review is long THEN lead with a short headline of its main point. Why: many visitors skip the body. Src: 062 "lead with the most impactful aspect of the review"
- IF you have video testimonials THEN use them as the first proof block after the pain section. Why: video is the strongest proof. Src: 062 "video testimonials are really powerful"
- IF you name the proof section THEN quantify it (Trusted by 75,000 plus ...) instead of "Testimonials". Why: a number gives a reason to trust. Src: 064 "trusted by 75,000 plus"
- IF you plan the page THEN spread two to three proof blocks across it. Why: trust must be rebuilt at each decision point. Src: 064 "two to three pieces of social proof per page"
- IF the page has a form or booking step THEN put a review at the point of conversion. Why: proof at the form pushes the hesitant visitor over. Src: 061 "reviews and testimonials at that form itself"
- IF a testimonial is used THEN prefer concrete, named, specific detail over praise. Why: concrete facts persuade. HWG: for health sites use service experience (clear talk, care, wait time), never a healing result. Src: 066 "barely getting one sale per day"
- IF you show proof per case THEN show before and after of the situation, not only a quote. Why: transformation stories prove the change. HWG: no before/after healing images or claims. Src: 064 "showing the before and after transformation"

### Offer, pricing and risk reversal
- IF you list packages or treatment options THEN limit them to about three. Why: too many options cause no choice. Test: 5 options to 3 gave +56% conversion. Src: 060 "Paradox of choice"
- IF the price is seen as high THEN build trust and benefit before the price appears. Why: customers fixate on price when they see it first. Src: 060 "before you even see the price"
- IF you show a step-by-step process THEN keep it to about three easy steps tied to a benefit. Why: it makes success feel likely and effortless. Src: 064 "start living in comfort in three steps"
- IF you state a policy or guarantee THEN place it next to the action control. Why: it removes fear at the key moment. Src: 065 "right underneath their CTA button"

### Section order
- IF you build a page THEN follow a fixed order: hero, proof, pain+solution, proof, value propositions (several), proof, how it works, FAQ, closer. Why: this order copies a sales call. Src: 064 "just like a sales call you don't jump straight into the pitch"
- IF you list value propositions THEN use four to five sections, each with a benefit headline. Why: the page acts as a salesperson. Src: 065 "four to five sections"
- IF the page has a differentiator section THEN write the benefit in the headline, not the label "our benefits". Why: skimmers read only headlines. Src: 062 "don't just put like our benefits"

### Imagery and visual design
- IF you have text paragraphs of benefits THEN add scannable icons with short benefit text. Why: people scan; icons are easier to digest. Src: 063 "people don't really read text they just scan it"
- IF the headline looks like body text THEN raise the contrast: larger, bolder, darker headline; lighter body. Why: a clear hierarchy guides the eye. Src: 064 "there is no visual hierarchy"
- IF you set body text THEN use 16 px on desktop and 14-16 px on mobile. Why: readability. Src: 061 "16 pixels on desktop normally"
- IF a layout looks trendy but odd THEN use the familiar pattern: headline and text, CTA below, image beside. Why: people expect known layouts (Jacob's law). Src: 065 "follow a proven layout or framework"
- IF the page has autoplay animation, parallax or moving content THEN remove it. Why: it distracts and confuses. Src: 065 "crazy autoplaying animations content moving all over the place"
- IF sections have large empty white space THEN tighten it, especially on mobile. Why: more information shows at once and more visitors scroll. Src: 065 "tightening up your space in every section"
- IF you add a heavy widget or iframe THEN test load time first. Why: one widget made the front page load over a minute. Src: 060 "over a minute to load"
- IF the main nav has few items THEN centre it, bold and prominent, with the logo left. Why: tests show more navigation use in most cases. Src: 063 "90 to 95% of cases"

### Forms and friction
- IF a lead form has many fields THEN reduce to the minimum for a qualified lead (about 4). Why: each field adds friction. Src: 062 "four form fields"
- IF a form needs more than one field THEN ask for email or the key item first and reveal the rest after (progressive disclosure). Why: one small step starts commitment. Src: 060 "as soon as you type in your email it opens up the rest"
- IF a form has fields side by side THEN use one column, top to bottom. Why: it is the most intuitive flow. Src: 061 "most intuitive experience is top to bottom"
- IF the field name is in the placeholder only THEN add a visible label above the field. Why: a distracted mobile user forgets what to type. Src: 061 "separating the label from the placeholder"
- IF you set the form block THEN use: action headline plus short check list on one side, form on the other, a testimonial below. Why: this is the standard high-converting form layout. Src: 061 "form on the right text on the left"
- IF the form headline says "Contact us" THEN write an action benefit (e.g. "Book your free first call") with 3-5 bullets of what happens next. Why: it states why and what to expect. Src: 065 "three to five bullet points"

### Mobile
- IF you review a page THEN check the mobile view first. Why: most visitors use mobile. Src: 063 "I always like to look at mobile view"
- IF proof is shown on mobile THEN show it up front and not inside a carousel. Why: carousel content is hidden. Src: 054 "don't hide this in a carousel"

### Testing and measurement
- IF you have any traffic THEN install a free heat map and recording tool (Clarity or Hotjar) and watch scroll depth and recordings. Why: it shows where people stop or hesitate. Src: 061 "Microsoft Clarity or we'll use hotar"
- IF a key element sits low and scroll depth is poor THEN move it up and test. Why: a video moved above the fold with a 30% drop-off gave about +90% across tests. Src: 061 "putting that video above the fold"
- IF someone says "best practice" THEN treat it as a hypothesis and test. Why: audiences differ. Src: 060 "there is no best practice"
- IF traffic is low (under about 1,000 visits a month) THEN use qualitative feedback: user tests, task walk-throughs. Why: you cannot reach statistical power. Src: 061 "the place that we always start is qualitative feedback"
- IF you need user feedback cheaply THEN run remote tests with your target users (about 40 USD per tester). Why: first-impression videos change client minds. Src: 060 "as low as $40 a tester"
- IF you can ask after conversion THEN ask "what made you fill out the form today?". Why: it is a cheap data source. Src: 061 "what made you fill out the form today"
- IF you A/B test THEN run it at least 7 days, usually 2-3 weeks, with a calculator for sample size. Why: short tests mislead even when "significant". Src: 061 "one week minimum at least seven days"
- IF you spend on ads or SEO THEN fix the page conversion first. Why: traffic to a weak page leaks money. Src: 060 "putting water into a leaky bucket"

## Patterns
### ThrillX high-converting landing page blueprint (Src: 062, 064, 065)
- Hero: Headline (dream outcome + what is in it for me, quantified), sub headline (pain first, then solution), optional eyebrow text naming the audience, one CTA, image or video that reinforces the value proposition. Text left, image right on desktop.
- Trust strip: Social proof 1 and social proof 2 both in the hero (rating, reviews, press, logos), plus a fear-reducing line under the CTA.
- Section order: Hero; pain section (pain + unique benefit); social proof 1 (video testimonials); value proposition 1-3 (four to five in total, benefit headlines, images); social proof 2 (written reviews); differentiators; how it works (3 steps); social proof 3 (logos + retention figure); FAQ; closer (mini hero with form).
- CTA design: Benefit-oriented label, high contrast, CTA in the hero and in every value section and the closer. Reply-time or guarantee lines under the button.
- Typography / visual: Headline larger, bolder and darker than body. Image before text. Tight spacing, no animation. Consistent type, colour and spacing.

### Free website audit page rebuild (Src: 062)
- Hero: Headline "get up to 3x more leads and sales in less than 24 hours" (quantified result and speed); body "tired of low conversions ... personalised video audit ... in 24 hours or less without having to spend a dime"; CTA; video (explainer) beside it.
- Trust strip: Three stats under the CTA (64% average conversion gain, 3.5x ROI, 5-8 quick wins), plus a results banner and client logos.
- Section order: Hero; pain section (tired of your page not converting); video testimonials with headline "join 1200 plus businesses"; value proposition headlines with numbers; written reviews (stars, review, photo, title, company, Clutch source); differentiators; how it works (fill the form, reply to email, relax); logos + 98% retention; FAQ ("Is this really free? What is the catch?"); closer with 3 short bullets, proof and a 4-field form.
- CTA design: First field is the website URL, more fields appear after (progressive disclosure idea from Segment). Final button "Get in touch". Form has full name, email, URL and one more field; no phone number.
- Typography / visual: Branding applied after the structure was done in Figma, desktop and mobile designed together. Images by AI tool for the first draft; custom graphics preferred.

### Hair loss clinic page teardown and mockup (Src: 064)
- Hero: Old page had a service headline ("stop hair loss without surgery"), cluttered text, a founder photo (no image on mobile) and the CTA at the bottom. Fix: benefit + time headline, CTA in the hero, form in the hero, before and after style image.
- Trust strip: Old proof was a generic quote. Fix: a specific testimonial and a second proof above the fold; Google review row at the very top as in the lawn care example.
- Section order: Hero; proof (before pain, because Google traffic is high intent); pain + solution; value proposition sections; two to three proof blocks; how it works; FAQ; closer.
- CTA design: Label "Get thicker hair now" (benefit); fear reducers right under the button; calendar booking replaced by a form.
- Typography / visual: Old page had no hierarchy (headline looked like body text). Fix: big bold headline, lighter body. HWG note: this niche example uses healing outcome promises; do not copy them for German health sites.

### Standard contact / demo page layout (Src: 061)
- Hero: Left: action headline (e.g. "Book your free website audit") with check marks of what happens next (e.g. reply in 24 hours). Right: the form.
- Trust strip: Testimonials below the check marks on the left.
- Section order: Headline, check marks, testimonials, form beside.
- CTA design: One button under the form, benefit label.
- Typography / visual: Single-column form, label above field, high contrast. On mobile everything stacks.

- Trust strip: Old "1000+ customers" sat at the bottom above the button; fix: put rating and press quotes first (examples: Front of the Pack 1.5k reviews, Honest Paws Trustpilot, Farmer's Dog quotes under press logos).
- Section order: Hero; category cards with benefit headlines; ingredients; story; best sellers. Product page: images instead of dots, one title, clickable review score, icon badges, price before delivery, guarantee under add-to-cart.
- CTA design: Two-step CTA; whole cards clickable; shop now button on collection cards.
