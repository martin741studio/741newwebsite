## Rules

### Hero / above the fold
- IF you start a page build THEN spend most of the effort on the hero (above the fold). Why: it is the only section all visitors see and most never scroll past it. Src: 015 "spend 80 to 90% of your effort just on this one section"
- IF you design the hero THEN include in this order: benefit headline, sub-headline, two social proofs, one CTA, fear-reducers, one reinforcing image or video. Why: this is the tested hero anatomy. Src: 022 "your goal is to fit at least all of this above the fold"
- IF you write the hero headline THEN lead with the one core promise/outcome, not who you are or what you do. Why: visitors need "why should I care" in 5 seconds. Src: 022 "lead with what is that one big core promise"
- IF you need a headline formula THEN combine end result + time period (if real) + emotional payoff. Why: the emotional outcome often converts better than the feature. Src: 022 "the emotional payoff which is what is the emotional end result"
- IF you write the sub-headline THEN explain how you deliver the headline: name the pain ("tired of X") then the solution with 3-4 concrete value props. Why: it adds context the headline cannot carry. Src: 022 "stop doing X no more Y"
- IF you test a headline THEN check three things: clarity, specificity, certainty. Why: these three decide whether the first 5 seconds work. Src: 017 "I want clarity I want specificity and I want certainty"
- IF the headline is a question or a buzzword (better, transform, elevate) THEN rewrite it as a definite, concrete statement. Why: questions and buzzwords lack certainty and context. Src: 017 "It's asking it as a question"
- IF a stranger lands on the page THEN they must know who you are, what you do and what is in it for them in 5 seconds. Why: otherwise they exit or never scroll. Src: 020 "who you are what you do and what's in it for me"
- IF you pick the hero image or video THEN make it show the service, the place, the team or the result, never unrelated stock. Why: the image should explain the offer even if nobody reads the text. Src: 024 "avoid using stock images or stock videos that have no correlation"
- IF the hero image is a person THEN show a real, smiling person and point their gaze towards the headline, CTA or form. Why: emotion first, and gaze steers the eye. Src: 021 "the baby is looking directly at the form"
- IF the service is visual or people-based (renovation, practice, therapy) THEN show multiple real work photos and photos of the team in the hero. Why: it feels human and personal. Src: 022 "photos of their team there"
- IF the hero is bloated (big banner, tall header, huge whitespace) THEN compress it so key info and proof fit above the fold. Why: wasted space pushes proof and CTA below the fold. Src: 024 "there's no need for huge blocks of white space"
- IF you compress the hero THEN keep a small visible edge of the next section. Why: a hero that fills the screen signals "nothing more here". Src: 018 "indicate that that section is actually scrollable"
- IF you design for mobile THEN make a separate mobile layout (stacked hero, rethought sizes and hierarchy), not a shrunk desktop. Why: copy-pasted desktop layouts underperform. Src: 027 "mobile deserves its own layout"
- IF you place text and menu on mobile THEN follow the F/Z reading pattern (left-aligned text, menu where thumbs/eyes expect it). Why: fixing right-aligned text and right hamburger gave +74% mobile leads. Src: 027 "scan in an F or Z pattern"
- IF intent is high (Google search) and the offer is simple to explain THEN put the form or contact action in the hero. Why: visitors already want the service. Src: 023 "less complicated to explain"

### CTA
- IF you set the CTA THEN use one primary action: large, high contrast, clear text. Why: choice slows decisions. Src: 016 "single clear call to action button that is painfully obvious"
- IF you write CTA text THEN state the benefit or what happens next, not "Submit" or "Contact us". Why: an actionable benefit button pulls more clicks. Src: 023 "get funded fast"
- IF the CTA colour is a brand colour THEN make it distinct from text and headline colours. Why: the eye must land on it. Src: 016 "distinct color from your text"
- IF the page is long THEN repeat the CTA in every key section (and keep one in the nav). Why: visitors decide at different scroll depths; click maps showed middle/bottom clicks. Src: 027 "scattered throughout every key section of your page"
- IF you place the CTA THEN put fear-reducers directly under it (guarantee, free first step, easy cancel, response time). Why: friction is highest at the point of action. Src: 023 "reduction of friction and FUDs is right near the critical point"
- IF the business wants calls too THEN show the phone number next to the CTA button. Why: it serves both contact types without a second competing CTA style. Src: 022 "book free consult and we also have a phone number"
- IF the page ends THEN add a closer section that works as a mini hero: recap headline, 3-4 benefits, CTA, social proof, form. Why: late visitors still carry doubt and need one last reason. Src: 022 "a mini above the fold"
- IF the last section has a form THEN give it a benefit headline, 3-4 "what you get/what happens next" points, then the fields. Why: "Contact us" gives no reason to act. Src: 024 "set clear expectations about what can I expect once I fill out the form"
- IF you want a lead to start easily THEN use progressive disclosure (ask email/one field first, reveal more fields after). Why: the first step creates commitment. Src: 023 "progressive disclosure"

### Forms and friction
- IF a form has first and last name THEN merge to one "full name" field. Why: fewer fields, less friction. Src: 016 "combined into full name"
- IF a field is not needed to reply THEN remove it (phone is the most disliked). Why: removing phone gave +92% on one site (check: WhatsApp/phone-first practices may need it). Src: 016 "People statistically hate giving out their phone number"
- IF you build form fields THEN keep placeholders and labels separate. Why: users forget what to type on mobile. Src: 024 "separating your placeholders and your labels"
- IF the nav has many items THEN cut to 4-5 (max 7). Why: paradox of choice. Src: 016 "no more than seven items in your navigation"
- IF text is dense THEN split into short paragraphs, bullets and bold key words. Why: +158% on one rebuild. Src: 016 "cut large blocks of text into bite-sized chunks"

### Social proof and trust
- IF you build the hero THEN stack two forms of proof above the fold (e.g. star rating + count, plus press/quote or number of clients). Why: tested repeatedly; most people never see lower sections. Src: 015 "stacking social proof which is basically having two forms"
- IF you have little proof THEN show what you have anyway. Why: some proof beats none. Src: 014 "some social proof is always better than no social proof"
- IF you show a review THEN add reviewer photo, name/detail and the source (Google etc.). Why: unverifiable text reviews look fake. Src: 015 "put the photo of the person leaving the review"
- IF "featured in" logos are your proof THEN add a short quote from the publication under each. Why: bare logos are ignored. Src: 021 "adding a oneliner and a quote from the actual publication"
- IF you have many reviews THEN show them open in a wall of love; do not hide them in a carousel/slider. Why: only 1-2% click past the first slide. Src: 022 "less than 2% of people statistically 1 to 2% will actually click through"
- IF you title the proof section THEN use a benefit headline ("Join hundreds in X who ..."), not "What our customers say". Why: the headline is read more than the body. Src: 021 "actually say the benefit and what your customers say"
- IF you choose testimonials THEN pick ones that match the offer. Why: off-topic proof looks forced. Src: 021 "directly relevant to the dream outcome or offer"
- IF you add a review THEN pair it with a real photo from the context (e.g. the customer's finished job). Why: reported +60-80% from this change. HWG note: for health, use practice/people photos, no treatment-result images. Src: 021 "actual image of the customer's lawn in conjunction with the review"
- IF you have video reviews THEN place them first among proof. Why: strongest verifiable format. Src: 023 "Generally videos are the top of that hierarchy"
- IF you place proof THEN scatter it across the page after each value block, not only at the bottom. Why: most never reach the bottom. Src: 024 "don't bury your reviews at the bottom of your page"
- IF the offer is visual THEN pick proof that is visual too (before/after, work photos) instead of statistics. Why: proof type should match the offer. HWG note: before/after for health treatments is risky, use work/practice photos. Src: 022 "the social proof is also strategically thought out"
- IF you design the page THEN aim for a clean, consistent, professional look (spacing, type, colour). Why: "premium" equals trust; 46% judge credibility by design. Src: 021 "premium comes down to one main thing which is trust"

### Section order and traffic source
- IF traffic is warm/high intent (Google, "near me") THEN go hero, then social proof straight away, little pain-building. Why: they already want the service. Src: 022 "leading with the social proof first"
- IF traffic is cold (Meta ads) or the offer needs explaining THEN go hero, then pain/problem section. Why: they must first see why the service matters. Src: 017 "immediately right after the above the fold we go into the painoint section"
- IF you run several traffic channels THEN build a separate page per major channel (message, section order, education level, conversion action). Why: reported +40-60%; duplicating a page costs hours. Src: 017 "separate landing pages for each major channel"
- IF you plan any section THEN define its objective (what they feel, think, do) first, then choose the design. Why: design serves the objective. Src: 017 "what is the actual objective of this section"
- IF you structure the page THEN let each section lead into the next like a story, with consistent spacing and type. Why: keeps the scroll going. Src: 027 "feel like a story"
- IF the page is long THEN keep it long only if every section adds value; fix structure, not length. Why: long pages give room for pain, goals, motivation. Src: 027 "a long landing page doesn't kill conversions bad structure does"
- IF you plan the page THEN copy the proven order: hero, pain or proof, 2-4 proof blocks, 4-8 value props, how it works, FAQ/fear reducers, closer. Why: stop reinventing layouts. Src: 022 "the value props and the USPS in which you want four to eight"

### Copy and objections
- IF you start a page THEN write the copy first and design around it. Why: layout must serve the message. Src: 022 "we will start with the copy first"
- IF you write value props THEN pair feature with benefit and put the benefit in the headline. Why: nobody cares about the feature alone. Src: 022 "combine the benefit with the feature"
- IF you check a page THEN read only the headlines; they must sell alone. Why: people read about 20% and scan the rest. Src: 021 "they should already be sold"
- IF copy talks about "we" THEN rewrite to "you" (what you get, what you achieve). Why: visitors care about themselves. Src: 017 "replace the word we with the word you"
- IF copy has industry jargon THEN replace it with plain words. Why: visitors do not know your terms. Src: 014 "opt for clarity rather than you know using jargon"
- IF you build a pain section THEN use Problem, Agitate, Solve: name it ("does this sound like you"), show what they miss, then the way out. Why: readers convert right after it. Src: 021 "problem agitate solve framework"
- IF the offer is complex or unknown THEN use long-form pain copy; people do read it. Why: data shows it is read and pre-sells. Src: 022 "people do read this"
- IF you need words THEN take them from reviews, support mails and calls. Why: customers already wrote your copy. Src: 027 "customers already wrote your copy for you"
- IF you write the FAQ THEN fill it with the real objections from calls, tickets and chats. Why: it answers doubts at the end of the page. Src: 027 "address all of your customers objections"
- IF the page has a how-it-works block THEN use 3-4 steps with benefit headlines, plus an image per step. Why: makes success feel effortless. Src: 022 "it's not just saying step one step two step three step four"
- IF you want differentiation THEN add a section with about six differentiators or a comparison table. Why: shows why you, not the competitor. Src: 023 "what makes you different than your competitors"

### Offer and risk reversal
- IF you have a guarantee, trial or easy cancel THEN show it at the hero CTA and again at the next decision point. Why: repeat fear-reducers where doubt returns. Src: 018 "reintroducing these items later on in the user Journey"
- IF you have no money-back guarantee THEN use another risk reducer (free first step, transparency, no-obligation). Why: some risk reversal must sit by the CTA. Src: 022 "not every business has one and doesn't need to have one"
- IF a promotion is aggressive or confusing THEN drop it; avoid timers and fake scarcity. Why: it raised leads but also chargebacks and low quality. Src: 017 "fake scarcity only five positions left"
- IF you optimize THEN judge by lead quality, revenue and fit, not raw conversion rate. Why: one firm had 900 leads/month at 12% but only 80 qualified. Src: 017 "The goal is not high conversions"

### Pricing display
- IF you show 3+ options/packages THEN cut to 3. Why: 5 to 3 options gave +53% conversion and +13.5% AOV. Src: 016 "cut down from five options to three"
- IF you show prices THEN show the exact saving in currency, not only a percent. Why: +25% in one test. Src: 016 "exactly how much money they would be saving"
- IF price causes hesitation THEN move it lower, after more trust blocks. Why: stacked tests gave 80%+ lift. Src: 027 "moving the price further down on the page"

### Imagery and visual design
- IF you use images THEN make them carry the value prop (infographic-style) rather than plain decoration. Why: images are processed far faster than text. Src: 015 "turn them into mini infographics"
- IF you write value props THEN add simple icons for scanning. Why: icons beat paragraphs for skimmers. Src: 025 "scanable visuals that resonates a lot more"
- IF you set type THEN make the headline 3-4x body size, 48-64px headline and 16-20px body, headline and body in different colours. Why: clear hierarchy. Src: 016 "at least 3 to 4x larger than your body text"
- IF you set the brand system THEN use 3-4 colours, max 2 fonts and 8-point spacing increments; related items closer together. Why: consistency signals investment. Src: 021 "rule of eights"
- IF everything is bold or loud THEN reduce emphasis to headline, CTA only. Why: too much emphasis means none. Src: 027 "too much emphasis means no emphasis"

### Speed
- IF the page loads under about 10 seconds THEN do not sacrifice design for milliseconds. Why: speed does not fix poor design. Src: 027 "obsessing over saving seconds or milliseconds"

### Testing and measurement
- IF you can pick one analytics tool THEN install free Microsoft Clarity (recordings, heatmaps, scroll depth, click areas). Why: no data limit, same core features as paid tools. Src: 019 "Microsoft Clarity is completely free"
- IF an important block is reached by only 30-40% of visitors THEN move it up. Why: visible fix from heatmaps. Src: 020 "only 30% of people or 40% of people are reaching that section"
- IF a block gets many clicks but few reach it THEN move that element up the page. Why: +18% from one such test. Src: 019 "10.41% of clicks were going towards the shop by industry section"
- IF you want the "why" THEN run a survey: "why did you buy / contact today" and "what almost stopped you". Why: gives real customer words and objections. Src: 014 "what stopped you from purchasing today"
- IF you want first-impression data THEN run cheap remote user tests (about $40 per tester). Why: qualitative feedback shows what clicks cannot. Src: 019 "$40 per tester"
- IF a test shows no winner THEN study why and build the next test on it. Why: ruled-out variables compound. Src: 027 "ab tests with no results are not failures"
- IF you hear "best practice" THEN treat it as a hypothesis and test your version. Why: implementation varies by audience. Src: 027 "there's no such thing as best practices"

## Patterns

### Bathroom and kitchen remodeling hero (Src: 022)
- Hero: emotional headline ("finally get the bathroom or kitchen you've been wanting"), sub-headline names pain then 3 benefits (family-run team, no subcontractors, on time and budget). Image side shows a scrolling stack of bathroom and kitchen work plus team photos.
- Trust strip: two proof blocks above fold (Social proof 1 and 2), not detailed.
- Section order: hero, then before/after pain section (visual: old bathroom vs new, pains vs benefits), value props.
- CTA design: "Book free consult" plus phone number side by side.
- Typography / visual: fear-reducers under CTA: no subcontractors, transparent pricing, 100% satisfaction guarantee. Mobile stacks everything.

### MedSpa client hero (Src: 022)
- Hero: emotional headline ("feel like yourself again") plus end result ("smoother, brighter skin that looks natural"); image side shows multiple before/afters scrolling.
- Trust strip: social proof 1 and 2 above the fold.
- Section order: not stated beyond hero; later has a 5-step "proven system" how-it-works with before/after per step.
- CTA design: one CTA with fear-reducers beneath (not detailed).
- Typography / visual: design picked to show all skin types, not one result. HWG note: before/after is a risk in German health advertising.

### Cosmetic dentist UK hero (Src: 022)
- Hero: copy about "finally feeling confident showing off your smile"; ONE core image of a smiling person looking toward the text.
- Trust strip: before/afters instead of stats, since the offer is visual.
- Section order: not stated.
- CTA design: not stated.
- Typography / visual: image chosen to match the emotion in the copy. (HWG note as above.)

### Therapy client hero (Src: 022)
- Hero: headline "finally feel understood, supported and in control from the first session".
- Trust strip: not stated.
- Section order: not stated.
- CTA design: not stated.
- Typography / visual: photos of the therapist smiling to create warmth; design built to carry the felt outcome.

### Solar panel cleaning page (high-intent Google traffic) (Src: 022)
- Hero: standard hero (benefit headline, two proofs, CTA, fear-reducers, image).
- Trust strip: right after the hero a wall of love with many reviews.

### The five-block template (Src: 022)
- Hero: dream-outcome headline, sub-headline, social proof 1 and 2, one CTA, fear-reducers, image/video.
- Trust strip: 2-4 social proof blocks over the page.
- Section order: hero, then pain OR proof (by traffic), value props / USPs (4-8), how it works, FAQ + fear reducers (1-2), closer.
- CTA design: one CTA per block; closer mirrors the hero.
- Typography / visual: copy first, then design around it.

### Longer template with differentiators (Datapel, Ryden, Lion Group examples) (Src: 023)
- Hero: benefit headline, body (pain "stop X" plus solution), CTA with fear-reducers underneath, social proof above the headline (review sites, logos), image of the product/work.
- Trust strip: stacked proof: star rating + review count, student/client count under CTA, success stats.
- Section order: hero, pain, social proof (video/case studies), value props (several), social proof, differentiators (six items or table), how it works (4 steps), social proof, FAQ, final recap.
- CTA design: CTA with benefit text; lead form in hero only if offer simple (Awesome Inc: +132% leads); closer repeats hero.
- Typography / visual: proof scattered in between each block; same layout reused for value props.
