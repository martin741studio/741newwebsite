## Rules
- IF a visitor arrives from an ad or search result THEN make the above-the-fold headline repeat the promise of the ad. Why: a mismatch between click promise and page kills the click value. Src: 028 "keep that same messaging congruent on the landing page itself"
- IF the page is a lead page with one goal THEN remove or cut the top navigation menu. Why: nav sends visitors to other pages instead of the CTA. Src: 028 "why send traffic outside of this page"
- IF the hero body text runs longer than 3 sentences THEN cut it to 2-3 sentences. Why: long text pushes proof and CTA below the fold. Src: 028 "consolidated down to two to three sentences max"
- IF the CTA button says "Kontakt" or "Contact us" THEN replace it with a benefit-led action text. Why: generic button text gives no reason to click. Src: 028 "contact us very overdone these days"
- IF visitors feel unsure THEN place risk reducers (guarantee, "no obligation", no-pills, free first step) right under the CTA button. Why: doubt peaks at the moment of action. Src: 028 "reduce fear and uncertainty near the critical point of action"
- IF the hero image shows only the location or a generic scene THEN show the outcome or the happy person instead. Why: the image must back up the headline promise. Src: 028 "relate the image to that value prop"
- IF you use proof in the hero THEN show two different forms (for example star rating plus a quote or number of clients). Why: tests showed two forms convert better than one. Src: 028 "we always go with two forms of social proof"
- IF you show "As seen in" logos THEN add a short quote from that outlet under the logos. Why: logo strips are overdone and weak alone. Src: 028 "actually having a quote from Dr phil"
- IF the page has one CTA at the bottom only THEN repeat the same CTA in every key section. Why: more buttons give more chances to convert. Src: 028 "more buttons you introduce the more likely someone is to convert"
- IF the page lacks explanation of the process THEN add 3 value-prop sections (how it works, what you get). Why: visitors need to understand the offer before they act. Src: 028 "value prop number one value prop number two value prop number three"
- IF paid traffic must book a call via an embedded calendar THEN offer a short contact form first. Why: calendar booking adds friction for cold visitors. Src: 028 "we typically don't want to add too much friction up front"
- IF the page ends with a weak "Want to know more?" line THEN build a closer section: final reason to act, 3 value props, CTA, risk reducers, image or form. Why: a mini hero at the bottom catches visitors who scrolled. Src: 028 "almost like a mini above the fold final recap"
- IF the hero offers two actions (book call AND get guide) THEN keep one core action. Why: competing offers split attention. Src: 028 "cut this down make it to one core action"
- IF reviews exist THEN place the star rating directly under the title / high in the hero. Why: reviews should be the first thing people see. Src: 028 "we always put the reviews as the very first thing people see"
- IF you show reviews THEN make them specific and verifiable (source named, photos of results, story, video). Why: generic text reviews are overdone and distrusted. Src: 029 "the more specific you are the more believable your proof is"
- IF you sell a service where people buy from people THEN use real photos of the team with smiling customers. Why: it builds instant human trust. Src: 029 "people buy from people they like"
- IF you use stock or fake AI images THEN replace them with real, custom images. Why: stock and AI pictures look fake and hurt trust. Src: 029 "stock imagery that has nothing to do with our offer"
- IF you write a headline THEN use: end result plus emotional payoff. Why: it tested +56 percent on a Google Ads page. Src: 029 "end result plus the emotional payoff"
- IF you write copy THEN address the reader with "you", not "we". Why: top pages speak about the reader. Src: 029 "they never say we"
- IF the page has many CTA buttons or elements THEN cut to one or two CTA buttons and remove non-critical elements. Why: too much info exceeds the cognitive threshold. Src: 029 "one or two CTA buttons at max"
- IF you plan design time THEN spend most of it (80-90 percent at the start) on the hero section. Why: every visitor sees it and 60 percent never scroll past it. Src: 030 "80 to 90% of your efforts just on this one section"
- IF the headline only says who you are and what you do THEN replace it with one hard-hitting benefit that answers "why should I care". Why: a headline test alone gave up to +80 percent. Src: 030 "the only thing you need is why should I care"
- IF you build mobile THEN do not hide reviews in a carousel or accordion; show them open. Why: what is hidden is not seen. Src: 030 "what is hidden will not get seen"
- IF you write section headings THEN avoid labels like "Our services", "How it works", "What makes us unique". Write the benefit in the heading. Why: label headings carry no information to scanners. Src: 030 "just simply say what makes you unique"
- IF a button, heading and body compete THEN differ in size, weight and colour so the order is clear. Why: a fixed hierarchy gave +64 percent. Src: 030 "clear flow of information from the headline to the sub headline"
- IF you feel like being creative with the layout THEN stick to the familiar pattern: one headline, body text close to it, proof on top, CTA below, risk reducers, then image. Why: Jakob's law: people expect familiar sites. Src: 030 "simplicity beats creativity every single time"
- IF a visitor lands THEN the hero must answer in 5 seconds: what is this, who is it for, what do I do next. Why: unclear pages lose visitors at once. Src: 031 "what is this who is this for"
- IF you write copy THEN borrow the words from customers (sales calls, tickets, reviews, Reddit and forums). Why: copy that mirrors customer words converts. Src: 031 "the best copy is not written it's borrowed from your customers"
- IF visitors reach the bottom of the page THEN give them an FAQ that answers real objections taken from calls and tickets. Why: the bottom is decision mode and doubts hold people back. Src: 031 "FAQs can be your secret weapon"
- IF testimonials exist THEN choose ones that name the same fear the visitor has and show the person went ahead anyway. Why: visitors recognise themselves. Src: 031 "testimonials around already pre-existing fear and uncertainty"
- IF you must choose between page speed and clarity THEN fix experience and content first; do not chase milliseconds. Why: a fast page does not repair weak design or content. Src: 031 "page speed is not the end all be all"
- IF you analyse behaviour THEN use free heatmap, scroll map and session recordings (Microsoft Clarity) plus qualitative input (surveys, user tests). Why: heatmaps show what happens, not why. Src: 031 "heat maps do not tell you the full story"
- IF a page looks cluttered THEN fix hierarchy with headings, white space, size, colour and contrast instead of deleting content. Why: clutter is a hierarchy problem. Src: 031 "there's no such thing as a cluttered page"
- IF a test loses THEN find out why (survey, recordings) and feed the learning into the next test. Why: losing tests compound into later wins. Src: 031 "a gold mine for your conversion rate"
- IF you make a claim THEN use exact numbers ("10,327 clients", "87 percent"). Why: people trust numbers more than words. Src: 031 "people trust numbers a lot more than words"
- IF the CTA feels like a sales push ("Buy now") THEN use a softer wording such as "see how it works" or "get instant access". Why: visitors resist obvious sales buttons. Src: 031 "the best performing CTAs often don't feel like a CTA"
- IF you have only a few testimonials THEN pick the strongest transformation stories and put those on top; do not show weak counts. Why: relevance beats volume. Src: 031 "handpick those testimonials and feature those at the top"
- IF you plan a page THEN write down customer pain points, wants, goals, dream outcome and the path from first thought to decision (persona and journey map) before you design. Why: pages built on random sections do not convert. Src: 032 "user personas and journey maps"
- IF traffic is cold (social ads) THEN educate, build rapport and show the key value first; IF traffic is warm (Google search, SEO) THEN be direct with the form or CTA at the very top. Why: awareness level differs. Src: 032 "someone coming from Google ads or even organic SEO ... solution aware"
- IF the hero wastes space (huge image, big white space, tall nav) THEN tighten it so headline, proof, CTA and image all show without scrolling. Why: tests showed +60 to +80 percent. Src: 032 "60 to 80% lifts in conversions from just this one change"
- IF you want customer insight THEN run a few paid user tests (3-5 testers) and exit surveys ("what almost stopped you?"). Why: they show why visitors hesitate. Src: 032 "all you really need is three to five tests"
- IF a form is submitted THEN ask a post-conversion question: what motivated you, what almost stopped you. Why: it reveals fears to fix on the page. Src: 032 "what almost stopped you from buying today"
- IF you write the hero promise THEN go one layer deeper than the outcome and name the core desire behind it (safety, being there for family, feeling attractive, freedom from pain). Why: the "why" drives the action. Src: 033 "sell the core desire not outcomes"
- IF the copy has jargon or long paragraphs THEN use short sentences, plain words, white space; test with a 12-year-old reading level. Why: easy-to-read text feels more trustworthy. Src: 033 "write your page so that a 12-year-old can understand it"
- IF the price or effort needs justification THEN list everything the service includes and what happens behind the scenes. Why: visible effort makes the price feel fair (labor illusion). Src: 033 "people can actually see all of the labor"
- IF you use loss framing THEN keep it factual and mild; in health, never threaten or promise (HWG). Why: loss feels twice as strong as gain, but fear claims break HWG. Src: 033 "losing feels twice as bad as winning feels good"
- IF the first screen looks cheap, cluttered or generic THEN fix typography, colours, spacing and copy first. Why: visitors judge in 50 ms and the verdict colours the whole page (halo effect). Src: 033 "people judge your website in 50 milliseconds"
- IF you build a page THEN write copy and offer first and design second. Why: strong copy is the backbone; design only amplifies it. Src: 034 "copy first design comes second"
- IF you write the headline THEN combine the logical end result (hyper-specific number or amount) with the emotional result and, where honest, a timeline. Why: specifics feel concrete. Src: 034 "making the copy super hyperspecific"
- IF you build the page structure THEN use this skeleton: hero, pain point (if needed), 2-3 proof sections, value-prop sections, fear/doubt reduction (guarantee, FAQ) near the bottom. Why: proven order from 400+ pages. Src: 034 "five main sections"
- IF the offer is complex or traffic is cold THEN put the pain section right after the hero; IF the offer is simple and visitors search actively THEN put proof right after the hero and the form may sit above the fold. Why: cold visitors need education, hot visitors need trust. Src: 034 "lead with the pain point section first"
- IF the service transforms people THEN show a before/after strip (auto-scrolling on mobile) in the pain or proof section. Why: it shows scale and result at once. Src: 034 "multiple before and after images that are autoscrolling"
- IF the offer is about feeling supported THEN show a grid of real practitioner photos. Why: warm faces back the emotional promise. Src: 034 "images of their actual therapist in kind of a grid format"
- IF you write the proof section headline THEN lead with a benefit or community ("join N people who got X"), not "Testimonials" or "Real people, real results". Why: visitors know they look at reviews. Src: 034 "join X number of members who achieve XYZ benefit"
- IF you use video testimonials THEN add a scannable headline that sums up what the person says. Why: otherwise there is no reason to press play. Src: 034 "a scannable headline that encapsulates what the review was actually about"
- IF you write a value prop THEN pair the feature with the benefit using "so that", and put the benefit in the section headline. Why: most visitors read headlines only. Src: 034 "if I just read the headline I'm already sold"
- IF the navigation lists more than 7 items THEN cut it to 7 or fewer and move About, Blog, FAQ, Contact into a small secondary menu. Why: above 7 items returns diminish. Src: 035 "never show any more than seven items on the navbar"
- IF you offer 4-5 packages or options THEN cut to 3. Why: a test with 3 instead of 5 bundles gave +53 percent conversion. Src: 035 "cut down from five bundle options to three"
- IF the page has long paragraphs THEN turn them into icons, bullets and sub-headlines. Why: +25 to +158 percent in tests. Src: 035 "chunk all of that content into scannable icons"
- IF several elements shout at once THEN lower the weight of secondary items (badges on a grey bar) so the CTA button is the strongest item. Why: emphasising everything emphasises nothing. Src: 035 "in trying to emphasize everything they've emphasized nothing"
- IF the contact page headline says "Contact us", "Let's talk" or "Get in touch" THEN replace it with a benefit headline (for example what the visitor gets). Why: visitors know they are on the contact page. Src: 036 "I already know I'm on the contact page"
- IF the contact page has a form THEN list 3-4 bullets of what happens after sending. Why: they give extra reasons to fill the form. Src: 036 "three to four bullet points of here's what I can expect"
- IF visitors reach the form THEN put a testimonial, logos or stats right next to the form. Why: the form is the point of greatest doubt. Src: 036 "social proof at the point of form entry"
- IF the form has more than 4 fields THEN cut to 4 or fewer (one full-name field). Why: each field adds friction. Src: 036 "four form fields or less"
- IF the form asks for a phone number and you do not need it THEN remove the field. Why: phone number is the most common objection. Src: 036 "phone number is one of the biggest objections statistically"
- IF fields sit side by side THEN stack them in one vertical column and mark required fields. Why: single-column forms finish faster. Src: 036 "simple vertical form field that generally has the fastest completion rate"
- IF the lead barrier must be very low THEN ask for the email first and reveal the other fields after (progressive disclosure). Why: a small first step raises follow-through. Src: 036 "progressive disclosure"
- IF your readers are motivated by the topic (for example a health problem) THEN long, detailed copy is fine if it is good. Why: people read what resonates; the bar for quality is higher. Src: 037 "the bar for your messaging is way higher"
- IF the value proposition needs room THEN stack several benefits into a 3-5 line headline. Why: these outperformed short slogan headlines in tests. Src: 037 "stacking benefits in the headline"
- IF visitors hold a wrong belief ("not for my case", "too late for me") THEN build a proof section that answers it (switch by case type, similar-case stories). Why: this objection stops buyers. Src: 037 "see people who started in the exact same position as them"
- IF the visitor wants a result but doubts it THEN add certainty: observed averages, a breakdown of how it works, proof from people in the same position. Why: desire without belief does not convert. Src: 037 "the more certainty that you can communicate"
- IF a big brand's page looks good THEN do not copy it; a small business lacks the brand pull. Why: the big brand sells before the page loads. Src: 037 "what works for Apple will not work for you"
- IF Clarity shows dead clicks on non-clickable items (logos, price cards, banners that look like buttons) THEN make them clickable or restyle them. Why: visitors expect a click and get frustrated. Src: 038 "people thought that these elements were clickable"
- IF the main CTA gets under about 5 percent of clicks in the heatmap THEN rework its position, wording or contrast. Why: 5-7 percent click-through is the benchmark used. Src: 038 "typically we like to see the click through be 5 to 7%"
- IF the scroll map shows a sharp drop after the hero THEN the hero fails to explain who/what/benefit in 5 seconds; fix that before adding proof. Why: social proof does not rescue an unclear hero. Src: 038 "it doesn't matter how much social proof you have"
- IF you review session recordings THEN watch until you see the main pattern (100-400 sessions in client work) and ask "why" for each action. Why: patterns show friction that numbers hide. Src: 038 "however many recordings it takes you to understand the main pattern"
- IF someone says "short pages convert better" THEN decide length by traffic and awareness: warm search traffic gets a shorter page with the form near the top, cold traffic gets more sections. Why: trust takes longer to earn today. Src: 039 "trust takes a lot longer to earn"
- IF the site design looks dated (1950s style) THEN modernise it. Why: dated design reads as a scam and attracts bad leads. Src: 039 "people just immediately think it's a scam"
- IF your traffic is under about 50,000 visitors a month THEN skip A/B tests; fix obvious problems with recordings, user tests and a full redesign. Why: samples are too small for valid tests. Src: 039 "anything under 50,000 visitors per month"
- IF a visitor already knows you (from a call, referral) THEN make sure the home page carries the same value props, proof and design as the ad page. Why: leads Google your name before they book. Src: 039 "they're probably going to Google your name and land on your homepage"
- IF any page can be an entry point (home, service page) THEN apply the landing-page rules to every page. Why: organic visitors land on any page. Src: 039 "any page that somebody lands on"
- IF lead quality matters THEN keep useful friction: ask for name and a qualifying detail and say who the service is not for. Why: a bare email form raises volume but cuts qualified leads and revenue. Src: 039 "good friction is any friction that helps drive more revenue"
- IF you judge success THEN track qualified leads and revenue, not only conversion rate. Why: conversion rate alone can hide worse quality. Src: 039 "conversion rate and never revenue which should always be the primary KPI"

## Patterns

### Thrillex lead-gen page framework (Figma template) (Src: 028, 034)
- Hero: benefit headline on top, sub-headline explains the method/USP, large benefit CTA, risk reducers under the CTA, image or video of the outcome on the side or below; 2 forms of proof inside the hero
- Trust strip: 2 proof forms above the fold (rating + quote/number); 2-3 proof sections in the full page
- Section order: hero, pain point (PAS or before/after, if cold or complex), social proof, value-prop sections (1, 2, 3), more proof, fear/doubt reduction (guarantee, FAQ), closer section
- CTA design: same benefit-led CTA repeated in every key section; closer repeats hero (3 value props, CTA, risk reducers, image or form)
- Typography / visual: size, weight and colour set one order: image, bold headline, subdued body text, big CTA; design follows the copy outline

### Hair-loss clinic redesign (Swiss trichology, Meta ads) (Src: 028)
- Hero: benefit headline "Get thicker, healthier hair in 12 weeks and feel confident looking in the mirror"; sub-headline names the pain, then the method (analysis, laser, plant actives); CTA "Get thicker hair now"; one single action
- Trust strip: rating "5 out of 5, 800+ reviews" at the very top; stats row (success rate, 12,800 men helped, 25+ years); placeholder data in the demo
- Section order: hero, pain section with PAS ("Talk to us if ..."), value-prop sections (process, how it works), success stories, closer section with form
- CTA design: CTA after each key section (old page had one at the bottom only); short contact form instead of calendar booking; under CTA: 60-day money-back, "no surgery or pills"
- Typography / visual: old page had 8 lines of hero text and two competing offers; new one cuts to 2-3 sentences and one action

### Weight-loss retreat page (bad example, Google Ads) (Src: 028)
- Hero: generic headline "Retreat to a new reality" does not repeat the ad promise "lose 6-20 lbs in 2-4 weeks"; nav visible; button "Contact us"
- Trust strip: only "as seen on" logos
- Section order: not described beyond steps 1-8 of the program, which belong in the sub-headline
- CTA design: "Contact us" button is prominent but weak; fix = benefit CTA, risk reducers, transformation photo, quote from the TV outlet next to logos, testimonials moved above the fold on mobile
- Typography / visual: hero image shows the venue, not the transformation

### HVAC client page (Src: 029)
- Hero: image first, then bold headline, then subdued body text, then big bold CTA; clean flow
- Trust strip: not described
- Section order: not described
- CTA design: one big CTA, 1-2 buttons at most
- Typography / visual: hierarchy by size, colour, weight; page converts at 9 percent on Google Ads

### Closet client / med-spa / kids bootcamp (visual proof examples) (Src: 029, 030)
- Hero: kids bootcamp page uses smiling child photos all through the page; tanning brand hero shows a smiling person using the product
- Trust strip: luxury closet page shows result photos next to text (+48 percent leads); med-spa uses before/after images and review-mined copy
- Section order: not described
- CTA design: not described
- Typography / visual: real photos of team and customers smiling (lawn care, cosmetic dentistry); kids bootcamp went from 2.5 to 5 percent

### Laser eye surgery page (Google Ads, high intent) (Src: 039, 034)
- Hero: form placed right at the very top
- Trust strip: social proof straight after the hero
- Section order: hero with form, social proof, short; little pain build-up
- CTA design: the form itself is the CTA at the top
- Typography / visual: shorter than cold-traffic pages; lawn care page with Google Ads uses the same pattern (form above the fold, one strong image separated from the text)

### Horse supplement page (cold Meta traffic) (Src: 039)
- Hero: not described
- Trust strip: heavy social proof
- Section order: reviews, ingredients, value props, comparison tables, more proof, many sections
- CTA design: not described
- Typography / visual: long page by design because cold traffic needs education

### Kitchen and bathroom remodel (pain section as before/after) (Src: 034)
- Hero: transformation headline; scrolling before/after images (auto-scroll on desktop and mobile)
- Trust strip: multiple transformations at once to show scale
- Section order: hero, graphical pain section (old kitchen photo with downside text overlay, side by side with the new kitchen and functional benefits), proof, value props
- CTA design: not described
- Typography / visual: text overlay on photos; side-by-side layout

### Therapy practice page (Src: 034)
- Hero: copy theme "feel supported and in control"
- Trust strip: grid of real therapist photos
- Section order: not described
- CTA design: not described
- Typography / visual: warm, welcoming faces in a grid back up the copy

### Contact page layout (userGems, Chili Piper, own client pages) (Src: 036, 032)
- Hero: benefit headline instead of "Contact us" (for example "Ready to grow your pipeline?"); 3-4 bullets with what you get
- Trust strip: testimonial plus logos or stats next to the form (point of form entry)
- Section order: headline, bullets, proof, form, all stacked vertically
- CTA design: submit button after max 4 fields; single column; labels separate from placeholders; required fields marked; Segment-style progressive disclosure (email first, other fields appear)
- Typography / visual: vertical form is faster than side-by-side fields

### Own agency page, 3,000 words (Src: 037)
- Hero: long stacked-benefit headline (3-5 lines)
- Trust strip: not described
- Section order: heavy copy about what the reader tried and failed at and the deep desires; one block says who the agency is not for
- CTA design: no CTA until far down the page, so visitors read first
- Typography / visual: few images; result 6.2 percent conversion, $66 per lead, 6x ROAS in month one

### League of Legends coaching proof section (Src: 037)
- Hero: not described
- Trust strip: Discord screenshots as reviews
- Section order: proof section with a selector bar at the top where visitors click their own rank (low to high)
- CTA design: not described
- Typography / visual: tab selector answers the belief "not for my level"

### Franchise client earning table (Src: 037, 034, 033)
- Hero: headline with an exact profit figure ("up to 300K in profit in first two years")
- Trust strip: real client results as proof further down
- Section order: headline claim, included items list, table of hours per job and jobs per week with income, arrows showing the sequence, proof
- CTA design: not described
- Typography / visual: table with arrows shows the logic; image-plus-text would not work for this message

### Microsoft Clarity homepage teardown (Src: 038)
- Hero: headline "Better your business" judged weak; image on the right got clicks (idea: show a video)
- Trust strip: 4.8/5 on Capterra, 4.5/5 on G2 in the hero
- Section order: not described; scroll map: 96 percent reach the first block, 87 percent the next
- CTA design: main CTA gets about 4-5 percent click-through (target 5-7 percent); check marks got clicks though not clickable
- Typography / visual: nav and text got clicks; use the area map to compare elements

### Parcel Safety hero (A/B winner) and Prepared hero (Src: 035)
- Hero: one large bold headline; Prepared hero shows bold headline, subdued body text, separated icons or images, prominent button
- Trust strip: badges on a grey bar so they do not compete with the CTA
- Section order: not described
- CTA design: red button, the strongest element on screen
- Typography / visual: +64 percent conversion and +72 percent revenue per visitor vs old hero with clashing colours

### Influence Hunter page (Src: 031)
- Hero: not described
- Trust strip: photo carousel and grid of the influencer/UGC content (supports "proof at scale")
- Section order: value-prop sections, each with a CTA that jumps to the form
- CTA design: buttons in all critical value-prop sections; recordings show visitors click mid-page
- Typography / visual: grid visual gave +115 percent; page converts at 7 percent
