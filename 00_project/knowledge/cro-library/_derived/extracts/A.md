# Group A extraction (files 001-013)

## Rules
- IF you plan the homepage work THEN spend 80-90% of the effort on the above-the-fold area. Why: every visitor sees it, about 60% never scroll past it. Src: 003 "80 to 90% of your efforts just on the above the fold"
- IF a visitor lands THEN make clear who you are, what you do and what is in it for them within 5 seconds. Why: opinion forms fast and the benefit part is what most pages miss. Src: 001 "immediately clear in 5 seconds or less who you are"
- IF you build the hero THEN include benefit headline, supporting subheadline, one benefit-led CTA, social proof and a fitting image. Why: this is the proven hero stack. Src: 006 "a benefit- driven headline followed by subcopy"
- IF the visitor arrives from a search result or ad THEN make the headline repeat the promise that earned the click. Why: message match keeps the visitor from bouncing. Src: 001 "congruent with the ad or whatever got people to click"
- IF the hero has empty space or oversized images THEN shrink them so headline, proof, CTA and reassurance fit in the first screen. Why: white space pushes key content below the fold where 60% never look. Src: 003 "really tightening up your above the fold space"
- IF you cut the hero down on mobile THEN fit proof, CTA and reassurance line in about four lines of text. Why: the virtual therapy hero rebuild gave +108% conversions. Src: 007 "we fit the CTA button and we fit the reduction of FUDs"
- IF the hero has many headlines, offers or buttons THEN remove everything not strictly needed. Why: the top of the page is the most expensive real estate. Src: 011 "cutting out elements that are not absolutely necessary"
- IF you write the hero headline THEN state the outcome the visitor wants and why they should care; use the subheadline to explain how you deliver it. Why: headline and subheadline have different jobs. Src: 007 "explain the dream outcome and why should I care"
- IF you write the subheadline THEN answer the biggest doubt about the headline promise. Why: it removes the main reason not to believe the headline. Src: 011 "the single largest concern someone has"
- IF the hero image appears THEN show the outcome or the real service that backs the headline, not a decorative picture. Why: the image should be proof for the headline. Src: 009 "hero image should add proof to the headline and sub headline"
- IF a headline template gives outcome plus deadline (like pain gone in 6 weeks) THEN do not copy it for a health practice; use service benefit, process, availability or experience instead (HWG). Why: healing promises and cure timelines are forbidden. Src: 003 "is it end chronic pain is it 3x my revenue"
- IF you need a headline angle THEN ask "so that" repeatedly until you reach the deepest benefit. Why: it moves copy from feature to benefit. Src: 009 "I like to use the so that principle"
- IF a headline names a feature or service THEN pair it with the benefit the person gets. Why: people buy the benefit, not the attribute. Src: 007 "combine the benefit with the feature"
- IF someone reads only the headings THEN the page must still tell the full story, so write every section heading as a benefit. Why: about 20% read, 80% scan. Src: 006 "people will only read 20% of the content"
- IF a section heading is a label (Why choose us, Our services, Testimonials) THEN rewrite it to carry the actual claim. Why: labels waste the strongest line. Src: 011 "Actually say what makes you unique in the headline itself"
- IF you head the review section THEN write "trusted by N people to get X" instead of "Testimonials". Why: visitors already know it is a testimonial block. Src: 011 "trusted by X number of customers to achieve"
- IF a line is clever or witty THEN rewrite it so it is clear first. Why: visitors only need to know what this is, who it is for, what to do next. Src: 006 "clear over clever"
- IF you can state a real number THEN use it in headline or proof line (review count, years, patients). Why: numbers read as objective. Src: 006 "50,400 happy customers is way more impactful"
- IF you write body copy THEN address the reader with "you" and what they gain, not "we do". Why: customer focus drives response. Src: 006 "The most powerful word in copywriting is you"
- IF the audience is searching for a solution (for example "Physio in town") THEN place trust proof high on the page and allow the contact action above the fold. Why: solution-aware traffic needs a reason to trust, not education. Src: 007 "we want to capture people immediately at the top"
- IF the audience is problem-aware (social, ads) THEN lead with an offer, proof and an empathic pain-to-solution story, not a hard form. Why: they do not yet know the solution. Src: 007 "we're not really leading with like a form capture"
- IF the page must rank for a keyword THEN keep the keyword in the H1 but style it small, with the conversion headline as the visible hero. Why: it serves SEO and conversion. Src: 003 "I make it a lot smaller on the page"
- IF you set the primary CTA THEN make it the single obvious action that is clear even when the page is blurred. Why: visual hierarchy shows the one next step. Src: 005 "if I was to blur out your entire page"
- IF you write CTA text THEN make it benefit-led and actionable (not "Submit" or "Buy now"). Why: the button text sells the next step. Src: 003 "a clear benefit- driven call to action button that stands out"
- IF the page is long THEN repeat the CTA in every major section. Why: visitors decide at different scroll depths. Src: 001 "somebody can convert at any stage of the journey"
- IF the visitor scrolls THEN keep a sticky CTA visible in the header. Why: action is always one tap away. Src: 012 "sticky call to action button that sticks with you"
- IF the CTA is a phone call THEN keep a sticky, tappable phone number on screen. Why: it worked across niches from lawyers to trades. Src: 012 "having a sticky phone number that sticks with you"
- IF a CTA would open a separate contact page THEN scroll to the on-page form (or open the chat directly) instead. Why: the test gave +220% conversions. Src: 001 "we saw a 220% lift in conversions"
- IF the nav has several buttons THEN reduce competing CTAs to the two that matter. Why: too many actions cause no action. Src: 003 "consolidated the CTA buttons just down to the two"
- IF you place a CTA THEN put a reassurance line directly under it (no obligation, answer time, opening hours, guarantee). Why: fear and doubt peak at the action point. Src: 006 "reduce FUDs near your critical points of action"
- IF the text is in the hero THEN make the headline clearly larger and heavier than body copy, around 3-4x. Why: size contrast creates the reading order. Src: 005 "headline be 3 to 4X larger than your body"
- IF the CTA is one of many coloured elements THEN give the CTA the only strong accent colour. Why: one different element is noticed first (von Restorff). Src: 004 "one obvious action that stands out"
- IF you show proof in the hero THEN stack at least two forms (for example Google rating plus handpicked quote). Why: stacked proof builds trust faster. Src: 003 "at least two different forms above the fold"
- IF you only have a few reviews THEN do not advertise the low count; show two or three handpicked quotes. Why: a small number weakens trust. Src: 001 "if you only have three reviews"
- IF you show "as seen in" logos THEN add what that publication said about you. Why: bare logos are overused and add little. Src: 001 "what did that publication specifically say about your brand"
- IF you show reviews THEN add source logo (Google), real name and photo, plus a short scannable headline. Why: verifiable reviews feel real. Src: 003 "we always put the original source of the review"
- IF you show reviews on a health site THEN keep the quotes about service and experience (friendliness, wait time), not treatment success or before-after. Why: HWG limits testimonials and result claims. Src: 007 "there's no verifiable source where was this review left"
- IF you build a review section THEN lay reviews out openly, not in a carousel. Why: carousels get under 1% engagement. Src: 003 "carousels statistically have less than a 1% engagement rate"
- IF you have many reviews THEN show a wall of proof (text, image, video) with a "show more" button on mobile. Why: sheer volume builds subconscious trust. Src: 004 "we have a show more button which opens up more proof"
- IF you can show proof visually THEN use photos of the real practice, team or setting instead of plain text quotes. Why: visual proof lifts conversion more than text. Src: 009 "to be as visual as possible"
- IF you make a claim (saves time, fewer worries) THEN place a matching handpicked testimonial next to it. Why: customers back up your claim. Src: 006 "back up your claims with testimonials"
- IF a visitor is about to contact you THEN show proof right next to the form or contact button. Why: last-minute hesitation causes drop-off. Src: 006 "social proof at the point of form entry"
- IF you write the offer THEN cover dream outcome, likelihood, speed and low effort (value equation). Why: it covers all four reasons people buy. Src: 008 "immediately articulated in your headline and your sub headline"
- IF you reduce risk THEN put badges, guarantees or credentials directly under the CTA. Why: this is where doubt is highest. Src: 009 "under your CTA button"
- IF you speak about speed THEN quantify how fast (appointment wait, reply time), never healing time. Why: shortens the perceived delay without a health promise. Src: 009 "how fast someone can get results"
- IF the process looks complex THEN show a how-it-works section with three or four steps. Why: more steps increase perceived effort. Src: 009 "just three or four because the moment you go higher"
- IF you structure a long page THEN use this order: sticky nav, hero, proof or pain-to-proof, value props, more proof, comparison, how it works, team, guarantee, FAQ, closer. Why: it is the tested full layout. Src: 012 "you want to scatter even more social proof throughout your page"
- IF the page is long THEN keep it long only if every section earns its place. Why: length does not hurt when copy is strong. Src: 010 "length of the page does not matter"
- IF the page ends THEN finish with a closer that copies the hero: benefit headline, 3-5 bullets, proof, reassurance, form or button. Why: skimmers jump straight to the bottom. Src: 012 "treat this section exactly like the hero section"
- IF the audience has a clear pain THEN open the section after the hero with the problem, agitate it, then present the solution (PAS), without disease claims on health sites. Why: starting with pain connects first. Src: 011 "content that begins with pain converts significantly higher"
- IF you sell a service delivered by people THEN add a "meet the team" or founder-story section. Why: people buy from people. Src: 012 "people ultimately buy from people"
- IF you write the FAQ THEN build it from real questions in sales calls and recordings. Why: it answers the true objections in the customer's words. Src: 012 "pull this from sales calls or recordings with customers"
- IF the price may shock THEN show trust and value before the price. Why: price objections drop when trust comes first. Src: 002 "we have to build more trust earlier"
- IF you show prices THEN show them up front with no hidden costs. Why: surprises make people leave. Src: 005 "show your pricing upfront"
- IF the copy sounds like AI or marketing jargon THEN rewrite it in simple, relatable language. Why: generic robotic text turns people off. Src: 013 "use Simple language on their website that's actually relatable"
- IF text blocks are long THEN chunk them into bullets, short lines, headings and icons. Why: easier to process builds trust. Src: 004 "chunking content into smaller digestible pieces"
- IF you pick people photos THEN show real, happy people in the situation the visitor wants. Why: viewers picture themselves there. Src: 004 "visualize ourselves in the same position"
- IF you want a premium first impression THEN show several real images (team, setting, work) rather than one stock photo. Why: it shows real people behind the work. Src: 004 "show multiple images up front that communicate multiple different parts"
- IF you design the contact area THEN write a benefit headline (not "Contact us"), 3-5 bullets and a social proof line. Why: it gives a reason to reach out. Src: 006 "benefit-driven headline as to why I should fill out that form"
- IF a visitor is about to contact you THEN tell them what happens next (reply time, first step). Why: it removes uncertainty. Src: 006 "paint them a vivid picture of what happens next"
- IF you use a form THEN keep it to four fields or fewer and merge first and last name. Why: fewer fields means less friction. Src: 001 "try to keep it to four Forum fields or less"
- IF the form needs lots of data THEN ask the first easy step first (email or name) and reveal the rest after. Why: people finish what they started. Src: 006 "ask people for simple information up front like their email"
- IF navigation has many items THEN cut to under seven and move the rest to the footer. Why: choices above seven give diminishing returns. Src: 005 "diminishing returns anywhere above seven items"
- IF you want a popup THEN delay it until intent shows (exit, scroll depth), never in the first seconds. Why: early popups add friction before understanding. Src: 005 "hit with a popup"
- IF you design for mobile THEN make a separate mobile layout, not a shrunk desktop. Why: spacing, buttons and hierarchy need their own thinking. Src: 006 "mobile deserves its own layout"
- IF the page is slow THEN fix extreme load times, but do not sacrifice design for milliseconds. Why: only waits past about 10 seconds clearly hurt. Src: 006 "if your landing page takes more than 10 seconds to load"
- IF you style the page THEN invest in a clean palette, type and spacing, without flashy animation. Why: people judge credibility by design. Src: 006 "46% of people judge a brand's credibility just based on the design"
- IF you use animation THEN use it only to explain or focus the message. Why: motion must serve an objective. Src: 004 "should help someone understand the message"
- IF the first view looks cluttered or dated THEN redesign it first. Why: first impressions colour everything after (halo effect). Src: 010 "people judge your business in just 50 milliseconds"
- IF every page can be a landing page THEN treat homepage and service pages with the same conversion rules. Why: visitors land anywhere. Src: 006 "treat every page like it's a landing page"
- IF the site gets low traffic (under about 40k visits per month) THEN skip A/B tests; make changes from Clarity data and watch the result. Why: no statistical significance. Src: 008 "make changes based on objective data that we collect in Microsoft Clarity"
- IF you read heatmaps THEN aim for about 3-5% clicks on the hero CTA; under 1% means weak hero. Why: it flags a weak offer. Src: 003 "I like to aim for like a 3 to 5% click-through rate"
- IF you run any test THEN start with headline and hero image. Why: most gains came above the fold. Src: 008 "split test the headlines on our landing pages"
- IF you want qualitative feedback THEN have four or five target users think aloud on the site. Why: that finds most usability issues. Src: 002 "we generally recommend four to five user tests"
- IF you review behaviour THEN watch at least 100 session recordings and note hesitation points. Why: it shows where people stall. Src: 013 "watch a minimum of 100 session recordings of users"
- IF you collect client feedback THEN ask what almost stopped them and what motivated them. Why: customers name the objections and drivers. Src: 013 "was there anything that almost stopped you"
- IF you have many reviews THEN mine them for repeated topics and reuse those words in copy. Why: it reveals what customers value. Src: 002 "doing something called review mining"
- IF someone says "short pages convert better" or "copy a competitor" THEN test against your own data instead. Why: best practices only get you so far. Src: 010 "best practices only get you so far"

## Patterns
### Influence Hunter landing page (Src: 001)
- Hero: benefit headline and body text, image replaced by a carousel of the influencer content they produced; "Learn more" CTA scrolls to the form on the same page.
- Trust strip: client result stats plus case studies with results (two proof types), directly in the hero.
- Section order: hero with stacked proof, benefit sections with testimonials in between, client logos mid-page, contact form near the bottom.
- CTA design: "Learn more" button repeated in every section; all buttons jump to the on-page form (A/B result +220%); clickmaps showed clicks on lower buttons.
- Typography / visual: not described beyond benefit headlines such as "we reach out to thousands of influencers so you don't have to".

### Inboxy B2B page (Src: 001, 006)
- Hero: headline "Land emails in the inbox effortlessly so that you can close more deals"; body text quantifies the result ("within 14 days of signing up"); CTA "improve your deliverability".
- Trust strip: stats (open rate, response rate, spam rate) plus a few client logos "trusted by high performance sales and marketing teams".
- Section order: hero, benefit sections (mostly described in text, not in detail).
- CTA design: benefit-led CTA text.
- Typography / visual: benefit-first headline derived with the "so that" ladder; test gave +134% conversions and qualified leads.

### Contact page / closer pattern (Src: 001, 006)
- Hero: not applicable; this is the form block.
- Trust strip: social proof directly beside the form.
- Section order: benefit headline, 3-5 bullets of what to expect, form, short blurb on next steps.
- CTA design: four form fields maximum, label separate from placeholder, single column.
- Typography / visual: replaces "Contact us"; part of the Line Group change that gave +142%.
