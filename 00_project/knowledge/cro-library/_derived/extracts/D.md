# Group D extract (files 041-053, Arsh Sanwarwala / ThrillX)

## Rules
- IF any page is an entry page (home, service, location) THEN put 80-90% of design and copy effort into the above-the-fold area first. Why: 100% of visitors see it, about 60% never scroll past it. Src: 043 "spend 80 to 90% of your efforts just on this one section"
- IF you write the hero headline THEN state the benefit for the visitor, not who you are or what you do. Why: a "who/what" headline gives no reason to care or scroll. Src: 049 "You do not want to put who you are or what you do in your headline"
- IF you write the hero headline THEN use end result + time period + emotional payoff (order can change). Why: covers function and feeling in one line. HWG: for health use a feeling/access/ease outcome, never a cure, symptom-removal or day count. Src: 043 "end result they want plus time period plus emotional payoff"
- IF no truthful time frame exists THEN drop the time period from the headline formula. Why: the speaker calls it optional. Src: 043 "This is optional as you know sometimes you may not always have this"
- IF the headline cannot answer "who, what, what is in it for me" within 5 seconds THEN rewrite it. Why: visitors leave after the first impression. Src: 050 "who you are what you do and what's in it for me"
- IF you choose between clever and clear headline THEN choose clear, even if long and not pretty. Why: direct-response headlines convert better than tag lines. Src: 046 "people don't want clever people want clear"
- IF you write the sub headline THEN lead with the pain ("tired of X") and follow with the solution listing 3-5 specifics. Why: contrast principle plus stacked USPs. Src: 051 "stop doing x no more y or whatever the pain point is"
- IF you need headline ideas THEN mine customer language (reviews, discovery call, Google/Maps reviews) and reuse the exact themes. Why: voice of customer beats guessing. Src: 045 "what's the voice of the customer"
- IF the page is a service or location page that must rank THEN keep the keyword in the H1 tag but make the big visible headline the benefit line. Why: serves SEO and UX together. Src: 048 "this is the H1 header that we're trying to rank for this keyword"
- IF you pick the hero image THEN show something that supports the headline claim (real people, real practice, real result context), natural and not staring at camera. Why: images carry the message fast; generic stock looks cheap. Src: 042 "try to pick something where it looks a bit natural"
- IF the offer is emotional or trust-based (therapy, healing) THEN use real photos of the practitioners above the fold. Why: images build emotion and trust. Src: 042 "use photos of the therapist above the fold"
- IF the hero body text is written THEN pack the top 3-4 value props into it as if the visitor sees nothing else. Why: most never scroll. Src: 042 "taking all of your value props ... encapsulating it into that one body text"
- IF the hero is built THEN place at least two different forms of social proof above the fold (e.g. review score + number of clients/years + press). Why: gives a reason to scroll; tests showed 2 beat 1. Src: 051 "two forms of social proof works a lot better than one"
- IF you choose hero proof THEN pick proof relevant to the main fear or goal of the visitor, not a generic quote. Why: irrelevant proof is checkbox work. Src: 043 "directly relevant to your core offer or dream outcome"
- IF only one proof type exists THEN stack more types (score, count, logos, testimonial). Why: stacking builds more trust at the top. Src: 050 "stacking social proof"
- IF there is empty white space between hero elements THEN cut it so review/proof sits above the average fold, also on mobile. Why: scroll maps showed proof sat below the fold. Src: 051 "bringing these reviews above the average fold more people are going to see it"
- IF a CTA button exists THEN put risk-reducers right under it (free first consult, reply time, no waiting list, hours, cancel terms). Why: fear peaks at the action point; one test gave +62%. Src: 043 "right underneath your call to action button"
- IF the practice replies fast THEN state the reply time under the CTA ("answer within 15 minutes"). Why: slow reply is the industry norm, so speed is a differentiator. Src: 042 "get a response in less than 10 15 minutes"
- IF you write CTA text THEN make it benefit-led and specific ("Get an instant quote", "Book free consult"), not "Submit" or "Contact us". HWG: no outcome promise in the label. Src: 051 "it says get an instant quote"
- IF the CTA is designed THEN make it large, high contrast and a different colour from every other element (blur test: it must still be findable). Why: unclear core action makes visitors leave. Src: 050 "if I was to blur out this entire page"
- IF two elements near the CTA share one colour (CTA and trust badges) THEN restyle so only the CTA is dominant. Why: competing elements hide the core action. Src: 045 "both of those are red"
- IF a text link acts as CTA THEN turn it into a real button. Why: a text-style CTA got 0% clicks while testimonials got more. Src: 051 "it's literally just text"
- IF the page needs hierarchy THEN use size, weight and colour: headline about 32px, body about 16px in a quieter colour, button big. Why: guides the eye top to bottom; +64% lift in one case. Src: 051 "the headline here is a lot bigger this is about like 32 33 pixel"
- IF the audience may prefer calling THEN show a dual CTA in the hero: primary "Book" button plus clickable phone number (or WhatsApp). Why: not everyone wants to book online. Src: 042 "not everyone prefers to just book a consult"
- IF you design the header THEN strip it to logo + CTA + phone, and make phone and CTA sticky on scroll, especially on mobile. Why: lets visitors convert at any scroll depth. Src: 051 "phone number in particular is super important for any service-based company"
- IF you debate sticky bar at top or bottom on mobile THEN either works; test only with data. Why: no substantial difference seen. Src: 042 "mixed results there's no one that's better than the other"
- IF a CTA repeats down the page THEN repeat the same check-mark reminders under each CTA. Why: A/B tested lift despite looking repetitive. Src: 042 "underneath each CTA right it's just a reminder"
- IF you use forms THEN use one full-name field, not first+last, and keep labels separate from placeholders. Why: fewer fields; placeholders vanish on mobile. Src: 051 "combine that into full name"
- IF the form is long THEN try progressive disclosure (ask email/phone first, reveal the rest after). Why: people who start a form finish it more often. Src: 051 "once they've already filled in one field they're way more likely"
- IF a field is not needed for the first contact THEN remove it; IF it is needed THEN say why you ask. Why: remove friction but keep lead quality. Src: 052 "if it is necessary justify it"
- IF you put a form above the fold THEN do it only for solution-aware, high-intent traffic (search for the service). Why: cold or problem-aware visitors need education first. Src: 051 "it's kind of premature you haven't really educated them yet"
- IF you lay out a form block THEN use: benefit headline, 3-4 bullets of what to expect, fields, CTA, risk reducer or testimonial under CTA. Why: tested layout. Src: 046 "three to four bullet points of what they can expect"
- IF lead quality matters more than volume THEN add one qualifying element to the form (e.g. price range, concern type) and judge by qualified leads. Why: removed spam leads completely in the case. Src: 042 "we haven't had a single spam lead"
- IF the purchase is emotional and anxious THEN send CTA clicks to a separate calm contact page instead of scrolling visitors down the whole page. Why: scrolling past many sections adds overwhelm. Src: 042 "that might actually cause additional overwhelm and additional anxiety"
- IF you build a contact page THEN include form at top, social proof, how it works and FAQ. Why: removes last doubts at the point of action. Src: 042 "reduce fear and uncertainty right around reaching out"
- IF you build a thank-you page THEN confirm receipt, say who calls and when, add FAQ, ideally a short "what to expect" video from the owner, then proof. Why: orients the lead and raises show-up and close rates. Src: 042 "helps with the close rate part of things"
- IF you write the final section THEN do not title it "Contact us" or "Get a quote"; use a benefit headline with a push to act, 3-5 incentive bullets, proof and the form. Why: the label wastes the last headline. Src: 043 "do not repeat contact us or get a quote"
- IF you design the closer THEN treat it as a mini hero: benefit headline, two proof types, risk reducers, CTA. Why: last push for those who scrolled all the way. Src: 048 "mini above the fold but at the bottom of the page"
- IF you plan section order THEN use: hero, pain OR proof, value props, how it works, team, optional guarantee, more proof, FAQ, closer. Why: tested blueprint across 80+ niches. Src: 046 "followed by social proof afterwards"
- IF the audience knows the service and compares providers THEN put social proof before the pain section; IF unaware or offer is complex THEN pain section first. Why: awareness level sets what is needed first. Src: 046 "we will actually put the social proof section before the pain proof section"
- IF you write the pain section THEN use problem, agitate, solve; calling out the exact symptoms/situations visitors recognise. HWG: describe situations, not illness claims or cures. Src: 043 "we want them to think this is exactly me"
- IF the offer needs more explanation THEN use sales-letter style copy but keep paragraphs to one or two sentences and bold key phrases. Why: long but scannable text still works. Src: 049 "At most maybe one to two sentences per paragraph"
- IF you list pains THEN always attach the way out in the same section. Why: pains alone do nothing. Src: 049 "without actually telling them what their solution is"
- IF you write real customer quotes THEN add a section with the exact phrases patients say in 3 themed boxes. Why: visitors see their own thoughts. Src: 042 "this is literally what's going through on in my mind"
- IF you write value-prop sections THEN build 3-4 at minimum (up to 8-10), each as feature + derived benefit ("so what?"), with benefit up front. Why: benefit is what people buy. Src: 051 "clear and timely reports so that i can make smarter decisions"
- IF you write any headline THEN make it carry the benefit so a headlines-only reader is sold. Why: people read about 20% and scan the rest. Src: 043 "somebody will only read the headlines on your page"
- IF a value prop is critical to a fear (e.g. "does it hurt?") THEN give it its own section. Why: that anxiety decides the booking. Src: 046 "this deserved its own separate section"
- IF you write social-proof headlines THEN use "Join N people who got [benefit]", not "Our reviews" or "See what customers say". Why: visitors already know it is a review block. Src: 049 "helping x number of customers achieve main benefit"
- IF you show reviews THEN show the full name, photo, source logo (Google) and a short scannable headline per review. Why: believability; +60-80% in tests. Src: 053 "full name and photo of the person leaving the review"
- IF you show reviews THEN pick different themes across reviews and cut long reviews to the best sentence as a headline without changing the text. Why: covers several fears; scanners read the headline. Src: 042 "we're just cutting out sentences we're not modifying the review"
- IF you show a review wall THEN lay it all out open (wall of love), not in a carousel. Why: carousels get under 1% engagement. Src: 046 "less than 1% of people will actually go and see the second"
- IF the page has text-only reviews THEN add photos, video or story detail. Why: text reviews are overdone and distrusted. Src: 046 "text testimonials are so overdone everyone does it"
- IF proof is spread THEN place 3-4 proof sections through the page between value props. Why: visitors can be unconvinced at any point. Src: 043 "three to four social proof sections on any given page"
- IF you write "how it works" THEN title it with an effortless benefit ("[benefit] in 3 simple steps"), keep 3-4 steps, and make the last step the end benefit. HWG: no outcome promise. Src: 051 "in just four simple steps"
- IF the business is service-based THEN add a meet-the-team section with real photos, specialities and a personal quote. Why: people buy from people. Src: 051 "people buy from people"
- IF you build FAQ THEN source questions from sales/first-call questions and objections, price first. Why: removes the real blockers and filters out poor fits. Src: 051 "most commonly asked questions that you experience on sales calls"
- IF the visitor says price or time is the objection THEN assume it is a smokescreen and answer the deeper doubt (will it work for me, will I connect). Why: unclear path to help is the real objection. Src: 042 "that's always smoke screens"
- IF the page includes a guarantee or free first step THEN show it as its own optional section and in the CTA risk-reducers. Why: lowers risk at the decision point. Src: 051 "if you have some sort of guarantee you can offer that here"
- IF the business seems boring or similar to competitors THEN still write out all basics as value props (process, reply time, qualifications). Why: most competitors never explain them. Src: 048 "the bar is very very low"
- IF you plan the copy process THEN write offer and copy first (8-12 distinct value props, 4-5 get own sections) and design around it. Why: design-first yields weaker pages. Src: 048 "we get the content first and the copy first"
- IF you gather insights THEN run a 1-hour discovery call with the owner and note exact customer phrases, triggers, status quo and objections. Why: the page should recreate the sales call. Src: 042 "the better the notes the better the copywriting as well"
- IF a long page worries the owner THEN keep it long if each section adds new value. Why: best pages are multiple sections long; people read when given a reason. Src: 046 "multiple sections long"
- IF the traffic comes from an ad or search THEN keep headline and message congruent with what got the click. Why: mismatch wastes the click. Src: 042 "congruent with whatever got the click"
- IF quiz funnels or lead forms bring junk THEN test a dedicated page that explains who you are and what happens next. Why: leads know what they sign up for; no spam in the case study. Src: 042 "they know what they're opting into"
- IF the page is a paid-traffic landing page THEN remove top navigation and external links (social, partner sites). Why: 33% of clicks went to nav and 3.28% left the site. Src: 051 "I just want them on this page alone"
- IF a non-clickable element looks like a button (logos in bordered boxes) THEN restyle or make it useful. Why: users click it and get nothing. Src: 044 "they have like a little gray border around them"
- IF the site menu is built THEN use plain clear labels and few items. Why: too many options and branded names confuse. Src: 052 "Clarity over cleverness"
- IF you show many options (services, packages) THEN cut to 3. Why: 5 to 3 options gave +53% conversions in a test. Src: 045 "cut down from five bundle options to three"
- IF long text blocks exist THEN replace with 4-6 icon + short line value props. Why: scannability; +23% to +33%. Src: 047 "putting scannable icons right and changing that text into value props"
- IF you pick copy tone THEN write at a 5th-8th grade reading level, with headings and bullets. Why: lower cognitive load. Src: 053 "fifth or eighth grade reading level"
- IF visuals are used THEN keep a clear visual identity: max 3 colours (one dark, two light), alternate dark/light section blocks, body text not pure black. Why: clean look builds trust. Src: 042 "use a max of three colors like one dark and two lights"
- IF the brand is calm/clinical THEN avoid very round buttons; about 12px radius. Why: very round buttons feel like SaaS. Src: 042 "you get a two round of a button it feels like a sass"
- IF you set type sizes THEN use at least 16px body (18px on mobile), headline fits 3 lines. Why: legibility and no orphan words. Src: 042 "16 pixels minimum font size"
- IF the brand needs credibility THEN invest in a professional look, not just a text page. Why: visitors read design as business quality. Src: 051 "it just comes across as more professional and credible"
- IF you do mobile THEN stack content vertically, put the image above the text, drop spare rows so the headline, one proof and CTA fit in the first screen. Why: first screen decides. Src: 042 "trying to squeeze as much above the fold without trying to make it overwhelming"
- IF you use carousels on mobile THEN keep key proof and "how it works" open; use carousels only for low-priority lists (team of 10+) lower on the page. Why: hidden content is not seen. Src: 042 "whatever is hidden will not get seen"
- IF mobile shows reviews THEN show the best 5-6 stacked, not all 9. Why: shorter page, still open. Src: 042 "we truncate that to the top six five or six reviews"
- IF you QA mobile THEN check on a real phone, not only in browser inspect. Why: inspect view misleads. Src: 042 "you can't just QA through inspect element"
- IF load speed worries you THEN fix gross problems (cheap shared hosting, 15 s loads) but do not trade UX for small gains. Why: small second gains matter little. Src: 052 "if it takes 15 seconds that's probably when you start losing people"
- IF visitor price shock is likely THEN build trust and value before showing a price; IF visitors are price-led they may be the wrong fit. Why: tests gave +80% when price moved down. Src: 052 "people who want to see the price immediately are not the right type of customer"
- IF you add urgency THEN use calm real info ("reply today", "book by 5pm, seen tomorrow"), not guilt timers. Why: timers raise friction and returns. Src: 052 "you will benefit if you make your purchase quicker"
- IF you design navigation, logo and search placement THEN keep standard practices for logo, menu and search position; test only the content. Why: users expect them. Src: 052 "every website should follow standard practices"
- IF you have under about 25,000-30,000 visitors per month on a page THEN skip A/B tests and build on proven practice plus qualitative checks. Why: no significant results. Src: 051 "you do not want to concern yourself with ab testing"
- IF you measure THEN install Microsoft Clarity (free): watch recordings of converters, filter rage clicks, use area click map and scroll map on mobile first. Why: shows what to move or cut. Src: 051 "filtering by those who have converted"
- IF scroll map shows a key section (proof, team) reached by few THEN move it higher and improve the content above it. Why: lower sections get fewer views. Src: 051 "validate all of your assumptions with data"
- IF click-through on CTA buttons is under about 3% THEN fix them (look, size, wording). Why: 3-5% is the benchmark. Src: 051 "you typically want to aim for a 3 to 5% click-through rate"
- IF traffic is low THEN add qualitative checks: 3-5 paid user tests with think-aloud tasks, and review mining of Google reviews by motivation and anxiety. Why: numbers say what, users say why. Src: 044 "three to five user tests that's really all you need"
- IF you ask new clients something THEN ask "What almost stopped you from booking?" at intake or in a follow-up. Why: surfaces objections for copy. Src: 044 "concerns doubts or uncertainties that almost stopped you"
- IF you set the main KPI THEN use qualified leads, show-ups and revenue, not raw conversion rate or cost per lead. Why: a high rate with junk leads is a loss. Src: 042 "main KPI should always be revenue"
- IF you plan tests THEN write a hypothesis ("if we do X we get Y because Z") and rank ideas (above fold, noticeable in 5 s, adds/removes element, user-test backed). Why: tests with evidence win. Src: 044 "if we do this process by doing XY Z we will then generate more leads"
- IF you scope pages THEN give each page one job (home: route to service page; service page: book) and build one page per service and location. Why: micro-conversions and SEO focus. Src: 048 "each page on your website should be getting somebody one step closer"

## Patterns
### Luxury closet landing page rebuild (Src: 046)
- Hero: social proof 1 (Google reviews) at very top, benefit headline ("upgrade your space" + functional benefit), sub headline starts with pain ("tired of messy closets") then lists USPs (3D mockup, fast install, cleanup, lifetime warranty), CTA, risk reducers under CTA (fast response, 0% financing, lifetime warranty), large product image.
- Trust strip: social proof 2 stacked next to proof 1 in the hero; mobile stacks the same items vertically.
- Section order: hero, pain/benefit section (short, subtle), social proof (Google review text + photo of the client's closet), value props, how it works ("seamless three-step white glove process"), closer.
- CTA design: one CTA with FUD line beneath; closer repeats benefit headline + proof + form on the right + background image.
- Typography / visual: old page used "who we are" headline, "how it works" first, testimonials in a carousel; result was +282% leads in one case, +109% (2.46% to 5.15%) in another.

### Form-in-hero page (reverse vasectomy, search traffic) (Src: 046)
- Hero: benefit headline + sub headline on left, lead form on the right (form headline, 3-4 bullets, fields, CTA, testimonial under CTA).
- Trust strip: two social proof items in hero; little trust building elsewhere because audience is solution aware.
- Section order: hero with form, pain section, social proof, value props, closer with form.
- CTA design: form button is the CTA; testimonial under the button.
- Typography / visual: direct-response headline, long and aggressive, accepted on purpose.

### Horse coaching program page (problem-aware Meta traffic) (Src: 046)
- Hero: benefit headline, proof, FUDs.
- Trust strip: social proof inside hero, more later.
- Section order: hero, long problem-agitate-solve section (reader scared of riding), social proof, value props.
- CTA design: CTA at end of PAS section ("get instant access now").
- Typography / visual: longer PAS because unaware audience and a complex new method.

### Virtual therapy landing page speedrun (Soul Thrillers) (Src: 042)
- Hero: left side headline ("Finally feel understood, supported and in control from the very first session"), body text with pain + 3 value props, 12px-radius primary button "Book free consult" plus phone-number outline button; right side practitioner photos; text kept to 3 headline lines at about 54px.
- Trust strip: stats box (6,400 Canadians helped, 90%+ happy with first match, 10 minute reply, recommend rate) with 3 avatar photos and stars; check marks under CTA (100% virtual, no wait list, 8am-10pm 7 days); insurance logos strip.
- Section order: hero, stats, pain section (sales letter, PAS, bold/underline), wall of love Google reviews (with headline per review), value prop "someone who cares", value prop "problem solvers", virtual therapy 4-point section with icons, video reviews, how it works (step pills, 4 steps), therapist slider (photo, name, specialty pills, quote), insurance logos, scrolling text band as visual separator, three-column "real quotes" boxes (colour-coded), FAQ, closer with 3 steps + form + proof, footer.
- CTA design: "Book free consult" repeated in every section, links to a separate contact page; sticky phone number and CTA on mobile; same check marks under every CTA.
- Typography / visual: heading serif (Newsreader), body Lato, 3 brand colours (primary pink, light pink, dark body grey 4848), alternating dark/light blocks, pink highlight pill behind key words, soft pink image backplates, custom graphics on images, mobile at 390px with top 5-6 reviews stacked and carousels only for team and matching.
- Result: no spam leads, close better than quiz funnel; case notes: quiz 35% to booking, 30-45% to client.

### Fitness client homepage (Src: 048)
- Hero: social proof 1 "join 7,000+ members", headline = end result + 6 weeks + emotional payoff, sub headline = value props condensed, results image on right, FUD line under CTA (instant access, step-by-step plan, cancel anytime), proof 2 = scrolling before/after results.
- Trust strip: member count + carousel of results with weight lost and time frame.
- Section order: hero, social proof, pain section (PAS long copy), programs/services with learn-more buttons, value props, more proof, how it works, meet the team, more proof, FAQ, blog/social entry points, closer.
- CTA design: CTA repeated through page; closer recaps benefit + proof + FUDs + image.
- Typography / visual: every headline carries a benefit, no generic "our reviews".

### Lawn care location/service page (Src: 048)

### Generic landing page blueprint (Figma template) (Src: 051)

- Trust strip: two proof types, e.g. "10,000+ dogs trained", "#1 voted", "15 years", "700+ Google reviews".

- Typography / visual: headline about 32px vs body 16px, button in strong blue, no empty white blocks.
