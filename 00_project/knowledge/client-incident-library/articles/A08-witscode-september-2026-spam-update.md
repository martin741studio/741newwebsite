# Google September 2026 Spam Update: How to Tell If You Were Hit

The Google September 2026 spam update started on September 24, 2026 at 09:15 PDT. Google says it applies globally and to all languages, and that the rollout "may take up to two weeks to complete."<sup>1</sup> As of October 2, 2026, Google's Search Status Dashboard still shows it as ongoing, and site owners have reported two waves of drops.<sup>1</sup><sup>4</sup>

If your traffic fell in late September, the spam update is one of several suspects. This post shows how to tell a spam-update hit apart from the other things that moved in September, and what to do while the rollout is still running.

## The short version

- Google started the September 2026 spam update on September 24, 2026 at 09:15 PDT. It is global, covers all languages, and "may take up to two weeks."<sup>1</sup>
- The rollout is still running as of October 2, 2026. The dashboard's only entry is Google's September 24 release note, with no end time set. If Google holds to its own estimate, the rollout ends on or before October 8.<sup>1</sup>
- That is far slower than the other three 2026 spam updates. March, June and August each finished in under three days, per Google's dashboard.<sup>2</sup>
- Search Engine Roundtable reported drops in two waves: September 25 to 27, then September 30 into October 1. Site owners also reported Discover and News losses. Google has not confirmed either wave.<sup>4</sup>
- There was no September 2026 core update. Google's list of ranking updates shows none, and the last core update started May 21.<sup>2</sup>
- Google has not said what this spam update targets. It called it "a normal spam update."<sup>3</sup> Its spam policies page is the reference for what any spam update enforces.<sup>7</sup>
- Don't make big changes until the dashboard marks the rollout complete. Google says algorithmic recovery depends on its systems learning "over a period of months" that a site complies.<sup>8</sup>

## What is the Google September 2026 spam update?

Google calls a notable improvement to its spam detection systems a spam update. Those systems include an AI-based system called SpamBrain, and Google lists each spam update in its ranking updates history.<sup>8</sup><sup>12</sup> The systems enforce Google's spam policies, and Google says sites that see a change after a spam update "should review our spam policies."<sup>8</sup>

Google's dashboard announcement for the September 2026 spam update is two sentences: released globally, all languages, up to two weeks.<sup>1</sup> It names no policy and no type of site. Google's LinkedIn post, quoted by Search Engine Roundtable, called it "a normal spam update."<sup>3</sup> Google's John Mueller told Search Engine Roundtable's Barry Schwartz the rollout is "likely to take longer than some of the previous ones."<sup>3</sup> Schwartz also lists the update as not targeting link spam.<sup>3</sup> That point is his reporting, not Google's wording.

Any post that says "this update targets X" without quoting Google is guessing.

Google's spam policies page, last updated August 28, 2026, lists 16 spam policies. They are cloaking, doorway abuse, expired domain abuse, hacked content, hidden text and link abuse, keyword stuffing, link spam, machine-generated traffic, malicious practices, misleading functionality, scaled content abuse, scraping, site reputation abuse, sneaky redirects, thin affiliation and user-generated spam.<sup>7</sup> Google says sites that violate them "may rank lower in results or not appear in results at all."<sup>7</sup>

Our August 2026 spam update breakdown shows how to audit a site against the content policies, template by template. That audit works for any spam update because it starts from the policies, not from a guess about targets. What is new in September is the two-week rollout, the reported waves, and how much harder they make the diagnosis.

When the spam update was announced, Schwartz wrote that the volatility seen before the announcement "was not it."<sup>3</sup> Treat those dates as real movement with no named cause.

## How do I know if the September 2026 spam update hit my site?

You can run these checks in Google Search Console without a developer.

**1. Check the Manual actions report.** In Search Console, open the Manual actions report. A green check mark means no manual action.<sup>10</sup> If an action is listed, a human reviewer flagged your site. That is a different problem with a different fix (see the recovery section below).

**2. Check the Security issues report.** Hacked content is one of Google's spam policies.<sup>7</sup> If something is listed, clean up the hack before you do anything else.

**3. Find the day the line broke.** Open the Performance report, set the date range to the last 3 months, and look at daily clicks and impressions. Find the first day of the step down. A step on September 25 to 27, or September 30 to October 1, lines up with the reported waves.<sup>4</sup> A step on September 15, or on September 22 or 23, came before the spam update started.<sup>1</sup><sup>5</sup><sup>6</sup>

**4. Compare periods.** Google's own steps: choose the Date filter, open the Compare tab, and compare the last 3 months to the previous period, then year over year. Setting the date range to the last 16 months also shows yearly seasonality.<sup>9</sup> The newest data can be preliminary and "might change in the next few hours," so don't judge on today's numbers alone.<sup>13</sup>

**5. Split by search type and surface.** Use the Search type filter to check Web and News separately.<sup>9</sup> Discover has its own Performance report.<sup>11</sup> Google says Discover traffic is "somewhat less predictable than Search traffic," so a Discover dip on its own is weak evidence.<sup>11</sup>

**6. Look at which pages lost.** In the Pages tab, sort by the click difference. If the losses cluster in one template or page type (location pages, programmatic posts, affiliate roundups, guest posts), that points to a policy pattern. If losses are spread thin across everything, it is more likely broad volatility or seasonality. That read is our practitioner judgment, not Google's guidance.

**7. Check the queries.** Run the drop queries through Google Trends. Google suggests this to see whether demand fell across the web or only for you.<sup>9</sup>

## Spam update, volatility, seasonality, tracking or manual action: how to tell them apart

Google lists six main causes of traffic drops: algorithmic updates, technical issues, security issues, spam issues, seasonality and changing interests, and site moves and migrations.<sup>9</sup> Here is how they tend to show up in your data. The patterns are our practitioner judgment, built on Google's diagnostic steps.

| What you see | Most likely cause | 
|---|---|
| Manual action listed in Search Console | Manual action. Fix and request review. | 
| Security issue listed | Hacked content or malware. Clean up first. | 
| Step down on September 25 to 27 or September 30 to October 1, clustered in one page type | Consistent with the September 2026 spam update | 
| Step down on September 15 or September 22 to 23 | Unconfirmed volatility before the spam update | 
| Same dip at the same point last year, and Google Trends shows lower demand | Seasonality | 
| Only Discover fell, Web search held | Discover variability, or a Discover-specific change | 
| Analytics (GA4) dropped, but Search Console clicks held | A tracking problem, not Google rankings | 
| Pages dropped out of the index, or URL Inspection shows errors | A technical issue, such as noindex, robots.txt or server errors | 
| Drop started right after you moved domains or changed URLs | A site move or migration | 

Search Console counts clicks from Google's side. GA4 counts visits from your site's side. If GA4 fell and Search Console did not, look at your tag, your consent banner, and any recent site deploy before you blame an update.

Check position as well as clicks. Google says small fluctuations in position "can happen at any time" and recommends "avoiding making radical changes if your page is already performing well."<sup>9</sup> A page sliding from position 3 to 5 needs a different response than a page falling off page one.

## What not to do while the September 2026 spam update is still rolling out

- **Don't call it early.** The dashboard still shows the rollout as ongoing.<sup>1</sup> A reported second wave already followed a quieter stretch.<sup>4</sup> Wait for Google to mark it complete, then measure.
- **Don't file a reconsideration request for an algorithmic drop.** Review requests belong to the Manual actions report, for sites with a listed manual action.<sup>10</sup> No form exists for an algorithmic demotion.
- **Don't mass-delete or mass-noindex pages.** Deleting on a guess removes good pages along with weak ones. Decide after the rollout ends, with page-level data (practitioner judgment).
- **Don't start a site migration or URL restructure.** Changing URLs mid-rollout mixes two causes in your data, and you lose the ability to tell them apart (practitioner judgment).
- **Don't panic-disavow links.** Google's announcement doesn't mention links, and Search Engine Roundtable reports this is not a link spam update.<sup>1</sup><sup>3</sup>
- **Don't trust posts that name a September core update.** None is listed on Google's dashboard.<sup>2</sup>

## How do you recover from the September 2026 spam update?

The path depends on which kind of action hit you.

**If you have a manual action:** Google says to fix the issue on every affected page. Fixing "just some pages will not earn you a partial return."<sup>10</sup> Then select Request Review and explain the problem, what you fixed, and the outcome. Google says most reviews take "several days or weeks."<sup>10</sup>

**If the drop is algorithmic:** there is no request to file. Google's guidance has three parts:

1. Review the spam policies and bring the site into compliance.<sup>8</sup>
2. Expect a slow return. Google says changes "may help a site improve if our automated systems learn over a period of months that the site complies."<sup>8</sup>
3. Expect no guarantee. Google says "there's no guarantee that changes you make to your website will result in noticeable impact."<sup>9</sup>

Links work differently. Google says when its systems remove the effect of spammy links, any ranking benefit they generated "cannot be regained."<sup>8</sup> Recovery then means earning that ground back with new, legitimate signals.

For the policy-by-policy audit order (improve, consolidate, prune), use the playbook in our August post linked above. For the technical side, such as parameter URLs, faceted navigation and templates that multiply near-duplicate pages, a technical SEO pass cuts the number of thin, near-duplicate URLs that spam systems can misread.

## Your September 2026 spam update checklist

### Today

1. Open the Manual actions report and the Security issues report. Screenshot both results.<sup>10</sup>
2. Open Google's ranking updates history and confirm the September 2026 spam update is still listed as ongoing.<sup>2</sup>
3. In the Performance report, note the exact date your clicks stepped down, if they did.
4. Check GA4 against Search Console for the same days. If only GA4 fell, fix tracking first.
5. Write down every site change you shipped since September 1: deploys, plugin updates, redirects, content batches. You need this to separate your changes from Google's.

### This week

1. Compare the period after your step down against the previous period and against last year.<sup>9</sup>
2. Split the comparison by search type (Web, News) and check the Discover report separately.<sup>9</sup><sup>11</sup>
3. Export the Pages tab and group losing URLs by template or page type.
4. Read each of Google's 16 spam policies against your worst-hit page type.<sup>7</sup> Note any honest match.

### After Google marks the rollout complete

1. Give your data at least a full week after the completion date, then rerun the comparisons (practitioner judgment).
2. If losses cluster in a page type that matches a policy, fix that template first, using the August playbook.
3. If losses are spread evenly and match last year, treat it as seasonality and keep publishing.
4. Log what you changed and when. Google says algorithmic recovery is measured in months, so you need a record to judge it against.<sup>8</sup>
5. Recheck after the next confirmed spam update on Google's dashboard.
