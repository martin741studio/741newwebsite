# 88% of Small Business Websites Have Backlinks From Likely Spam Pages

## Methodology (as reported)
- Backlink records for 249 randomly chosen US small business websites, 56 industries, up to 1,000 backlinks per site.
- 68,859 live backlinks collected on 2026-09-23. Each has a third-party spam score from 0 to 100.
- A backlink counts as "spammy" at a score of 60 or higher.
- 60,673 text links had their anchor text analysed.

## Key findings (as reported)
1. 88% of small business websites have at least one backlink from a likely spam page.
2. On average 25.2% of a site's backlinks are spammy (median 24.7%). 11.6% of sites have half or more of their backlinks flagged.
3. One spam domain links to 204 of the 249 sites. Eight other domains link to 185 to 203 sites each.
4. Backlinks from the strongest linking sites are spammy 0.2% of the time, against 22.8% for links from rank 1 to 100.
5. 36% of backlinks use a bare web address as anchor text. 17.9% use the business name.
6. "website" is the most common anchor (4,346 links), then "visit website" (3,114).
7. Two gambling phrases are in the 15 most common anchors: "mix parlay" (748) and "slot gacor gampang menang" (599).

## Recommendations (as reported)
- Do not rush to disavow. Google says it ignores most spam links on its own.
- Keep disavow files for bought links, manual penalties, or sudden floods from one domain.
- Bare URL and "website" anchors are normal and need no fix.
- Earn links from strong, relevant sites (local press, associations, suppliers, partners).
- Review the most common anchors and the spam share once or twice a year. Watch for unexplained gambling, pharmacy or foreign-language phrases.
