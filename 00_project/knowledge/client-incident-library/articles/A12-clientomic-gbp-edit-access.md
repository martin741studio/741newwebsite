Four pathways can change what customers see on a Google Business Profile. An owner or Manager can edit information through a linked Google Account. An authorized representative can work on the profile after the business owner gives express permission. A member of the public can suggest an edit without becoming a user on the profile. Google may review an edit and may contact the profile's verified phone by call, text, or WhatsApp to confirm business details; information provided in response may update the profile (Google Business Profile Help, 2026).<sup>\[2\]</sup> These pathways are not interchangeable. Direct role access appears in People and access; a public suggestion does not. Google says people who do not own or manage a profile can still suggest an edit (Google Business Profile Help, 2026).<sup>\[2\]</sup> Google also requires authorized representatives to have the business owner's consent (Google Business Profile Help, 2026).<sup>\[3\]</sup> **A visible change does not by itself prove that a stranger has account access.**

An authorized representative is a person or company the owner has permitted to handle business information. Permission to help does not require primary ownership. Google's third-party rules tell representatives to keep the business owner informed and preserve the owner's control.<sup>\[3\]</sup>

Quotable distinction: A public Google Maps suggested edit is a proposal from someone who does not manage the profile; it is not evidence that the person has signed-in access to the business owner's Google Account.

A Google Business Profile Manager can perform most recurring profile work, but a Manager is not an Owner. Google Business Profile manager permissions include editing the business name, hours, address, phone number, services, attributes, photos, products, posts, and other daily profile information. Managers cannot add or remove users or remove the profile. Those controls belong to owners (Google Business Profile Help, 2026).<sup>\[1\]</sup> Google reviews profile changes before publishing them, so having permission to submit an edit does not mean every change will appear immediately.<sup>\[2\]</sup> Each person should use a separate Google Account rather than the owner's password. If someone needs to maintain hours, services, photos, or posts, Manager access is normally the narrower operating role. **Keep Primary ownership in a business-controlled account** and reserve additional Owner access for people who truly need user-management authority.

| Role | Profile edits | User management | Profile removal | Best fit | 
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary owner | Yes | Yes | Yes | Business-controlled account that must retain final control | 
| Additional owner | Yes | Yes | Yes | Trusted internal person who needs continuity and user control | 
| Manager | Yes | No | No | Employee or provider handling recurring profile work | 

The Google Business Profile owner vs Manager distinction is about control, not effort. A Manager may do nearly all daily maintenance while the owner keeps the ability to approve who has access.

New owners and managers must wait seven days before they can remove other users, delete a profile, or transfer primary ownership (Google Business Profile Help, 2026).<sup>\[1\]</sup> That delay is a role rule, not a recovery estimate.

Use the same owner-first model described in this guide to who should create a Google Business Profile: the business controls the primary role, and legitimate helpers receive only the access their work requires.

Quotable distinction: A Google Business Profile Manager has operational edit access, not user-management control: managers can update profile information, but only owners can add or remove users or remove the profile (Google Business Profile Help, 2026).

Follow this six-step access check:

1. Sign in with the business-controlled Google Account that should remain in control.
2. Open the relevant Google Business Profile.
3. Select Business Profile settings, then People and access.
4. Review every owner, Manager, and pending invitation. Record the name, role, and business reason for access.
5. Cancel unfamiliar invitations and remove unknown users when the signed-in account has Owner permission.
6. Keep the business owner as Primary owner and invite legitimate helpers through their separate Google Accounts.

If you find a role you do not recognize, record it before changing access. That preserves a clean timeline if the public information changes again.

Start with evidence, not an accusation. Record the exact field, the public wording, and the date you noticed the change. If another person controls the existing profile, an authorized business representative should request ownership of that profile rather than create a replacement. If it is denied, Google may provide an appeal path. If there is no response after three days, a Claim or Verify option may appear, but Google says that option is not always available.
