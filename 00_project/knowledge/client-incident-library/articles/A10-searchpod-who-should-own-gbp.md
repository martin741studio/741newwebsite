## Key facts

- Google Business Profile has distinct Primary Owner, Owner, and Manager access levels, and only Primary Owner can fully control who else has access to the listing.
- Reviews stay attached to the Business Profile listing itself and are not lost when access changes hands, as long as the listing itself is not suspended or merged.
- Google has suspended Business Profiles over name or address mismatches with the real business, a risk when a listing is managed without close coordination between the business and whoever holds access.
- Google provides a request process, through the Business Profile help center, for a business to reclaim ownership of a listing it believes belongs to it, typically requiring proof like business registration documents.
- A business email tied to the company's own domain, rather than a personal address or an agency's domain, is the more durable choice for holding Primary Owner long term.

## Primary Owner Versus Manager: Know the Difference

Google Business Profile has distinct access levels, and the top one is Primary Owner, which controls who else can be added, removed, or promoted on the listing. Below that sit Owner and Manager roles, which can edit information, respond to reviews, and post updates, but cannot remove the Primary Owner or transfer the profile without permission.

The business itself, meaning you or a person you trust inside the business, should hold Primary Owner using an email address the business controls long term, not a personal email tied to one employee or an agency's own domain. If the account that holds Primary Owner ever becomes inaccessible, so does control of your listing, including your reviews, photos, hours, and the ability to respond to customers.

An agency doing your local SEO or managing reviews only needs Manager access to do that work. Manager access lets them post updates, answer questions, and respond to reviews without ever needing the keys to the listing itself.

## Why This Distinction Actually Matters

A Google Business Profile carries real weight for a local business: it is often the first thing a nearby customer sees, and it holds your accumulated reviews, which do not transfer to a new listing if the original one is lost or locked. If an agency holds Primary Owner and the relationship ends badly, getting your own listing back can take a support request to Google and real delay, during which your profile may go unmanaged.

Google has also suspended profiles for policy violations tied to how they were set up, such as a business name that does not match the real name of the business, or an address that does not correspond to an actual location. If an agency manages the listing without close coordination with you, a well meaning change on their part can trigger exactly this kind of review or suspension.

Keeping Primary Owner with the business protects against both problems: a hard exit from a bad agency relationship, and a policy issue you did not know was happening on a listing you did not control.

## How to Set It Up Correctly From the Start

If your profile already exists, check your current ownership by signing in to Google Business Profile and looking at the Users section. If the agency's email shows as Primary Owner and yours does not appear at all, ask them to add your business email as an Owner immediately, even before you decide anything about the relationship long term.

If you are setting up a new listing with a new agency, ask them to verify and claim it under your business email from day one, then add themselves as a Manager. This is a small request that a professional agency will handle without pushback, since it does not limit their ability to do the work.

Use a business email tied to your domain, not a personal Gmail address belonging to one employee, so ownership survives staff turnover as cleanly as it survives an agency change.

## What to Do If You Do Not Currently Have Access

If you discover an agency holds Primary Owner and is unresponsive or uncooperative, Google has a process for requesting ownership of a Business Profile you believe belongs to your business, through the Business Profile help center, which typically asks you to verify your relationship to the business in question.

Document your relationship to the business while you go through this: proof of business registration, a utility bill at the listed address, or other evidence Google's process asks for. This can take time, which is exactly why getting Owner access confirmed early with any new provider matters more than fixing it after the fact.

Once access is restored to you, add any agency going forward as a Manager only, and keep at least one Owner level login inside the business itself that does not depend on any single employee or outside provider.
