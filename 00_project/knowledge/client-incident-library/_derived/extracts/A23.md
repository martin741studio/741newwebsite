# A23 6000 Euro für Werbung – was tun bei überraschend hoher Agentur-Rechnung?
Source: articles/A23-anwaltde-6000-euro-werbung-agentur-rechnung.md, ARTICLE, published 2026-08-27, RAin Anuscha Blanke on anwalt.de, https://www.anwalt.de/rechtstipps/6000-euro-fuer-werbung-was-tun-bei-ueberraschend-hoher-agentur-rechnung-280851.html
Reliability: Lawyer's Rechtstipp from the client side. Fresh (2026-08-27). Medium closeness: it shows how a client lawyer attacks an agency invoice. Background, not legal advice.

## Practice from this source
- The client lawyer first checks the contract type: service (Dienstvertrag) or work (Werkvertrag).
- A contract for one defined online ad is usually a work contract (BGH VII ZR 71/17, 22.03.2018).
- Ongoing support or "optimisation" without a defined result is more likely a service contract.
- The lawyer asks the agency to prove the work done: screenshots, statistics, campaign logs.
- Without proof of work, the lawyer disputes the invoice.
- Hidden price clauses or unclear renewals in standard terms (AGB) can be invalid as surprising.
- If no price was clearly agreed, only the usual pay applies (§ 632 BGB).
- The lawyer cites § 627 BGB: services of a higher kind based on special trust can end at any time.
- The client side gathers emails, call notes, invoices and payment records. The agency should keep the same records.
- Many disputes end in a settlement with a reduced amount or instalments.

## Use for client-access-lockout
- D: Expect a demand for proof of work. Keep dated evidence for each month: changed URLs, reports, GBP posts, emails.
- E: Write offers and invoices with a clear price, term and service list. Unclear price or term terms weaken our claim.
- E: Expect the client side to test § 627 BGB. Prepare a view on whether our program is a "service of a higher kind".

## Limits
- The article targets cold-call advertising sellers. Our case is a regular program client.
- It says a service contract can "always" be terminated. That is not general law; it depends on term, § 621 and § 627 BGB.
- It does not cover default of acceptance (Annahmeverzug) or access blocks.
