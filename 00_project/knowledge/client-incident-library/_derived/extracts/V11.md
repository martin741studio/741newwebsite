## Practice from this source
- The August 2026 spam update rolled out from about 18 to 21 August. It was global, for all languages.
- Google called it a normal spam update. It was not a specific link spam update.
- If rankings move around an update date, "do not immediately assume your website is broken."
- Google uses an AI-based spam system called SpamBrain.
- Google defines spam as action mainly to manipulate rankings. Examples: mass low-value content, keyword stuffing, near-identical city pages, link manipulation, site reputation abuse.
- Local landing pages are not bad. Each page needs a real reason: different service, problem, examples, pictures.
- Ask the agency "why would these websites naturally want to mention my company?" Do not count links.
- The speaker claims, from his own tests, that the update was also about backlink quality. He says "it's not publicly said." This goes beyond Google's statement.
- After a drop, do 5 checks: Search Console, content quality, backlink profile, technical problems, overall strategy.
- Check 1: use Search Console to find which pages and queries lost visibility.
- Check 4: rule out other causes first: indexing, migration, redirects, robots.txt, noindex tags, server problems.
- He says an agency without Search Console access "is not a real SEO agency."
- Google's spam systems may need months to see that a site complies again. A fix today does not return rankings tomorrow.
- Warning: distrust anyone who promises to fix a spam update penalty "in 7 days."
- He says Google indexes only about 1% of produced content. He gives no source. Do not reuse this number.

## Use for client-access-lockout
- B Account ownership and access: the source says Search Console is the first check after any drop. Without access, the agency cannot do that check. This supports "blocked access stops the agreed work."
- A Verification of claims: the 5 checks give a calm, ordered way to test a scary claim before any action.
- F Communication with a mistrustful client: the business-owner framing and the "no 7-day fix" warning help to set honest timelines.
- G Competitor fake audit poaching: the video itself ends by selling an audit after a scary update. It shows the pattern: update fear, then "first thing we recommend is an audit."
