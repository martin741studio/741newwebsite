# O06 Google Search Central: Spam policies for Google web search
Source: official-docs/search-central-spam-policies.md, OFFICIAL, living page (last updated 2026-08-28 per page metadata), fetched 2026-10-12, https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies
Reliability: High. Google's binding policy text for organic search.

## Practice from this source
- Google detects policy violations "through automated systems and, as needed, human review that can result in a manual action".
- Link spam is "creating links to or from a site primarily for the purpose of manipulating search rankings".
- Examples: buying or selling links, excessive link exchanges, "using automated programs or services to create links to your site", low-quality directory or bookmark links, widely distributed footer links, forum signature links.
- The policy defines link spam by purpose and by who creates the links. It targets links a site owner buys or arranges.
- The page does not say that unsolicited links from third parties are a violation by the target site.
- Hacked content and malicious practices are separate policies. They act on the site itself.

## Use for client-access-lockout
- A/C: Ask one question about each flagged link: did the agency or the client create or buy it? If no, it is not "link spam by the site" under this policy.
- A: Check the agency's own work against this list (no bought links, no automated links, no low-quality directories). If something fits, admit it.
- G: A report that calls third-party spam links the client's "violation" misreads this policy.

## Limits
- The page does not contain the words "negative SEO". Google's statements on ignoring unsolicited links come from the disavow page (O02) and from secondary reports (A01, A06).
