# A12 Can Someone Else Change Your Google Business Profile Information?
Source: articles/A12-clientomic-gbp-edit-access.md, ARTICLE, published 2026-09-02, Ivan Kalin / Clientomic, https://clientomic.com/blog/google-business-profile-edit-access
Reliability: Medium-high. Local SEO practitioner. Cites Google Business Profile Help throughout.

## Practice from this source
- Four ways a profile can change: owner or manager edits, an authorized representative, public "suggest an edit", Google's own updates.
- A public suggested edit is not proof that someone has access.
- Six-step access check: sign in with the business account, open People and access, record each owner, manager and pending invite with the reason, cancel unknown invites, remove unknown users, keep the business as Primary owner.
- "Start with evidence, not an accusation." Record the field, the public wording and the date before you change anything.
- "If you find a role you do not recognize, record it before changing access." This keeps a clean timeline.
- Authorized representatives must keep the owner informed and in control.
- Request ownership through Google's flow. Do not create a duplicate profile.

## Use for client-access-lockout
- D: "Record before you change" is a good general incident rule. Take dated screenshots first, then act.
- F: "Evidence, not accusation" fits both sides: the agency should not accuse the client, and should not accept accusations without evidence.
- B: An agency is an authorized representative. Its duty is to keep the owner informed and in control.
