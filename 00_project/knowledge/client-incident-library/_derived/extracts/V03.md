## Practice from this source
- Before you answer a claim, check the facts: "Let me check the details before I give you an answer." Never guess about an important client problem.
- Correct a false claim without "you're wrong": "I understand your point. However, according to our records, the situation is slightly different."
- Point to the written agreement as the neutral source: "According to our agreement, ..." and "Let me clarify what was agreed."
- If the facts show that you were wrong, say so at once: "You're right. My email did say Friday. I apologize for the confusion."
- If the error is yours, use three steps: recognize the problem, apologize, say what you do now. Do not say "I'm sorry, but".
- If the client made the mistake, do not blame. Say "there seems to have been a misunderstanding" and check the facts together.
- For an emotional complaint, ask specific questions, for example "Could you confirm which orders were affected?" This moves the talk to facts.
- In an angry email, reply to the problem, not to the emotion. Take time before you answer.
- Do not promise a result that you cannot control: "I don't want to make a promise before I confirm the details."
- Give a specific time for the next update ("by 3:00"), not "soon".
- If the client is rude, set a calm limit: "I want to help, but I'd appreciate it if we could keep the conversation professional."
- Work outside the agreed scope needs extra time or cost. Say so before you start, not after.
- Confirm changes in writing: "Just to confirm, you'd like us to ... Please let me know if I've understood everything correctly."
- At the end, check that the client agrees with the solution, and follow up later to make sure that the problem stays solved.
