# V10 Important Change to SEO (New Google Spam Update)
Source: videos/V10-important-change-to-seo-new-google-spam-update.md, VIDEO, uploaded 2026-08-19, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8vj7VmOJti0
Speaker/channel: Nathan Gotch (internal only; never name in client material)
Reliability: SEO trainer and course seller; says openly that he does not fully trust Google; much is his guess about the August 2026 spam update; close to our case on the spam-update question, weak on links.

## Practice from this source
- Google told Barry Schwartz that the August 2026 spam update did not target link spam or site reputation abuse.
- The speaker doubts this: "I don't fully trust what Google will say." This is his opinion, not data.
- His list of probable targets is a guess: scaled unoriginal content, scraped content, doorway pages, keyword stuffing, expired domain abuse.
- He names local doorway pages as a classic risk: many city pages where only the city name changes. "Just don't do that."
- He says that easy links (social profiles, free profile links) have no value. Only links that need effort have value.
- He claims evidence that expired domains do not "reset". He says Google claims they do. This contradicts Google's statement.
- He gives a free checklist of about 70 variables that he says come from Google documentation.
- After an update, do not overreact: "Don't start going absolutely crazy changing a bunch of stuff."
- Let the update settle. Build an action list first. Then fix the big, clear issues one by one.
- A ranking loss usually has several causes together, not one small thing.
- A loss is usually recoverable. Only very manipulative sites may not recover.
- Note: he also suggests AI to make city pages "somewhat unique". This is weak advice. Google judges doorway pages by purpose, not by wording.

## Use for client-access-lockout
- C Disavow and spam links: Google's own statement says the August 2026 spam update did not target link spam. A spam-link list is therefore no evidence that this update hit the site.
- A Verification of claims: use the "do not overreact" practice. Check data first, then act. Do not let a scary audit force fast changes.
- F Communication with a mistrustful client: the "several causes, recoverable" point helps to calm the client without false promises.

## Limits
- No content on disavow, negative SEO, or how to read a backlink audit.
- Speaker distrusts Google and sells training; his target list is speculation.
- Covers the August update, not the September 2026 update.
