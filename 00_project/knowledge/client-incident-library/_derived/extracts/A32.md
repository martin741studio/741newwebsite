# A32 Professional Apology Email to a Client: Templates for Real Situations

Reliability: Email-client vendor blog. It promotes its own product in a few places. The writing advice is concrete and matches other apology guides. It is generic, not about SEO.

## Practice from this source
- Collect five facts first: what happened, when, what the client saw, what is fixed, and the next promise you can keep.
- Call first when the issue involves money, access problems or reputation risk. Then send the email as a written recap.
- Apologize in the first sentence. Name the exact mistake in plain words.
- Say the client impact in one sentence. Do not exaggerate it.
- Keep the cause to one factual line. The article says "Long backstories sound defensive."
- Do not use "if you felt", "unfortunately" or passive phrases like "a miscommunication occurred".
- If a vendor caused part of the problem, still own the client impact.
- Give the fix, the owner and the next update with an exact day, time and time zone.
- Offer a credit or refund only when the issue cost the client time, money or work. Get internal approval first.
- Apologize for the impact, not for facts you cannot yet confirm. Over-apologizing invites "this wasn't even your fault".
- Confirm any change to invoices, links or login resets through a second channel.
- Set a reminder for every promise before you send.

## Use for client-access-lockout
- F Communication with a mistrustful client: use this structure for the part where we admit a real mistake. Keep it short and specific.
- F Communication with a mistrustful client: do not apologize for false audit claims. Apologize only for what we checked and confirmed.
- D Evidence and documentation: the "five facts" step forces us to verify before we write.

## Limits
- Generic customer-service advice. It does not cover a client who accuses the agency of something it did not do.
- Product plugs for Mailbird and Parakeet are in the text.
