# V06 10 Years of Building an Agency: The Lessons Nobody Sees
Source: videos/V06-10-years-of-building-an-agency-the-lessons-nobody-sees.md, VIDEO, uploaded 2026-08-12, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-2PItawmGiQ
Speaker/channel: Belkins Podcast (managing director of a B2B lead-generation agency, 10 years in delivery and account management) (internal only; never name in client material)
Reliability: An experienced agency delivery lead talks about retention and unhappy clients. The podcast is the agency's own marketing channel (bias: brand image). The service is outbound lead generation, not local SEO. About one fifth of the 77 minutes is relevant to our case.

## Practice from this source
- Do not learn about the client's unhappiness at the cancellation. Ask the client for feedback early and often. In one lost case, the team only heard one week before the cancellation.
- When the client and the agency agree that something does not work, change it at once. He asks: if the fix takes two weeks, "are we just charging them for fun?"
- Before a hard call with an unhappy client, hold an internal review. Look at what we did wrong first, not only at what the client did wrong.
- Be honest that failure is often two-sided. Some faults are ours and some are the client's.
- A good client says "we are here with you, let's make it work." A client who only wants results without cooperation fails in about 80% of cases, in his experience.
- Some clients do not accept a hard truth and stop the cooperation. Expect this result as one possible outcome.
- Do not trust AI tools for facts without checking. He saw AI give wrong data, and it agreed when he challenged it. "It's AI failure" is not an excuse that a client accepts.
- Collect all data from the work and analyse it. Then go back to the client with "this is what we learned" and ask for input.
- Staff stay motivated when the client gives feedback and treats them as part of the team.

## Use for client-access-lockout
- A Verification of claims: tool and AI outputs can be wrong. Check each item of the scary audit by hand, and check our own data before we use it as proof.
- D Evidence and documentation: a data review of all our work ("this is what we learned") is a good format for the evidence pack.
- H Retention vs clean separation: an early feedback loop prevents surprise lockouts. A client who refuses cooperation (for example, no access) is a strong sign for a clean separation.
- F Communication with a mistrustful client: do an internal review of our own mistakes before the client call.

## Limits
- The context is US B2B outbound lead generation. It says nothing about account access, Search Console, disavow or competitor audits.
- The advice comes as stories in a promotional interview, not as a tested method.
