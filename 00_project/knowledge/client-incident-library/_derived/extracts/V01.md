# V01 How to Start Any Conversation the Right Way (Chris Fenning)
Source: videos/V01-how-to-start-any-conversation-the-right-way-chris-fenning.md, VIDEO, uploaded 2026-10-11, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CKAyfGItpgg
Speaker/channel: TED, speaker Chris Fenning (internal only; never name in client material)
Reliability: TED talk by a workplace-communication trainer. He claims 75,000 people tested the method. He has no visible sales pitch in the talk. The topic is general work messages, not client conflict, so the fit to our case is indirect.

## Practice from this source
- Start each message with one sentence that names the topic. Make it specific, for example "I'd like to talk about July sales figures."
- In the second sentence, say what you want the reader to do with the message (answer a question, make a decision, only read).
- If the reader does not know what to do with a message, attention drops. The speaker calls this selective attention.
- Put the main point in one sentence near the start. He quotes the military rule: "Put the bottom line up front."
- Give the details only after topic, intent and point are clear.
- Before you write, ask three questions: What is my topic? What do I want the other person to do? What is my point?

## Use for client-access-lockout
- F Communication with a mistrustful client: use topic, intent and point as the opening structure of the first message after the lockout. Example: topic = removed Search Console access; intent = we ask for a decision; point = work is paused until access is back.
- E Contract and pausing work: the "point first" rule makes sure that the client reads the pause notice. The pause notice must not be hidden in the details.

## Limits
- The talk does not cover conflict, mistrust, apologies or technical claims.
- It gives a message structure only. It gives no content for our case.
