# A35 6 Signs It's Time to Fire a Client, Even a Nice One
Source: articles/A35-wunderlandmedia-signs-fire-a-client.md, ARTICLE, published 2026-08-15 (metadata modified 2026-08-25), Kemal Esensoy / Wunderlandmedia, https://wunderlandmedia.com/signs-its-time-to-fire-a-client
Reliability: Founder of a small web and SEO agency writes from eight years of own practice. Personal opinion, no data. It is about ending a relationship, not about a dispute.

## Practice from this source
- Check the economics: hourly earnings from this client against the newest client.
- If the only reason left to keep a client is guilt, the author says that is not a reason.
- If you can name what you would do with the freed hours, the decision is mostly made.
- End at a clean moment: after the last invoice is paid and with no work mid-flight.
- Keep the exit message to two sentences: a clear end date and a promise of a smooth transition.
- Give no fake reasons and no long essay. The author says a long email reads like an apology and invites negotiation.
- The handover decides your reputation: documented access, transferred accounts and a summary of how things work.
- Offer a referral to a better-fit provider if you can.
- Expect a short revenue dip after the exit.

## Use for client-access-lockout
- H Retention vs clean separation and handover: use the short, calm exit wording and the "thorough to the last day" handover if we choose separation.
- F Communication with a mistrustful client: keep separation messages short. Do not justify at length.

## Limits
- About friendly clients and fit, not about a client who blocks access in fear.
- One person's experience. No contract or legal angle.
