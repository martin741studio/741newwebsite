# O07 Google Search Central: Do you need an SEO?
Source: official-docs/search-central-do-you-need-an-seo.md, OFFICIAL, living page (metadata 2026-06-05), fetched 2026-10-12, https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/do-i-need-seo
Reliability: High. Google's own advice to site owners who hire SEOs. It is also the standard the client will compare us against.

## Practice from this source
- Ask the SEO: "Will you share with me all the changes you make to my site, and provide detailed information about your recommendations and the reasoning behind them?"
- If an SEO offers an audit, "only grant read access to Search Console (at this stage, don't grant them write access)".
- "Before making significant changes to your site based on a third-party tool's audit," check the advice against official Google guidance.
- Ask if they "cite official Google documentation as supporting evidence".
- "Be wary of SEO firms and web consultants or agencies that email you out of the blue."
- "No one can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google."
- "Be careful if a company is secretive or won't clearly explain what they intend to do."
- "You are responsible for the actions of any companies you hire." The site owner carries the risk.
- "You should never have to link to an SEO." Avoid link popularity schemes.

## Use for client-access-lockout
- F/D: The client has a right to a full change log and the reasons. Give it without being asked twice.
- G: Google itself tells site owners to check third-party audits against official docs and to distrust unsolicited SEO emails. Quote this neutrally.
- G: An outside party that does an "audit" should get read access only. Recommend this to the client for any second opinion.
- F: The client's fear is rational: Google tells her she is responsible for the agency's actions.

## Limits
- Written for site owners, not for agencies. No conflict-handling guidance.
