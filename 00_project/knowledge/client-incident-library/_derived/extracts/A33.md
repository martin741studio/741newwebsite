# A33 What effective SEO client onboarding looks like: The first 90 days
Source: articles/A33-sel-seo-client-onboarding-first-90-days.md, ARTICLE, published 2026-10-02, Kelly-Anne Crean / Search Engine Land, https://searchengineland.com/effective-seo-client-onboarding-first-90-days-492666
Reliability: Agency practitioner writes in a major SEO trade medium (owned by Semrush). Experience-based, no product pitch. It is about prevention at the start, not about an incident.

## Practice from this source
- Ask early: who in the business owns the website, the content and the development work?
- Ask what the client expects to see in month one, three and six. Misaligned timelines cause much of the friction.
- Document everything from the kickoff. Send it to the client for confirmation. Store it where both sides can see it.
- Agree on responsibilities in writing. The article says friction comes from "assumptions about who owns what".
- The agency owns strategy, delivery and communication. It must flag issues early, not wait for the report.
- The client must give access to analytics, Search Console and the CMS. The client must also give business context.
- Technical SEO work is mostly invisible. Without regular updates, clients assume nothing happens.
- Use a short and fixed rhythm of updates on work in progress.
- Lead reports with business results. Use SEO metrics only to explain them.
- Do not promise a result or a timeframe before you know the start point.

## Use for client-access-lockout
- D Evidence and documentation: confirmed kickoff notes and written responsibilities are evidence of the agreed scope.
- F Communication with a mistrustful client: invisible work creates doubt. A third-party audit lands harder when the client never saw our work log.
- B Account access: the article names Search Console access as a client duty for the work. Use this to explain why blocked access stops the work.

## Limits
- About onboarding. It gives no steps for an incident or a lockout.
- Written for larger clients with development teams, not a single practitioner.
