# A34 Before you hire an SEO auditor, know what you're paying for
Source: articles/A34-sel-before-you-hire-seo-auditor.md, ARTICLE, published 2026-09-11, Adam Riemer / Search Engine Land, https://searchengineland.com/before-hire-seo-auditor-488053
Reliability: Experienced SEO consultant writes in a major SEO trade medium. He also sells audits, so he has an interest. The advice is aimed at buyers of audits. It is useful to judge a third-party audit.

## Practice from this source
- The author says many audits "only crawl the site and call it an audit".
- A real audit explains which issues matter, why they exist and what was done on purpose.
- Tools and LLMs do not know technical debt or intentional choices. A crawl can flag things that are correct.
- A to-do list from a crawl can contain items that "could cause your website to lose SEO traffic".
- Buyers can test the same crawler tools to see if a report is only a tool export.
- A good audit shows explanations and URL lists, and fits the advice to the site and business.
- Ask the auditor to mark which parts come from a crawler and which come from manual review.
- Intentional settings, for example robots.txt blocks or thin pages, can look like errors to a tool.
- A good auditor asks about such choices before or during the audit.

## Use for client-access-lockout
- G Competitor fake audit poaching: use these criteria to check the third-party "backlink audit". Is it a tool export without manual review?
- A Verification: ask which tool made the spam list and what manual checks were done.
- F Communication with a mistrustful client: explain calmly that a tool flag is not a finding. Show the difference with our own data.

## Limits
- About site audits in general, not backlink audits or disavow.
- Written for buyers. It gives no wording for an agency that a client now distrusts.
