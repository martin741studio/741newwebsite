# V02 How to Deal with Difficult Clients in English

Speaker/channel: Business English Podcast (internal only; never name in client material)
Reliability: Two hosts of a business-English learning channel. They teach language, not client management, and they use scripted role plays. The advice matches common customer-service practice. One role play (agency client who adds scope) is close to agency work; nothing covers tool access or SEO.

## Practice from this source
- Do not explain or defend in the first reply to an angry client. First show that you understand. Formula: "Empathy first, solution second."
- You can say "I'm really sorry you're dealing with this" before you know whose fault it is. The hosts call it "a human response, not a legal confession."
- Take ownership of the fix, not the guilt. "I take full responsibility" means "I own the solution now."
- Do not say "calm down", "it's not my fault", "you should have" or "as I already explained". These phrases make the conflict worse.
- Keep the talk on the future: what happens next, who does what, by when.
- If you escalate, name a specific person or role and a time. "I'll see what I can do" is weak.
- Give a fixed update interval, for example 24 hours, and one single point of contact.
- When the client gives hard feedback that is true, accept it briefly: "That is fair feedback, and I'm sorry." Do not add an excuse.
- When the client threatens to leave, do not panic or argue. Remind the client of the shared history and say that you want to keep the relationship.
- Use facts and numbers, not feelings. "Numbers are neutral. Feelings can be argued with."
- To say no: "Boundary first, alternative second." Close one door and open another at once.
- Present the problem as "you and me against the problem". Put the facts on the table and look at them together.
- After each difficult call, send a follow-up email the same day with three parts: thanks, what we agreed, next step with a date.

## Use for client-access-lockout
- F Communication with a mistrustful client: empathy first, no defence, no "you should have", facts and numbers as neutral ground.
- D Evidence and documentation: the same-day written follow-up records what was agreed and protects both sides.
- E Contract and pausing work: "boundary first, alternative second" fits the pause message. Example: no work without access, but we restart the same day when access is back.
- H Retention vs clean separation: the reply to the "we will look at other providers" threat (calm, refer to history, written action plan) gives a model for the retention attempt.

## Limits
- The episode does not cover how to verify technical claims or how to deal with a third-party report.
- The role plays are scripted and in English. The phrases need adaptation for German clients and the du/Sie register.
