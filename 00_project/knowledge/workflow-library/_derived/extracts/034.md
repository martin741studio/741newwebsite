# 034 How to Build a One Person AI Business (Using Claude Code)
Source: Nate Herk | AI Automation, 2026-08-11, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LVAHYV4Xrto
Reliability: Medium-low for Claude Code technique. This is a business-model video from an ex-agency owner who promotes his paid community (AIS+) and courses. Prices and stats are his own or cited reports, not verified. Very little Claude Code mechanics.

## Rules (IF situation THEN action. Why.)
- R1 IF you sell AI work THEN position as a partner who sells outcomes, not a builder who sells features. Businesses pay more for outcomes.
- R2 IF you scope a project THEN tie it to one of three buckets: get more customers, make each customer worth more, or cut costs. If it moves none, skip it.
- R3 IF you scope a build THEN fill four blanks before you start: bucket, specific KPI, baseline today, target after 60 days. No four blanks means no project. He cites 13% of AI projects reaching production (Capgemini).
- R4 IF you find the real problem THEN have the client walk through operations in order, front to back. Listen for the first spot with friction. That spot is usually the constraint. The fix may not need AI.
- R5 IF you sell in steps THEN use the service ladder: education (1 hour, 100 to 500 USD, default 200), audit (500 to 3,000 USD), project (2,500 to 10,000 USD), retainer (3,000 to 10,000 USD per month).
- R6 IF the audit is the deliverable THEN make it a 10 to 40 page deck or PDF of waste and automation chances, ending with a proposal for the first project.
- R7 IF you price a project THEN anchor on the manual cost. Example: 15 hours a week cut to 1 hour. Be ready to explain how you reached the number.
- R8 IF you are new THEN first build your own AI operating system in Claude Code for 2 weeks. Start with a scheduled morning brief (calendar, tasks, inbox). Then adapt it for clients.
- R9 IF you have no industry network THEN do not niche down on day one. Aim for five conversations with five real owners in month one. Niche down after the same problem appears three times. Pick a niche early only if you worked in it, your warm list is there, or you are obsessed with it.
- R10 IF you look for clients THEN go in order: warm outreach, Upwork (keywords: AI integration, workflow automation, Claude Code workflows), then building in public.
- R11 IF you do outreach THEN aim for your first 10 no's, not the first yes. Each conversation is data.
- R12 IF a project has results THEN turn it into a case study with baseline and after numbers plus a testimonial. Use the client profile as your next niche avatar.
- R13 IF you scope a product THEN ask Claude for the smallest valuable product in 2 weeks: core feature for 80% of the pain, features to cut from V1, tech stack, competitors.

## Mistakes to avoid
- Jumping straight to retainers without proof or case studies.
- Building without a KPI and baseline.
- Choosing a niche from zero data and defending it for months.
- Treating each "no" as a verdict.
- Building an AI agent when a simple workflow solves the constraint.

## Relevant for a non-developer business workflow (SEO/marketing agency, Cowork, scheduling, client deliverables)
- The four blanks (bucket, KPI, baseline, 60-day target) work for local SEO program scoping.
- Audit as a paid first step with a PDF and a first-project proposal matches an agency entry offer.
- Case study = baseline plus after numbers plus testimonial. Fits client proof.
- Skip the tone of cold outreach advice if it conflicts with your own rules.
