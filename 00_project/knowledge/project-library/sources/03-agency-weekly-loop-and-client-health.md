# 03 — The agency weekly loop and early warning numbers (external, 2 sources)

Fetched 2026-11-03 via search excerpts.
- Productive.io, "How to Run Agency Project Management in 2026? Full Guide" (updated 2026-08-23): https://productive.io/blog/agency-project-management-guide/
- AMSOS, "Agency Project Management. A Guide for Agency Owners": https://amsos.agency/publication/agency-project-management

## Findings
- Most of agency PM happens in the weekly loop, not in the plan: status in ONE place, one short check-in, changes priced before work starts. — Productive
- The usual failure: status lives in four places (task board, client email, timesheet, the PM's head). — Productive
- Hybrid delivery needs one named person who keeps the milestone track and the task track in step; otherwise the task track finishes work the milestone track never approved. — Productive
- Estimating: list deliverables not phases; assign to roles not names; add the un-estimated block (kickoff, review, revision rounds, PM, handover) = a fifth to a quarter of total. — Productive
- Client health: ask for a score after each milestone, then watch two behaviours: reply speed and feedback rounds per piece. "A client who used to reply in a day and now takes four has usually decided something." — Productive
- Account manager owns relationship and commercial talk; project manager owns plan and budget per job. — AMSOS

## Marketing and not transferable
- Productive sells agency software; hours and invoicing sections assume time-and-materials billing; 741 sells fixed programmes.

## Usable for 741
The Project Manager is the named person who keeps roadmap milestones (client_goals) and work_items in step. One status place = the portal. Two early-warning numbers per client in the weekly line: client reply speed (days) and open decision cards older than 7 days. The un-estimated block explains why "1–2 h per client per day" in the Upwork model needs a 20–25 % reserve.
