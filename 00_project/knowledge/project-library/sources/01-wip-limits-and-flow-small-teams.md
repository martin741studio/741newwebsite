## Findings
- Little's Law: cycle time = WIP ÷ throughput; lower WIP, faster delivery. "A team with no WIP limit often has eight items 80 % done and zero delivered." — Rock
- WIP limit rule of thumb: number of people working in that stage plus one (Teamwork) or 1.5× the people (IPM); start conservative, adjust after two cycles. — Teamwork, IPM
- Write the limit into the column name ("In Progress (3)"); exceeding it is a team decision, never a silent click. — Rock
- Pull, do not push: a doer pulls the next card when capacity opens; a manager assigning into a full column breaks flow. — IPM
- Idle capacity is visible when a downstream column is empty while an upstream one is full. — IPM
- Kanban fits continuous client maintenance and reactive work; Scrum fits fixed-scope builds; agencies mix both. — Rock
- Each card: title, one responsible person, due date, supporting link. — IPM
- "As the freeway approaches 100 % capacity it becomes a parking lot" (Benson); do not plan people at full utilisation. — Rock

## Marketing and not transferable
- Tool vendors; dollar-value metrics and multi-column boards assume a software team.
- 741's doers are part-time, remote and partly AI; the WIP rule still holds per person, the board tooling is the portal.

## Usable for 741
WIP limit per doer = 2 active work items (one person plus one); the Project Manager flags a third as "parked" with a reason. Per client, at most one "in progress" item per milestone. Pull, not push: the daily list offers the next items; the doer confirms the pull.
