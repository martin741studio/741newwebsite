# 02 — Single-owner accountability (RACI, DRI) and what the accountable person must do (external, 3 sources)

## Findings
- Golden rule: exactly one Accountable owner per task; two means nobody, zero means nobody. — IPM
- In small teams Responsible and Accountable are often the same person; fine if the distinction (doing vs owning the outcome) stays clear. — IPM
- A task with an R but no A "belongs to everyone and, in practice, to no one"; an un-assignable A is a governance gap to resolve before work starts. — IPM
- Responsibility can be delegated; accountability cannot. — Connexxo (McGrath & Whitty)
- The accountable person must give five things: clear expectations (outcome, measure, method), clear capability (skills, resources, or reassign), clear measurement, clear feedback, clear consequences. "Miss any one and accountability falls through that gap." — Connexxo
- Authority must match accountability; an accountable owner creates the conditions for success, not waits for results. — Connexxo
- DRI = one named person per decision or outcome; RACI for many-stakeholder projects. — Unicorn Labs
- Too many Rs on one person is the second most common error. — IPM
- The "contract game": one side defines, the other builds, neither owns the whole problem; every miss has a ready excuse. Structural, not a people problem. — Connexxo
