## Key points
- Asking clients to create an account is a common way to stall a review.
- Best tools cut feedback to one link and a couple of clicks.
- Clients should point at the exact spot, not describe it in a paragraph.
- Auto-captured browser and device data cuts follow-up questions.
- Sending clients an old version is a top cause of wasted revisions.
- Version compare helps reviewers see what changed.
- Reviews stall when clients forget. Manual chasing does not scale.
- Automated reminders keep sign-off moving.
- Before round one, tell clients what to click, how many rounds, and the deadline.
- Split client and internal feedback so they do not talk over each other.
- Feedback spread over email and chat weakens the record.
- A record of who approved what, and when, settles later disputes.
- Rigid, mandatory approval chains can be too heavy for quick client sign-off.
- Cites a Microsoft figure: about 117 emails a day, most skimmed in under a minute.
