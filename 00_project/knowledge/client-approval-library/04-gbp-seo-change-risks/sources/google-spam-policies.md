# Spam policies for Google web search (Google Search Central)

- URL: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies
- Type: Google official documentation (living page), last updated 2026-08-28
- Fetch date: 2026-10-13

## Key points

- Doorway abuse: pages built to rank for similar queries that funnel users to one destination.
- Google gives location examples: many pages or domains aimed at cities or regions that funnel to one page.
- Scaled content abuse: many pages made mainly to manipulate rankings, often with unoriginal content. Generative AI used to mass-produce low-value pages is an example.
- Link spam includes guest posts, press releases and articles with optimized (keyword-rich) anchor text, especially paid ones.
- Keyword-rich links in footers, widgets and templates also count as link spam.
- Paid links are fine only with rel="nofollow" or rel="sponsored".
- Site reputation abuse: third-party content hosted to use the host's ranking signals.
- Google detects spam with automated systems and human review. Violating sites rank lower or disappear.
- Manual actions show in Search Console. Algorithmic spam demotions do not.

## Relevance for 741

- Thin city pages with swapped place names are a known doorway pattern. New location pages need unique local content and agency review.
- AI-generated pages at scale are a risk. Each page must add real value.
- Clients must not add keyword-rich footer links or buy links. These edits need a lock.
