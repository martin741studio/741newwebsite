## Key points

- Sterling Sky calls the primary category "the one decision you can't screw up". Google offers more than 4,100 categories.
- Case: an HVAC company changed its primary category from "Air Conditioning Repair Service" to "Air Conditioning Contractor". The business fell from position 1 to position 31.
- A quick revert of the wrong category restored the rankings.
- Department or practitioner listings must not use a primary category that conflicts with the main listing.
- Services (predefined and custom) play a large role in ranking. Service names must make sense (a law firm must not list an injury as a "service").
- Hours: longer opening hours can help rankings, because Google prefers businesses that are open at search time.
- URL test: a law firm practitioner listing linked to a niche page (bus accident, then bicycle accident). Rankings for those terms improved after a couple of weeks.
- For most businesses the homepage is the best GBP link, because it has the most authority.
- Linking the GBP to a page that already ranks well organically can hurt the organic result (Google limits duplicate appearances, the "diversity update").
- Sterling Sky recommends UTM parameters on the GBP website link for tracking.

## Relevance for 741

- Primary category = highest ranking risk of all GBP fields. Lock it. Only the agency changes it, with a baseline scan before and a scan after.
- The GBP website URL is a ranking lever. A change needs the same process as a category change.
- Keep the previous value of every critical field in the change log so that a revert takes minutes.
