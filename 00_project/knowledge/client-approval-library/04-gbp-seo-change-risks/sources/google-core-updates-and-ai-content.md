## Key points

- Google runs broad core updates several times a year. They do not target single sites.
- After a drop, first check that the update has finished. Then wait at least one week and compare with the week before the update.
- A small position loss needs no drastic action. A large, lasting loss needs a full content review.
- Google warns against quick fixes based on SEO folklore.
- Delete content only as a last resort. Rewriting or restructuring is the preferred fix.
- Some changes show effect in days. Google may need months to confirm an improvement. The next core update can be the next chance.
- The October 2026 AI content guidance says AI can help with research and structure.
- Mass-produced AI pages without added value can break the scaled content abuse policy.
- Google says to fact-check all AI output by hand, including titles, meta descriptions, structured data and alt text.
- Google refers to quality rater guideline sections on scaled content and low-effort main content.

## Relevance for 741

- Do not ship large site edits during a running core or spam update. Wait until the rollout ends, then measure.
- A content rewrite is a medium-risk change. It can help or hurt. It needs a before/after check, not a gut decision.
- Client copy edits that come from AI tools need a fact check. Health and legal claims (HWG) raise the stakes.
