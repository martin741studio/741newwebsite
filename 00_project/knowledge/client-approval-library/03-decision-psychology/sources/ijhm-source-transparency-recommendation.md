# Unveiling the black box: How source transparency shapes GenAI-driven hotel recommendation adoption

- URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0278431926003257 (DOI 10.1016/j.ijhm.2026.104878)
- Authors: Yi Bu, Mai Nguyen, Yunen Zhang, Isaac Cheah, Anwar Sadat Shimul
- Journal: International Journal of Hospitality Management, Vol. 140 (January 2027 issue)
- Published online: 2026-08-11
- Type: Peer-reviewed; one field experiment in a hotel plus two online experiments

## Key points

- High source transparency means showing where a recommendation comes from and what it is based on.
- Field experiment: 92.7% of guests (102/110) accepted the recommended room with high transparency vs 83.6% (92/110) with low transparency.
- Rejections nearly halved: 7.3% vs 16.4%.
- Transparency works through two paths: the recommendation feels more authentic and less manipulative.
- The effect is strongest for "prevention-focused" people, who want to avoid mistakes more than they want gains.
- When the recommendation also shows negative information (for example a downside), transparency raised adoption for everyone.
- For us: our clients (doctors, lawyers) are often prevention-focused. Show the basis of "741 recommends" (data source, number) and name a downside or trade-off honestly.
- Caveat: hotel and GenAI context, not B2B services. The mechanism (authenticity vs manipulation) should transfer.
