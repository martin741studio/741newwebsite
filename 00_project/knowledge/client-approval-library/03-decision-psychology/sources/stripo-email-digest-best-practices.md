# Email digest: Best practices, examples, and templates that get opened

- URL: https://stripo.email/blog/design-newsletter-blog/
- Author: Zore Mustafaieva (Stripo)
- Published: 2026-09-15 (page date "15 September"; image version timestamp confirms 2026)
- Type: Email-design practitioner guide
- Underlying data named: Optimove 2025 Consumer Marketing Fatigue Report; Litmus (average 8.97 seconds per email); Mailchimp segmentation data; MailerLite benchmark of 3.6 million campaigns

## Key points

- One well-chosen digest reduces inbox fatigue compared with many short emails.
- Optimove 2025: 70% of consumers unsubscribed from at least 3 brands in 3 months because of fatigue.
- Focus on 3 to 5 key items. Go to 8 to 10 only when each item is very short.
- Put one clear main item at the top, then secondary items.
- Use one primary CTA that is more visible than the others. Keep button text to 4 words or fewer.
- Readers spend about 9 seconds on an email. Structure must be scannable at a glance.
- Put the main idea in the first 30 to 40 characters of the subject line.
- Set a minimum-content rule: skip the digest when there is nothing worth sending.
- More than 60% of opens happen on phones. Buttons should be at least 44 px high.
- For us: daily digest = "N items wait for you, 1 due soon", one button to the portal, no empty digests.
