# Anticipated Regret Psychology: How It Shapes Choices

- URL: https://psychologyexposed.com/anticipated-regret-psychology/
- Author: Michael Reed (Psychology Exposed)
- Published: 2026-08-28 (modified 2026-09-02)
- Type: Research summary
- Underlying sources named: review of regret and decision emotions (PMC8718115); research on reversibility and counterfactual thinking (PMC9384371); review of what people regret (PMC2394712); 2023 systematic review and meta-analysis on decision avoidance and post-decision regret (PMC10575496)

## Key points

- Fear of future regret can push people toward action or toward delay. Neither direction is always "safer".
- People often prefer a default, a delay, or someone else's recommendation because it lowers their personal responsibility.
- This feels safer, but it does not make the decision better by itself.
- Reversible choices can lead to more "what if" thinking and lower satisfaction in some settings.
- Regret depends on whether a person can still correct the choice later.
- Tactics against regret: set a decision date, define what new evidence would justify waiting, use a process you can defend even if the result disappoints.
- Break big decisions into small reversible parts.
- Compare two regrets: the regret of acting vs the regret of not acting.
- For us: a clear deadline plus a visible, defensible reason reduces regret. Show that most items can be changed later, but do not invite endless reopening.
