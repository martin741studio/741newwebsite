# Notification Design Psychology: When Alerts Help and When They Annoy

- URL: https://medium.com/@atticusli/notification-design-psychology-when-alerts-help-and-when-they-annoy-1f8be3e0956c
- Author: Atticus (Better Decisions, Medium)
- Published: 2026-08-28
- Type: Behavioral-science practitioner article. Cites interruption research and clinical alert-fatigue findings (no single study named in detail).

## Key points

- Alert fatigue comes from habituation: a repeated signal without real consequence gets ignored.
- Users then build an automatic "dismiss" reflex that also hits the important messages.
- "Notification death spiral": sending more to fix falling engagement makes each message weaker.
- Clinical alert research: when more than 90% of alerts get overridden, clinicians start to override critical ones too.
- People prefer information in batches. One daily digest of five updates feels more useful and less intrusive than five single messages.
- A digest needs one context switch and one judgement, not five.
- A fixed cadence lets users plan for it and gives them a sense of control.
- Use three urgency tiers: immediate (under 5% of all messages), timely, and informational (digest or in-app only).
- Interruption research: about 23 minutes to fully resume a task after an interruption.
- "Trust budget": if more than 80% of messages are dismissed, you spend attention faster than you earn it.
