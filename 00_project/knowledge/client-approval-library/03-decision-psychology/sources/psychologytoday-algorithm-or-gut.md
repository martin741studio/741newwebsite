# Should You Trust the Algorithm or Your Gut?

- URL: https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/how-to-make-better-choices/202609/should-you-trust-the-algorithm-or-your-gut
- Author: James R. Langabeer, PhD, EdD (Psychology Today, "How to Make Better Choices")
- Published: 2026-09-15
- Type: Expert blog summary
- Underlying studies named: Dietvorst, Simmons & Massey 2015 (algorithm aversion, J. Exp. Psych: General); Longoni, Bonezzi & Morewedge 2019 (resistance to medical AI, "uniqueness neglect"); Niszczota & Kaszás 2020 (robo-investment aversion); TD Bank 2026 consumer survey

## Key points

- Algorithm aversion: after one visible error, people distrust an algorithm faster than a human who made the same error.
- Automation bias is the opposite failure: people over-trust output because it looks objective.
- Most people do not calibrate. They pick one mode (trust all or reject all) and keep it.
- Trust drops when the decision feels personal, high-stakes or identity-relevant.
- Health, recruitment and finance are domains with strong resistance.
- "Uniqueness neglect": people believe a system cannot see what makes their case special.
- A recommendation without visible source or reasoning ("black box") pushes people to blanket trust or blanket rejection.
- For us: our clients are doctors, therapists and lawyers. Their practice is personal and identity-relevant. A recommendation must show it was made for their case, by a person, with a visible reason.
- One wrong recommendation can cost more trust than many correct ones earn. Pre-checking matters.
