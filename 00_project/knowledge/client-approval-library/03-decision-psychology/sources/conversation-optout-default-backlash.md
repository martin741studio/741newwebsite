# England made organ donation the default, so why are fewer families saying yes?

- URL: https://theconversation.com/england-made-organ-donation-the-default-so-why-are-fewer-families-saying-yes-292809
- Author: Eamonn Ferguson (University of Nottingham), The Conversation
- Published: 2026-10-09 (page metadata `pubdate`)
- Type: Behavioral-economics analysis of UK donation data (NHS figures)

## Key points

- England moved to a "soft" opt-out: if you do nothing, you have "deemed consent". The family still has the final say.
- Family agreement is 87% when the person actively opted in, but only 46% under deemed consent.
- Overall family consent fell from 69% (2020-21) to 59% (2024-25). Donation fell by 9%.
- Opt-outs rose from 0.6 million (2019) to 2.9 million (2026). Opt-outs grew faster than opt-ins.
- Families say no more often under deemed consent because of anticipated regret: they fear being wrong without a clear signal.
- A default can change how people see the act itself, for example as something taken rather than given.
- "Lone-wolf effect": seeing others opt out pushes people to opt out more than seeing others opt in pulls them in.
- Lesson: a silent default is weaker than an active, recorded choice. People still need a way to say "yes" actively.
- For us: an auto-applied recommendation after 7 days is "deemed consent". Clients may push back later. Give an easy active "yes", log the default clearly, and make the reason visible.
