# Replication of "Procrastination, Deadlines, and Performance: Self-Control by Precommitment"

- URL: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/09567976261460772
- Authors: Kyle Hyndman, Alberto Bisin
- Journal: Psychological Science (issue 37/8, August 2026)
- Published online: 2026-07-15
- Type: Peer-reviewed replication of Ariely & Wertenbroch 2002, Study 2

## Key points

- The original 2002 study claimed that evenly spaced, imposed deadlines beat self-set deadlines and one final deadline.
- This replication did not confirm it. Deadline structure had a negligible effect on errors found, days late and payment.
- Evenly spaced external deadlines did not stand out in reducing procrastination.
- Most people still chose a binding deadline for themselves when allowed (about 89 to 93%).
- The original paper is under scrutiny for data problems (reported by Data Colada; not the subject of this replication).
- For us: do not expect a complex deadline schedule to drive behavior. One clear, visible deadline per item is enough. Keep the 3-day reminder and 7-day default simple.
- Support strength: one replication, moderate sample (about 124 analyzed). Treat as a caution against over-engineering deadlines.
