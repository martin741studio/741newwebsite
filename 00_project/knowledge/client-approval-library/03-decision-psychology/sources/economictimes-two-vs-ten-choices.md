# Psychology says why being given two choices can sometimes feel better than being given ten

- URL: https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/international/us/psychology-says-why-being-given-two-choices-can-sometimes-feel-better-than-being-given-ten/articleshow/133158015.cms
- Author: Aastha Raj (The Economic Times)
- Published: 2026-08-11 (updated 2026-08-13)
- Type: News summary of older peer-reviewed research
- Underlying studies named: Iyengar & Lepper 2000 (jam study, 6 vs 24); Chernev, Böckenholt & Goodman 2015 meta-analysis (99 observations, 7,202 participants)

## Key points

- The 2015 meta-analysis found four moderators: choice-set complexity, task difficulty, preference uncertainty, and decision goal.
- Overload shows up as lower satisfaction or confidence, more regret, delayed choice, and switching later.
- Ten options are not overwhelming for someone who already knows what they want.
- With many options, it is easier to imagine that another choice was better. This raises anticipated regret.
- With two options, the rejected set is small. Regret and mental effort stay low.
- Choice overload can cause deferral: people put the decision off.
- For us: "switching later" maps to change requests. A small, clear set lowers later change requests and the urge to postpone.
