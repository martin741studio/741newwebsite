# Why Agencies Lose Clients In The First 90 Days (And How To Stop It)

## Key points

- The first 90 days carry the highest churn risk. Agencies with formal 30/60/90-day check-ins report lower first-year churn (cites Focus Digital 2026).
- Send a welcome email within 24 hours of signing. Set the channel and request all tool access in it.
- Use a specific onboarding questionnaire: personas, differentiators, business challenges, what success means.
- Tell the client in plain words what happens next, who owns each task, and when they hear from you again. Silence reads as inactivity.
- Kickoff in week 1 maps stakeholders: decision-maker, day-to-day contact, technical people, and content approvers.
- Send a documented success plan within 48 hours of kickoff.
- Week 2: a simple benchmark report, not a long audit.
- Roadmap split into quick wins, mid-term and long-term work, ranked by impact versus effort.
- Days 15-30: weekly updates in one fixed format (done, in progress, next). Send a first "wins report" even before rankings move.
- Day 30/60/90 reviews. Day 90 is a live business review, not only a document.
- A weekly 3-5 minute video update can beat a monthly written report.
- Warning signs of churn: shorter replies, delayed approvals, new faces on calls.
- Spend about 5 minutes per meeting on client education.

## Relevance for 741

- Delayed approvals are an explicit churn signal. The portal should track approval speed per client and flag a slowdown.
