# How Creative Agencies Present a Campaign Concept to a Client (2026)

- Publisher / author: Storyflow (presentation-tool vendor), "Justkay"
- Published: 2026-08-31 (updated 2026-10-10)
- Source type: vendor blog by a practitioner; tool ranking is self-promotional, method part is useful

## Key points

- Present two routes, ranked. One is the recommendation, fully developed with visible reasoning.
- The second route must differ in strategy, not only in decoration, and is shown less developed.
- If both routes look equally polished, you have made two recommendations, not one.
- Keep rejected routes out of the main path. Showing killed work invites the client to bring it back. Store them in a record with the reason.
- End with the one decision you need and the next milestone.
- Approval often happens later, by a person who was not in the meeting. Build a version that explains itself without a presenter.
- "It doesn't feel like us": go back to the insight and show how the idea follows from it. Taste arguments lose.
- "Can we see it another way?": ask which layer fails (strategy, idea or execution). This turns a vague retreat into a scoped revision.
- Say plainly what the client must supply and by when. Hidden client workload stalls approved work.
- Within 2 hours, send a 3-bullet summary: what was shown, the decision asked, the next milestone.
- Follow up with a specific question, not "just checking in".
- Log every objection and answer. Later, the log settles reopened decisions fast.
- Quote: "you have made two recommendations" (when both routes look equal).
