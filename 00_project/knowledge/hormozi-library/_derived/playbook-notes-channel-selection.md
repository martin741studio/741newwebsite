## 1. Which Core Four channel to prioritize first, for a B2B agency

- Primary priority: direct outreach and paid acquisition, not organic content. Cold/warm outreach, direct sales calls, or paid ads should be the primary acquisition engines early on — outreach costs minimal capital and gives immediate, predictable cash flow; paid ads scale predictably if capital is available. (1.2M Followers in 6 Months; 14 Years of Marketing Advice)
- Organic content should NOT be the primary early lever: it needs 12-18 months of sustained investment before it generates substantial pipeline. Treating it as the main lead-generation engine risks starving the business of near-term revenue. (same sources)
- Organic content's real job at this stage is lead nurture and proof, not top-of-funnel generation: when a prospect gets a cold email, a call, or sees a paid ad, they look up the founder's profile and site — organic content's job is to pass that "legitimacy test" (recent activity, real domain knowledge, operational credibility). (1.2M Followers; Guide to Viral Video Hooks)
- Organic content is also a low-risk testing ground: it lets a founder test hooks, headlines, and positioning at zero ad-spend risk, and winning organic angles can be fed directly into cold-outbound sequences or paid-ad copy. This is the explicit bridge between 741's own organic posting (`personal-posts`) and its outreach/ads work.

## 2. Lead Getters: scaling an acquisition channel beyond personal time

- A structured three-phase transition to bring a channel in-house, for a B2B agency that starts by hiring out:
  1. **Pay down ignorance debt (months 1-6):** hire a specialized agency (roughly 3,000-5,000 EUR/month for basic execution, or 15,000-30,000 EUR/month for premium strategy). The goal is not permanent outsourcing — it's forcing baseline consistency and learning the platform's mechanics roughly 5x faster than figuring it out alone.
  2. **Co-creation and SOP documentation:** lock a strict 6-month agreement upfront, require the agency to explain its decision logic, and have an internal team co-create alongside them, turning their workflow into internal SOPs as it goes.
  3. **In-housing and consulting transition:** once internal staff match the agency's expertise, move production fully in-house (an employee gives 100% time allocation and deeper brand context than an outside agency can), and downgrade the agency to a lower-cost strategic-consulting retainer.
- This maps directly onto 741's own position: team-roster capacity (Gayo) and the existing affiliate/partner stream in `marketing-quarter-campaign` are the "Lead Getter" layer already partly built; this framework gives the sequencing logic for when to bring a channel fully in-house versus keep it outsourced.

## 3. Focus and depth-before-width: choosing and committing to ONE channel

- Niche depth over breadth ("small pond king"): resist generic, broad content. Define the problem narrowly enough that "in that tiny pond, you are king" — e.g. a specific operational bottleneck for high-ticket clients, not generic "business tips." Directly actionable for 741: name the specific gap (e.g. local-SEO visibility for a specific vertical), not "marketing advice."
- Choose ONE primary platform where the target decision-makers actually spend time and where the founder naturally understands the native format. Master that single channel's packaging and distribution rules before expanding to a second platform.
- Posting cadence on B2B platforms (LinkedIn named specifically): 3-5x/week. Daily low-effort posting is actively penalized by modern recommendation algorithms, which weight depth, dwell time, and saves over raw frequency.
- Minimum evaluation horizon before judging a channel: at least 60 days for algorithmic topic authority to build on a B2B platform. Don't judge or abandon a channel inside that window.

## 4. Structuring the activity: ownership, volume, and speed

- Role split: the founder owns raw insight, proprietary case studies, and direct "how I" commentary ("here is what we did and the data we gathered"), never preachy "how to" advice. The team/agency owns pre-production packaging, visual formatting (e.g. turning a text post into a carousel), editing, and scheduling.
- Volume benchmark for "done": 3-5 high-depth, save-worthy primary posts per week (e.g. PDF carousels or long-form text) as the core unit of "did the work."
- Speed to engagement: replying to comments within the first hour gives a meaningful visibility boost on algorithmic platforms. On faster conversational platforms (e.g. X), responding within 5-15 minutes triggers early-engagement-velocity weighting that can carry outsized ranking impact in that first window. This is the content-engagement analogue to the inbound-lead speed-to-lead rule already in `_derived/visibility-leads-master-reference.md` (the 60-second/391% figure, there for lead response rather than post engagement).
