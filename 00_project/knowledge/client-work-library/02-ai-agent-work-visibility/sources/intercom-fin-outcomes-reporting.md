## Key points
- Fin counts two kinds of resolution. "Confirmed": the customer says the answer helped. "Assumed": the customer leaves and does not ask for more help.
- An assumed resolution is a guess. A customer who leaves angry can still count as resolved.
- "Escalation" means Fin passed the case to a human. It can come from the customer, from a rule, or from guidance.
- "Procedure handoff" means a planned process ended in a human handoff. Intercom reports it apart from other escalations.
- "Abandoned" means no qualifying answer. A clarifying question with no reply does not count as a resolution.
- If the customer comes back to a resolved case, Intercom removes the resolution. Outcomes can change after the fact.
- A performance funnel shows each path: involved, resolved, handed off, escalated, pending. A user can open the real conversations behind each number.
- The quality score counts only cases that Fin handled with no human.
- Each resolution and each procedure handoff costs a fixed fee. This ties outcome labels to money.

## Gaps / bias notes
- The pages show no exact update date.
- The vendor bills on "assumed" outcomes. The label serves billing as much as truth.
- The pages do not show which source document Fin used for each answer.
