# When Should You Disclose AI Use? The PACED Framework
- URL: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/disclose-ai-paced/
- Type: UX research article (Nielsen Norman Group)
- Published: 2026-09-25 (Taylor Dykes)
- Fetched: 2026-10-13
## Key points
- No single rule decides disclosure. PACED gives five factors: Policy, Audience, Context, Expectations, Degree (role) of AI.
- A disclosure is a human-readable statement about how AI took part. Machine watermarks are not disclosures.
- Labels from third parties drew the strongest negative reactions in the reviewed studies. Proactive disclosure by the creator is the safer default.
- Check legal and platform rules first (for example, EU AI Act cases).
- Content that depends on sincerity, expertise or personal connection gets more scrutiny. Routine information gets milder reactions.
- Creators and readers often disagree about the need for disclosure. Test the expectation with real users.
- More AI involvement makes a stronger case for disclosure. It can also cause a larger negative reaction. This is a trade-off.
- Form: small icons or pills on short items, inline notes or cards on long items. The disclosure must work for screen readers.
## Gaps / bias notes
- The studies cover text content and general audiences. They do not cover agency services or client portals. Use for 741 is an inference.
- No German-market or health-sector data.
