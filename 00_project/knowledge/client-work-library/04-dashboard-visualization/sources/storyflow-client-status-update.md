# How to write a client status update
- URL: https://storyflow.so/blog/how-to-write-a-client-status-update
- Type: Practitioner article (creative-production founder)
- Published: 2026-08-10 (Justkay)
- Fetched: 2026-10-13
## Key points
- The update must answer the client's real question: "Do I need to do something?" It is not a record of team effort.
- The first four lines hold the status word with date, the client action, and what changed. Detail goes below a divider.
- Put "what I need from you" in line two. If nothing is needed, say so.
- Each update is a dated record. Log decisions with the name of the decider and the date.
- Blockers get an owner, a deadline and a consequence. Old blockers stay visible until closed.
- Report a date slip in the first update after it becomes true. Show the date calculation.
- Define green, amber and red once at kickoff. Amber means a named risk with a fix. A false green damages trust.
- Send on a fixed day each week, also when nothing changed.
- Leave out automated task-state dumps and vague notes like "waiting on assets". Keep the detail part under about 200 words.
## Gaps / bias notes
- Written for video and creative projects. One author's practice, no data.
