# Client Portals for Agencies: What Your Client Should Actually See
- URL: https://atwork.so/blog/client-portal-for-agencies
- Type: Vendor blog article (small-agency portal tool)
- Published: 2026-09-01 (byline, Daniel Kroes)
- Fetched: 2026-10-13
## Key points
- A portal is a branded page where the client sees the state of their own work. It is not the full task board.
- The client needs four items: where the work stands (phase, next step, what waits on the client), the current deliverables, a named contact, and a short FAQ.
- Show only the current file. Do not show a thread with many versions.
- The FAQ covers turnaround times, what counts as a revision, and when invoices go out.
- Hide internal estimates and per-task assignees. Also hide margins. Unfinished work stays hidden until it is ready for judgment.
- The portal must be a scoped view of data that the team already keeps. A status change in the source must change the client view.
- If the portal update becomes a separate task, the article says it will be wrong in about three weeks.
- Start with the least complicated retainer client. If that client stops asking "where are things?", add a second client.
- Ask for a sign-in only when the portal holds documents or invoices.
## Gaps / bias notes
- Vendor content, but the advice is tool-neutral.
- No data on client behaviour. The "three weeks" figure is the author's experience.
- No status wording templates.
