### The hidden constraint — LinkedIn accounts, not people

You need 12 usable LinkedIn accounts, not 12 workers. That is the harder part.

**Add seats in waves of 2–3, not all at once.** Confirm the 80 % assumption on
seats 2 and 3 before you hire seats 4 to 12.

## 6. The real bottlenecks

The lead volume is not the constraint. These three are.

**1. Sales capacity.** At Ø 5.000 €, 20 deals per month need about 100 held calls.
That is 4–5 calls per working day, all on Martin today.
Above 30.000 € per month you need a second closer.
Model the closer at 1.500–2.500 € base plus 10 % commission.
A higher deal value fixes this too: fewer, bigger calls beat many small ones.

**2. Delivery capacity.** See section 6a. Do not try to price this bottom-up.

**3. One-off revenue.** The 3.699 € package is a 90-day product.
100.000 € per month in one-off revenue means selling 27 new clients every month, forever.
A monthly retainer after the 90 days changes the whole model.
Track MRR and one-off revenue as two separate lines from now on.

## 6a. Delivery cost — measure it top-down, not per client

Martin is right: with a team behind the work, a true cost per client does not exist.
People work across many clients. Their time does not split cleanly. Stop trying to split it.

**Use the top-down method instead. Three numbers, once per month:**

1. **Total delivery cost** = delivery payroll + subcontractors + all delivery tools.
   One number. No allocation. No guessing.
2. **Active clients** = every client in delivery that month, new and running.
3. **Cost per active client** = 1 ÷ 2.

This gives a fully loaded figure. It is honest, and it takes 5 minutes per month.

### The number that matters more: capacity per person

Cost per client tells you the margin. **Clients per delivery person tells you when to hire.**

Track it: how many active clients can one person carry before quality drops?
For the 90-day local-SEO program, plan 10–15 and correct it with real data.

### Why active clients, not new clients

The 90-day program runs 3 months. So the active base is about 3 × the monthly intake.

| New clients / month | Active clients (steady state) | Delivery people at 12 each |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | 30 | 2,5 |
| 16 | 48 | 4,0 |
| 20 | 60 | 5,0 |

**The load peaks 3 months after the seats ramp, not in the same month.**
Hire delivery ahead of the sales ramp, or month 4 breaks.

## 7. Meta ads — first model (added 2026-10-11, benchmarks only, not real data)

Target offer: the 3.699 € 90-day program. Audience: German SMB, same ICP as the
LinkedIn and email channels. Test budget: 500–1.000 €/month to start.

### Finding: the landing page does not end in a booked call

`04_site/de/pages/local-seo.html` is the page this channel should point to. It carries
a quiz that writes straight to Supabase `advertorial_leads` (see `leadgen-funnel-infra`)
and fires a client-side Meta Pixel `Lead` event. **It does not book a call.** It ends
in: "your personal visibility analysis + video arrives by WhatsApp/email within 2 hours."
A booked call only happens after that video goes out — a manual step today.

This changes the funnel. "1 lead" for this channel = 1 quiz completion, not 1 booked
call. That is actually good news for the tracking gap: leads land in Supabase
regardless of whether Meta's own pixel/CAPI fires, so **CPL can be measured correctly
from day one**, independent of the attribution fix. See `meta-lead-attribution-gap` —
the client-side-only pixel still needs a server-side CAPI event for Meta to *optimise*
delivery toward this Lead event; without it, the campaign will spend toward clicks or
engagement, not toward people who finish the quiz.

**Before spending:** fix the CAPI event (or at minimum, let the campaign optimise on
a Meta pixel custom conversion wired to the same `fbq('track','Lead', …)` call that
already fires). Count the actual CPL from the `advertorial_leads` table, not from
Ads Manager.

### Funnel (updated)

`Ad spend → clicks → quiz lead → booked call → held call → closed deal`

| Step | Rate | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per lead (quiz completion) | 30–60 € | Benchmark, German local-service Meta ads (see sources below) — not 741's own data |
| Lead → booked call | 20 % (assumption) | No data yet — the 2-hour personalised video likely helps this, but unverified |
| Call show rate | 70 % | Reused from the cold-email funnel (section 3) — same closer, same process |
| Call → close | 20 % of held | Reused from the cold-email funnel (section 3) |
| **Lead → closed deal** | **2,8 %** | 0,20 × 0,70 × 0,20 |

### Cost per deal and budget needed

| CPL | Cost per deal | Spend for 1 deal/month | Spend for 2 deals/month (= 1 LinkedIn seat) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 € | ~1.070 € | ~1.070 € | ~2.140 € |
| 45 € (midpoint) | ~1.610 € | ~1.610 € | ~3.210 € |
| 60 € | ~2.140 € | ~2.140 € | ~4.290 € |

At 3.699 € revenue per deal, a 1.070–2.140 € CAC still leaves 71–71 % gross margin
before delivery cost — similar to the LinkedIn seat's 84 %, just a higher CAC per deal.
**The channel can work economically. The open question is the 20 % lead→booked rate**,
which is a guess, not data.

### What the first test budget actually buys

| Monthly spend | Deals/month (CPL 30–60 €) | Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| 500 € | 0,23–0,47 | 850–1.740 € |

## Option C — Retainer model (added 2026-10-10)

Target: companies with 1–10 M € revenue (realistic corridor 3–10 M), **5.000 €/month
retainer**, agency delivery at 1.000 €/client/month, Gayo 5 % commission (12-month tail).

| Per client / month | Amount |
|---|---|
| Retainer | 5.000 € |
| Agency | −1.000 € |
| Gayo commission | −250 € |
| **Contribution** | **3.750 € (75 %)** — 4.000 € (80 %) after the commission tail |

**100 k = 20 active clients, not 27 new deals per month.** LTV 60.000 € vs. 3.699 € (16×).
2 new clients/month = Gayo's contract KPI (2–4 booked calls/week at 20 % close).

| Month | no churn | Ø 12-month lifetime | Ø 6-month lifetime |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | 60.000 € | 48.800 € | 39.900 € |
| 12 | 120.000 € | 77.800 € | 53.300 € |
| 18 | 180.000 € | 94.900 € | 57.700 € |
| steady state | — | **120.000 €** | **60.000 €** |

**Churn is the lever.** 12-month lifetime reaches 100 k in month 19. 6-month lifetime never
does. The first 90 days decide retention — the agency must show visible results.

P&L at 20 active clients: 100.000 − 20.000 agency − 5.000 commission − 1.200 fixed =
**73.800 € (74 %)**. Add an account manager (1.500–2.500 €) from ~12–15 clients → ~72 %.

Open: agency capacity for 20 parallel clients; retainer fit at the 1 M end (6 % of revenue).

## Option D — Website Care retainer (added 2026-10-11)

Martin's idea: sell the current "top notch" website build as a recurring product, not
a one-off. Setup fee **500 €** once. Then a monthly fee. The client talks to an AI chat
window for edits, not to a person. This also solves retention: a client on a monthly
plan does not disappear after the 90-day program ends. Base today: **5 clients**.

### Setup fee — covers onboarding, not profit

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Setup fee (one-time) | 500 € |
| Build cost (4–6 h at the systemised workflow) | ~200 € |
| **Setup margin** | **~300 € (60 %)** |

### Monthly delivery cost per client

| Item | Cost / month |
|---|---|
| Hosting | 8 € |
| AI chat tool | 15 € |
| Minor edits (30 min budget) | 15 € |
| **Total** | **~40 €** |

### Price needed for 100.000 € from this product alone

| Monthly price | Clients needed | Contribution / client |
|---|---|---|
| 297 € | 337 | 257 € (87 %) |
| **397 €** | **252** | **357 € (90 %)** |
| 497 € | 201 | 457 € (92 %) |
| 597 € | 168 | 557 € (93 %) |
| 797 € | 126 | 757 € (95 %) |
| 997 € | 100 | 957 € (96 %) |

**Recommended price: 397 €/month.** It stays far above cheap website-maintenance plans
(20–50 €/month) and signals the "top notch" quality. It also stays well under the
monthly-equivalent of the 3.699 € 90-day program (1.233 €/month), so it reads as the
easy, logical next step for a client who finishes that program.

### P&L at 252 active clients (397 € price)

| Line | Amount / month |
|---|---|
| Revenue (252 × 397 €) | 100.084 € |
| Delivery cost (252 × 40 €) | −10.080 € |
| **Contribution** | **~90.000 € (90 %)** |

### The real question: how fast can the base grow

252 is a client count, not a deal count. The fastest feeder is the existing funnel, not
a new sales motion:

1. **Program graduates.** Offer the Care retainer to every client finishing the
   3.699 € 90-day program. At a 50 % take rate on the 27 deals/month target, that is
   ~13–14 new Care clients per month, with no extra CAC.
2. **Referral discount.** Give the referring client 1 free month (cost: ~40 €, the
   delivery cost) for every new Care client they bring in. Cheap, because the margin
   per client is already 90 %.

### Churn is the lever again (same pattern as Option C)

Steady-state active clients = new clients per month ÷ monthly churn rate.

| New clients / month | Churn 4 %/month | Churn 6 %/month |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | 250 clients → 99.300 € | 167 clients → 66.200 € |
| 14 | 350 clients → 139.000 € | 233 clients → 92.500 € |
| 20 | 500 clients → 198.500 € | 333 clients → 132.200 € |

At 14 new/month (the program-graduate rate above), **4 % monthly churn clears 100 k**;
6 % churn does not. Website-care plans usually churn slower than lead-gen retainers
(the product is passive, the client forgets it is a cost), but this needs real data
after the first 3 months.

**Open:** real build-cost and support-time data (the 200 €/40 € figures are estimates);
whether one support person, backed by the AI chat, can really hold 250+ clients;
whether 397 €/month undersells a "top notch" site once the Care base gets real volume.
