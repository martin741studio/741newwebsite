# Retention and Platform Specs for Short-Form and Long-Form Video

Research date: 2026-10-27. Scope: hook, pacing, captions, safe zones, loudness, export specs, transitions, colour, long-form structure, and platform ranking statements.

## How to read this file

- "Recent" means published after 2025-10-27. Older items carry the label OLD.
- "Undated" means the fetched page showed no date. The fetch date is 2026-10-27.
- "[snippet]" means the claim came from a search result summary. The page was not opened.
- "[estimate]" means a number from a secondary source. Do not treat it as a platform rule.
- "[vendor]" means a tool or agency that sells to creators. Treat its claims as marketing.
- Official pages that did not load are listed under Gaps.

## 1. Hook (first 1-3 seconds)

- Viewers decide to stay or leave within about 1 to 2 seconds. No platform gives this figure. (Source: https://aibrify.com/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-curve-playbook, 2026 page title, third-party [snippet])
- YouTube Studio's audience retention report shows an "Intro" value. Intro is the share of viewers still watching at 30 seconds. (Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en, undated, fetched 2026-10-27)
- The same YouTube page advises: "Modify the first 30 seconds of your video and experiment with different styles." (Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en, undated, fetched 2026-10-27)
- YouTube Shorts analytics show "Viewed vs. swiped away." YouTube publishes no official benchmark for it. (Source: https://growcreator.pro/blog/viewed-vs-swiped-away-youtube-shorts, undated [snippet])
- The YouTube community post on this metric did not load. Its body is unread. (Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/community-video/273390203/new-youtube-shorts-metric-viewed-vs-swiped-away?hl=en, undated, fetched 2026-10-27, body not rendered)
- A third-party guide treats 70% or more as a strong opening. It treats under 30% as a weak opening. These are estimates. (Source: https://growcreator.pro/blog/viewed-vs-swiped-away-youtube-shorts, undated [snippet, estimate])
- YouTube does not document whether this metric affects ranking. (Source: https://growcreator.pro/blog/viewed-vs-swiped-away-youtube-shorts, undated [snippet])
- TikTok names three groups of ranking input: user interactions, video information, and device and account settings. (Source: https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you, 2020-06-18, OLD, fetched 2026-10-27)
- The TikTok post gives one example of a strong interest signal. The viewer finishes a longer video. (Source: https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you, 2020-06-18, OLD, fetched 2026-10-27)
- The TikTok post states that follower count is not a direct factor. (Source: https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you, 2020-06-18, OLD, fetched 2026-10-27)
- The fetched TikTok text names no hook signal and no swipe-away signal. (Source: https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you, 2020-06-18, OLD, fetched 2026-10-27)
- Adam Mosseri named watch time, likes per reach, and sends per reach as Reels signals in January 2025. The primary post was not found. (Source: secondary coverage, https://creatorlanehq.com/blog/instagram-sends-per-reach-2026, January 2025 per coverage, OLD [snippet])
- Per secondary coverage, likes matter slightly more for connected content. Sends matter slightly more for unconnected content. (Source: https://creatorlanehq.com/blog/instagram-sends-per-reach-2026, January 2025 per coverage, OLD [snippet])
- Meta has not published a numeric weight for sends. The "3 to 5 likes" figure is unofficial. (Source: https://socialday.live/features/instagrams-dm-share-signal-now-drives-3-to-5-times-more-reach-than-likes, undated [snippet, estimate])
- Third-party guides suggest a hook of 5 to 10 seconds for long-form videos. (Source: https://www.creator-playbook.com/articles/your-intro-sucks-fix-first-30-seconds, undated [snippet])
- Third-party guides name hook types, such as visual hooks and open loops. No platform source defines these types. (Source: https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-script-writing/, undated [snippet])
- Third-party guides advise opening on the most visually striking frame. They advise cutting intros and logos. Greetings should also go. No platform source confirms this. (Source: https://aibrify.com/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-curve-playbook, 2026 [snippet])
- A 2026 third-party benchmark puts Shorts under 60 seconds at 40% to 55% average retention. This is an estimate. (Source: https://retensis.com/blog/audience-retention-benchmarks-2026, 2026 [snippet, estimate])
- Third-party guides name 15 to 45 seconds as the best Short length. This is a heuristic. (Source: https://www.opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention, undated [snippet])

## 2. Cut rate and pacing

- No study measured average shot length for TikTok, Reels, or Shorts. The 2026-10-27 searches found none. (Source: search of 2026-10-27, no primary study found)
- A 2026 roundup reports average TikTok video length at 42.7 seconds, up from 39 seconds in 2023. This is a video length, not a shot length. (Source: https://vidico.com/news/short-form-video-statistics/, 2026, undated [snippet, estimate])
- One agency benchmark reports organic Reels peak at 45 to 60 seconds and TikTok at 15 to 30 seconds. Its platform guidance dates from 2021. (Source: https://inbeat.agency/blog/ugc-video-length-benchmark, 2026 [snippet, estimate, source attribution not confirmed])
- A third-party guide suggests a visual change every 2 to 3 seconds, or every 3 to 5 seconds. It calls 3 to 5 seconds a test start. (Source: https://clip-forge.io/blog/vertical-video-retention-editing-playbook-2026, 2026 [snippet, estimate])
- A jump cut is a continuous shot with a span of time removed. It leaves a visible break in the action. (Source: https://artlist.io/blog/editing-techniques, undated [snippet])
- No source gave a timing rule for punch-ins. Punch-in timing is editor practice and is not sourced here.
- No source gave a timing rule for jump cuts in talking-head video. This gap remains open.

## 3. Pattern interrupts

- Third-party guides name four interrupt types: sudden zoom, sound effect, b-roll insert, and on-screen text. (Source: https://shortzly.com/blog/short-form-video-retention-strategies, undated [snippet])
- One guide describes a sudden zoom as a 200% crop on a face or key element. It lasts 2 to 3 seconds. (Source: https://shortzly.com/blog/short-form-video-retention-strategies, undated [snippet])
- One guide suggests one deliberate interrupt every 10 to 15 seconds in clips over 30 seconds. (Source: https://shortzly.com/blog/short-form-video-retention-strategies, undated [snippet, estimate])
- Another guide suggests a new visual, sound, pace, or question every 8 to 12 seconds. (Source: https://www.gofaceless.ai/en/blog/optimize-video-engagement-pattern-breaks, undated [snippet, estimate])
- Claims of "40% to 60% higher retention" come from one vendor's internal analysis. The method is not disclosed. Do not use them. (Source: https://clip-forge.io/blog/vertical-video-retention-editing-playbook-2026, 2026 [snippet, vendor, unverified])
- No source gave frequency rules for camera-angle changes or speed ramps.

## 4. Caption conventions (2025 to 2026)

- No peer-reviewed study in 2026 compares word-by-word and phrase captions. (Source: search of 2026-10-27, no primary study found)
- One vendor suggests three to five words at a time for educational content. (Source: https://blitzcutai.com/blog/best-caption-style-tiktok, undated [snippet, vendor])
- One vendor reports an 11% completion lift for word-by-word over uniform burned-in captions. The vendor does not disclose its method. (Source: https://tapescribe.com/blog/video-captions-best-practices-2026, 2026 [snippet, vendor, estimate])
- The same vendor advises highlighting key words with a colour shift, a scale change, or bold. Timing follows the speech. (Source: https://tapescribe.com/blog/video-captions-best-practices-2026, 2026 [snippet, vendor])
- One vendor says caption presence matters more than the exact style. (Source: https://blitzcutai.com/blog/best-caption-style-youtube-shorts-2026, 2026 [snippet, vendor])
- For TikTok, one vendor recommends white text with a black outline, or yellow text with a black outline. (Source: https://blitzcutai.com/blog/best-caption-style-tiktok, undated [snippet, vendor])
- For YouTube Shorts, one vendor recommends white as the default and yellow for high-energy content. It recommends a 2 to 4 px black stroke. (Source: https://blitzcutai.com/blog/best-caption-style-youtube-shorts-2026, 2026 [snippet, vendor])
- No source gave a caption font size relative to frame height.
- No source names the font families used by top creators. Montserrat and Inter are not verified here. TikTok Sans is also not verified.
- No source gave a maximum words-per-line value.
- Caption safe positions follow the Safe Zones section below.
- Accessibility guidance was not researched. The platform caption help pages did not load.

## 5. Safe zones

- The official Reels size page did not render. No verified overlay values for Instagram come from this file. (Source: https://help.instagram.com/1038071743007909/?helpref=uf_share, undated, fetched 2026-10-27, page not rendered)
- Meta ad guidance, as quoted by third parties, keeps text and logos out of the top 14%, the bottom 35%, and 6% per side. (Source: https://www.biddyco.com/blog-posts/meta-ad-specs, 2026 [snippet, secondary])

## 7. Export specs

### Instagram Reels

- Aspect ratio range: 1.91:1 to 9:16. Minimum 720 px and 30 fps. (Source: https://help.instagram.com/1038071743007909/?helpref=uf_share, undated [snippet of help page])
- Maximum length: 3 minutes, since January 2025. The earlier cap was 90 seconds. (Source: https://www.threads.com/@mosseri/post/DE-efFqyStv?hl=en, 2025-01-18, OLD [snippet])
- Recommended frame: 1080 x 1920 at 9:16, H.264, MP4 or MOV. (Source: https://sproutsocial.com/insights/social-media-video-specs-guide/, undated [snippet, secondary])
- File size: sources conflict (1 GB or 4 GB). Check in the app. (Source: https://sproutsocial.com/insights/social-media-video-specs-guide/, undated [snippet, secondary])

### TikTok

- Content Posting API, last updated 2026-08-04, fetched 2026-10-27:
  - Container: MP4 recommended. WebM and MOV accepted.
  - Codec: H.264 preferred. H.265, VP8, and VP9 accepted.
  - Frame rate: 23 to 60 fps.
  - Width and height: each 360 to 4096 px.
  - Maximum file size: 4 GB.
  - Length: most accounts up to 3 minutes. Some accounts 5 or 10 minutes.
  - The initialize endpoint accepts up to 10 minutes.
  (Source: https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/content-posting-api-media-transfer-guide/, 2026-08-04)
- In-app length: up to 10 minutes for most accounts, and up to 60 minutes by file upload for some. (Source: https://www.skedsocial.com/blog/tiktok-video-size-guide, 2026 [snippet, secondary, conflicts with API limit])
- Recommended frame: 1080 x 1920 at 9:16. (Source: https://skedsocial.com/blog/tiktok-video-size-guide, 2026 [snippet, secondary])
- In-Feed ad cap: 500 MB. (Source: https://skedsocial.com/blog/tiktok-video-size-guide, 2026 [snippet, secondary])

### YouTube Shorts

- Length: up to 3 minutes for vertical or square video. The official FAQ dates the change to a rollout from 2024-10-15. (Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/community-guide/302729680/faq-longer-shorts?hl=en, 2024, OLD; body not rendered, claim from search snippet)
- Third-party 2026 guides say the in-app camera now records up to 3 minutes. The official page does not confirm this. (Source: https://likefy.com/en/how-long-can-a-youtube-short-be/, May 2026 per page title [snippet, secondary])
- Desktop upload: vertical or square, 3 minutes or less. (Source: https://likefy.com/en/how-long-can-a-youtube-short-be/, May 2026 per page title [snippet, secondary])
- Encoding: use the YouTube long-form values below. The official encoding page has no Shorts-specific note.

### YouTube long-form (official encoding page)

Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171?hl=en, footer shows 2026, fetched 2026-10-27.

- Container: MP4, with fast start (moov atom at the front).
- Video codec: H.264, High profile, progressive, 4:2:0, CABAC, 2 consecutive B-frames, closed GOP of half the frame rate, variable bitrate.
- Frame rate: the same as the recording. Common rates: 24, 25, 30, 48, 50, and 60 fps.
- Audio: AAC-LC or Opus. 48 kHz. Stereo, stereo plus 5.1, or stereo plus Eclipsa Audio.
- Audio bitrate: mono 128 kbps, stereo 384 kbps, 5.1 512 kbps.
- Video bitrate (Mbps):

| Resolution | SDR 24/25/30 fps | SDR 48/50/60 fps | HDR 24/25/30 fps | HDR 48/50/60 fps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2160p (4K) | 35-45 | 53-68 | 44-56 | 66-85 |
| 1440p (2K) | 16 | 24 | 20 | 30 |
| 1080p | 8 | 12 | 10 | 15 |

## 8. Transition taxonomy

- J-cut: the next scene's sound starts before its picture. L-cut: the previous scene's sound continues after the picture changes. (Source: https://www.adobe.com/eg_en/creativecloud/video/discover/j-cut-and-l-cut.html, undated [snippet])
- Adobe states that an L-cut can establish the setting of a new scene. (Source: https://www.adobe.com/eg_en/creativecloud/video/discover/j-cut-and-l-cut.html, undated [snippet])
- Jump cut: removes time from a continuous shot. It signals urgency or disorientation. (Source: https://artlist.io/blog/editing-techniques, undated [snippet])
- Match cut: links two shots through shared shape, colour, movement, or idea. (Source: https://artlist.io/blog/editing-techniques, undated [snippet])
- Punch-in (digital zoom): a crop that tightens the frame. Third-party guides use it as a pattern interrupt. (Source: https://shortzly.com/blog/short-form-video-retention-strategies, undated [snippet])
- Hard cut, whip pan, zoom transition, dissolve or crossfade, dip to black or white, speed ramp, mask transition, and glitch: no source was fetched. Their usage is editor practice and is not sourced here.

## 9. Colour look conventions

- Teal and orange suits skin tones. Skin sits in the orange range, so teal in the shadows separates faces from the background. (Source: https://blog.motionarray.com/learn/video-effects/orange-and-teal-lut/, undated [snippet])
- Skin protection method: isolate skin with an HSL secondary, invert the mask, then move the background toward teal. This tutorial uses Lumetri. (Source: https://blog.motionarray.com/learn/video-effects/orange-and-teal-lut/, undated [snippet])
- One LUT seller claims that orange or teal presets can push skin toward unnatural orange. This is a vendor claim. (Source: https://www.sparklestock.com/?p=298817, undated [snippet, vendor])
- A LUT vendor advises applying the same LUT to stills and b-roll for a matched look. This is vendor advice, not a standard. (Source: https://magichour.ai/products/color-grader/teal-and-orange, undated [snippet, vendor])
- No source covered "clean and bright" or "desaturated and moody" looks.

## 10. Long-form retention (YouTube, 8 to 20 minutes)

- The YouTube retention curve has four shapes: flat (watched to the end), gradual decline (normal), spikes (rewatch or share), and dips (abandon or skip). (Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en, undated, fetched 2026-10-27)
- YouTube advises that top moments late in a video suggest that strong content should move earlier. (Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en, undated, fetched 2026-10-27)
- Highlights (intro, spikes, dips, top moments) appear only for videos of at least 60 seconds with at least 100 views. (Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en, undated, fetched 2026-10-27)
- A third-party guide quotes YouTube's Creator Academy: high retention and watch time may increase Search and Suggested placement. The official Creator Academy page was not opened. (Source: https://creatoracademy.youtube.com/, not opened; quoted in a third-party search snippet)
- A third-party guide advises a 70% intro retention as solid and 80% as exceptional. These are estimates. (Source: https://www.lenostube.com/en/youtube-audience-retention-average-good-and-best-benchmarks/, 2026 [snippet, estimate])
- A third-party guide advises a teaser for the next section every 2 to 3 minutes. It advises placing the strongest moment about two-thirds through. (Source: https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-hooks-and-retention/, undated [snippet])
- No source covered re-hooks at a fixed 60 to 90 second interval. This value is not verified.
- No source covered chapters or end screens. This gap remains open.

## What this means for an editor

1. Put the strongest visual or question in the first 1 to 2 seconds of each video.
2. Export Reels and Shorts at 1080 x 1920, 9:16, H.264, MP4, at the recording frame rate.
3. Export YouTube long-form as H.264 MP4 with AAC audio. Use 8 Mbps at 1080p for 24 to 30 fps, and 12 Mbps for 48 to 60 fps.
4. Keep captions out of the bottom 350 to 400 px and the right 120 px on Shorts. Keep faces clear of the same areas.
5. Test the TikTok bottom safe value (250 px against 480 px) on a real upload before setting a template.
6. Treat a visual change every 3 to 5 seconds as a test start, then tune against the retention curve.
7. Use word-by-word captions with the active word highlighted. Use white text with a black outline.
8. Target -14 LUFS for YouTube only after verifying it. Keep true peak at -1 dBTP or lower as a safe ceiling.
9. Place the open loop before 30 seconds. Place the best moment around two-thirds of the way through long-form videos.
10. Protect skin tones when you apply a teal-orange look. Apply the same grade to b-roll and check it on a phone.
