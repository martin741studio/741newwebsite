## Raw dictation

"What really helps." We can take content from Hamusi and put it into exercises and tasks that we're using to improve ranking, right? There is also some truth and in general there is just not enough education. The easy things to execute on are just to improve. Topics are also around execution, tasks, routine, conditioning, long-term thinking, and being able to execute over months by distributing responsibility. A lot of the company thinks they can do a lot by themselves now. They think AI takes out existing infrastructures and the execution of workers for sure. ## Core argument extracted

Accepted narrative: AI makes agencies unnecessary — companies believe they can now do the execution themselves.

Contradiction: things genuinely are getting easier, but "easier tools" is not the same as "less real-world execution needed." The gap was never access to information or content — there is already not enough education, but the actually hard part is sustained execution: routine, conditioning, long-term distributed responsibility, month over month.

Real-world evidence: Martin sees companies try the DIY route and then come back to agencies anyway.

Takeaway: the real value of an agency isn't gatekeeping content or tactics — it's turning content into exercises/tasks that get executed consistently over time. "What really helps" is execution infrastructure, not access to more information.
