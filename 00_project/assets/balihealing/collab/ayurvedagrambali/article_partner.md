## The Physiology of Detoxification: Ancient Concept, Modern Translation

In classical Ayurvedic texts, the accumulation of toxic metabolic residue is referred to as *Ama*. When digestive fire (*Agni*) is weakened, undigested nutrients ferment in the gastrointestinal tract and circulate through subtle bodily channels (*Srotas*), creating heaviness, brain fog, and systemic fatigue.

Modern functional medicine describes a remarkably similar phenomenon:
- **Phase I Liver Detoxification:** The enzymatic oxidation of lipid-soluble toxins into intermediate compounds via cytochrome P450 pathways.
- **Phase II Liver Conjugation:** The binding of these reactive intermediate compounds with sulfur amino acids, glutathione, or glycine so they can be safely excreted through bile and urine.
- **Cellular Hydration & Lymphatic Flow:** The fluid dynamics required to transport metabolic waste out of interstitial spaces and through the lymphatic system.

Both traditions emphasize that detoxification requires energy and essential resources. While herbal decoctions and therapeutic massages loosen accumulated waste, the physical body must be sufficiently hydrated and nourished at the cellular level to process and eliminate these substances without causing severe detox fatigue or electrolyte depletion.
