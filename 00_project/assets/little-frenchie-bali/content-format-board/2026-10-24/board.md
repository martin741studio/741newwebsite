## 5. Format ideas

### Idea 1: Single-Scene Hook, phone version
- **Idea:** One steak frites plate, one take, one line. The plate shows the French food that people search for.
- **Opening line (first 1–3 s):** "Steak frites in Pererenan. Not what most people expect in Bali."
- **Opening image:** A plate of steak frites lands on the table. The camera stays still on the plate.
- **Second sentence (proof line):** "A French bistro dish, cooked here, with the fries on the side."
- **How to shoot:** One phone at table height, one take, no cuts. A hand sets the plate down. The knife cuts the steak. Props: the plate and one glass. The owner confirms the dish is on the current menu.
- **Length:** 20–25 seconds, under 60 seconds at most.
- **Ending:** End on the first cut of the steak. The last line repeats the opening words, "Steak frites in Pererenan."
- **Quality bar:** Passes: the real plate, the real table, the real light, and a first line that lands in 3 seconds. Fails: stock footage, a logo intro, a "welcome" line, or a polished ad look.

### Idea 2: Fast-Cut Story, visuals only
- **Idea:** A short brunch clip with no speech. Fast cuts show the table, the coffee and the eggs benedict. One Tripadvisor reviewer calls the eggs benedict the best they have had.
- **Opening line (first 1–3 s):** "Brunch in Pererenan, and the yolk is already running."
- **Opening image:** A knife cuts into the eggs benedict. The yolk runs over the plate.
- **Second sentence (proof line):** "The clip shows coffee and sunlight on the garden table."
- **How to shoot:** One phone on a tripod or a steady hand, three to five angles of the same table. Cut on each action: pour, cut, sip, sunlight across the plate. Sound: the room only. Props: the egg dish, one coffee, one pastry. The owner confirms the eggs benedict is on the current menu.
- **Length:** 20–25 seconds.
- **Ending:** End on the last sip or the last bite. The final frame matches the opening yolk shot.
- **Quality bar:** Passes: a clear first second, a cut on each action, real food on a real table. Fails: a slow pan over empty tables, a title card with no picture, or a voice-over that explains the menu.

### Idea 3: Talk-to-Camera, phone version
- **Idea:** The owner answers one question on camera: "French food in Bali, what should I order first?" This idea needs a speaker. OPEN QUESTION: ask the client.
- **Opening line (first 1–3 s):** "French food in Bali. This is what I would order first."
- **Opening image:** The owner, phone in hand, at the bar or a table, face to the camera.
- **Second sentence (proof line):** The owner names one dish and one reason. Only facts the owner can confirm.
- **How to shoot:** One phone, one person, the restaurant room behind. Keep the natural pauses. Props: one plate and one glass.
- **Length:** 20–25 seconds.
- **Ending:** End on the dish. The last line repeats the question from the opening.
- **Quality bar:** Passes: the owner speaks in plain words, uses their own words, and names one dish with one reason. Fails: a script read aloud, a claim the owner cannot back, or a polished intro.

### Idea 4: Why-We-Do-It Educational, one French method
- **Idea:** The owner explains one French method behind the steak frites. This answers the search "French restaurant Bali". The method is stated by the owner. OPEN QUESTION: ask the client: which step does the kitchen use?
- **Opening line (first 1–3 s):** "Steak frites has two ingredients. Here is the step that decides it."
- **Opening image:** The owner holds a raw potato in one hand and a steak on the board in the other.
- **Second sentence (proof line):** The owner names the step and says why it matters, in one sentence.
- **How to shoot:** One phone at the prep table, one person speaking, hands in frame. Two cuts at most: the step, then the finished plate. Props: one potato, one fry, the plate.
- **Length:** 20–25 seconds.
- **Ending:** End on the finished plate. The last line repeats the opening sentence about two ingredients.
- **Quality bar:** Passes: the owner shows the real step in the real kitchen and names it in plain words. Fails: a step the owner does not do, a claim about other restaurants, or a recipe list.

### Idea 5: Single-Scene Hook, moment version
- **Idea:** A sunset wine moment at the garden table. One scene, one story, and the place is the hook.
- **Opening line (first 1–3 s):** "Sunset wine in Pererenan, from the garden table."
- **Opening image:** A glass of wine on a garden table, the sun low behind it. A hand lifts the glass.
- **Second sentence (proof line):** "A glass, a table, and the garden as the light fades."
- **How to shoot:** One phone, one locked shot at table height. A guest lifts the glass once. No second action and no cut. Sound: the room and the garden. Props: a glass of wine and a candle.
- **Length:** 20–25 seconds.
- **Ending:** End on the sip, as the sun drops out of frame. The last frame matches the first frame.
- **Quality bar:** Passes: real light at sunset, the real garden, and a clear first second. Fails: a studio lamp, a stock sunset, or a scene from another venue. The owner confirms wine is served in the garden.

### Idea 6: Fast-Cut Story, with context line
- **Idea:** A short clip for guests who search for Canggu but want a calmer place. The context line places the restaurant in Pererenan, five minutes from Canggu.
- **Opening line (first 1–3 s):** "Canggu is busy. Pererenan is five minutes away."
- **Opening image:** A busy road in Canggu, then a quick cut to the calm front door of the restaurant.
- **Second sentence (proof line):** "Little Frenchie, Jl. Munduk Kedungu No.39, Pererenan."
- **How to shoot:** One phone, three or four angles: the road, the door, the sign, one plate. The context line appears on screen: "Pererenan, five minutes from Canggu."
- **Length:** 20–25 seconds.
- **Ending:** End on the door. The last shot is the busy road, from the same angle as the opening.
- **Quality bar:** Passes: the first second shows the difference, the context line is correct, and the address is exact. The owner checks the drive time. Fails: a clip from the wrong street, a map animation, or a claim about traffic.

## 6. How to run it
- Cadence: Proposal: three to five short videos a week on Instagram Reels first. Add TikTok and YouTube Shorts later, once one idea works.
- Testing: Make four to six opening-line variants of the same video. Judge after 24 to 48 hours on how many viewers watch past 3 seconds. Drop any variant under 20 to 25 percent.
- Avoid: links in the caption text, generic AI captions, more than a few hashtags, outros, "thanks for watching", and any promise about views.
