# Format taxonomy — the 4 proven short-video formats (internal reference)

Source: `00_project/knowledge/content-format-library/001-you-only-need-4-content-formats-alex-garcia.md` (video published 2026-06-24, pulled 2026-10-24). Internal only — the creator's name, "Cut 30", and the format names below never appear in a client-facing board. Use `label-map.md` for the client-facing labels.

## Pre-step: content level (readiness check) — not a format
Three questions the client answers before any tier is chosen:
1. Do operations break if video volume doubles in the next 30 days?
2. Does quality drop if volume doubles in the next 30 days?
3. Does the team's skill set match the tier they aim to produce in 30 days?
Rule: 2 of 3 answered "fine" → the client can move up a level now. 1 of 3 → spend 30–60 days building infrastructure first. Advice from the source: execute optimistically, strategize pessimistically — one or two hurdles can collapse a plan built on everything going right.

## Format 1 — One-Shot (one scene, one story, one shot)
Role: top-of-funnel reach, middle-of-funnel education, or product videos. The easiest format to execute.
Rule: a great One-Shot = a **title hook** (on-screen text) + a **visual hook** (the single-scene story).
| Level | Name | What it is | What it needs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Lo-fi single scene | One phone shot + text overlay (e.g. founder on a bike, an "unpopular opinion" text that pays off in the second line) | A title hook and a visual hook, nothing else |
| 2 | Hi-fi single scene | Same idea, cinematic: sound design, color grading, composition (e.g. a runner tracked on a gimbal) | A camera operator, basic grading, sound |
| 3 | Vignette | A short moment that transports the viewer into the brand's world; optimize the angle, the aesthetic, or the action (e.g. a friend arriving by bike, coffee already waiting, a payoff handshake) | A scene worth watching, composition, a payoff |
| 4 | Vignette with dual stimulation | One scene, two stories: a second action in the foreground, midground, or background (e.g. a model posing while a customer queue loops behind) | Blocking two actions in one frame |

## Format 2 — Ten-Shot (a short, snappy story through fast, varied shots)
Role: high-retention visual showcase.
| Level | Name | What it is | What it needs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | No text | Pure visuals: ~4 shots in the first 1–1.5 s, angle changes, contrast, constant motion; one well-chosen sound carries it | A sound that fits, visually pleasing shots |
| 2 | Text overlay | Text sets the context for what the visuals then show (e.g. "This is your sign to get X for the summer"); tripod, a few angles, smooth cuts | One context line, 3–5 angles, clean cuts |
| 3 | Narration | A script (or a sound that matches the story) + a shot list/storyboard matched line by line to the script | Pre-production: write the story, then match shots to it |

## Format 3 — Yap (talk to camera, feels like two friends talking)
Three things separate a good from a great talker: **storytelling** (most important — a good storyteller wins even with no personality), **presence and personality** (how you show up on camera), **packaging** (how an idea is wrapped so people want it).
| Level | Name | What it is | What it needs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Raw | Phone propped up, talk straight to camera | Composition, lighting, a title hook — storytelling wins here |
| 2 | Graphic | Visuals added only where a visual says it better than words ("visual amplifiers" for one sentence or point) — not a green-screen video | A few custom graphics, used sparingly |
| 3 | Prop | The talk is built around a physical prop: a poster board with numbers revealed, a TV screen mirroring visuals, a bulletin board, a notebook | One prop that drives the story |
| 4 | Cinematic | Shot cinematically, own color grade, props like an easel or whiteboard, B-roll overlays | Camera, lighting, grading, B-roll |

## Format 4 — How-we-do-it / Why-we-do-it (educational talking head with a differentiator)
Not generic category education: category education **on top of something unique** (positioning, differentiation, unique experience, or a contrarian take).
Sourcing rule from the source: collect all DMs, story replies, FAQs, and the comments on competitors' content; use a public-questions tool for the category and the competitors; then answer every question through the brand's own positioning. Example named in the source: a bakery explaining why bakers don't use chocolate bars.

## Mapping notes for the board
- Readiness level decides the ceiling: a client at level "1 of 3" gets Lo-fi One-Shot, Raw Yap, and How-we-do-it first; Ten-Shot narration and Cinematic Yap are later tiers, not starting points.
- The on-camera person belongs to Yap and How-we-do-it. One-Shot and Ten-Shot can be made without anyone speaking — the fallback when nobody at the client wants to be on camera.
- Every format idea still follows the hook/retention/loop/length rules in `playbook-notes.md` §A–D.
