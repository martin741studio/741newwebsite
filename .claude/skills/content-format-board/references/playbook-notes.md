## A. Hook (first 1–3 s)
- Attention window per platform: Instagram Reels — visual pattern interrupt within 1.0 s, value promise by 3.0 s. TikTok 1.5 s. YouTube Shorts 2.0 s. Over 70 % of viewers decide in the first 3 s. (Deep Research Report; AdMove AI hooks guide)
- Pair a visual pattern interrupt (sudden movement, mid-action start, unexpected framing) with a high-arousal verbal line. (ReelForge; UGC Copilot)
- Numerical specificity in the hook ("how we fixed X in 14 days") — the notebook cites 2.4× the 3-second hold vs a vague claim. (14 Years of Marketing Advice)
- Negative/contrarian framing ("Stop doing X", "The #1 mistake…") — cited 1.3–1.8× higher hook rate vs positive framing. (Deep Research Report)
- Speak the core service/topic keywords in the first 3–5 s so the platform transcribes and indexes the video for search/AEO. (Deep Research Report; Social Media Trends 2026)
- Zero-delay entry: no intro, no logo animation, no "hey guys" — start at the peak problem or proof. (YouTube script guide)

## B. Body and retention
- Retention bridge: one sentence right after the hook that validates the open loop and gives immediate proof, no setup talk. (YouTube script guide)
- Change something visual or auditory every 3–5 s (angle cut, text overlay, B-roll, pitch shift). No speech block longer than 8 s without a visual change. (Deep Research Report; Socialync)
- Jump-cut through setup; slow down only at the key value or transformation reveal. (Socialync)
- "How we" over "how to": frame around real operational experience ("here is how we solved this for a patient/client"), never abstract advice — the latter reads as preaching and provokes skepticism. (Hormozi content strategy; 1.2M Followers)

## C. Ending and loop
- End the exact moment the payoff lands. No summary slide, no outro, no "thanks for watching" — these cause a retention cliff that hurts distribution. (Deep Research Report; Virvid looping)
- Seamless loop: write the last line so it flows syntactically and visually back into the opening (same angle, same position, verbal callback). Every replay counts as a view; completion rates past 100 % trigger wider distribution. (Virvid; 1.2M Followers)

## D. Length and compression
- 20–25 s is the retention sweet spot (cited ~42 %+ completion) and the easiest length to engineer into a loop. Short-form discovery ceiling: under 60 s (under 90 s on LinkedIn, captions mandatory there). (Virvid; Deep Research Report)
- One video = one core idea, no secondary tangents. Trim every beat that does not deliver value, build to the payoff, or hold curiosity. (YouTube script guide; Socialync)

## E. What "quality" means per these sources
- Platforms rank on a depth score (dwell time, saves, substantive comments), not view counts. Saves cited at 5× a like's weight; 10+-word comments at 15× a like. 30 s+ of dwell unlocks wider distribution. (Deep Research Report; LinkedIn 2026 reports)
- "Language > razzle": clear messaging and proprietary knowledge beat camera gear, color grading and effects, which distract from educational retention. Real settings and human imperfection beat polished ads and generic AI copy. (agence Eminence 2027 predictions; Social Media Marketing 2027)
- For a local service business, quality = vertical, niche-specific videos that attract ideal paying local clients, not broad viral view counts. (How to become rich with social media)

## F. Where the real audience questions come from (process question 1 + 2)
- Direct touchpoints: DMs, story replies, customer-service FAQs, questions asked on sales calls. DMs signal buyer intent; sales-call questions reveal the exact objections blocking a sale. (Alex Garcia 4 Formats; Hormozi)
- Competitor comment sections and discussions — unaddressed objections, complaints, market gaps. (Hormozi)
- Query tools: Answer The Public (category questions around competitors across TikTok/IG/YouTube); Answer Socrates (Google search questions, autocomplete, social question clusters before volume peaks); in-app search insights (e.g. TikTok Creator Search Insights). (Alex Garcia; Answer Socrates; Deep Research Report)
- Forum mining: BuzzSumo Question Analyzer across forums, Reddit, Quora, Q&A sites; comment clusters and niche message boards. (BuzzSumo)
- Trend prediction: Exploding Topics and Glimpse surface rising topics 12–18 months before saturation. (Exploding Topics)
- 741 mapping: the skill's live Reddit/forum/blog search (`firecrawl_search`) is the no-cost version of the forum-mining step; DataForSEO PAA/related questions is the equivalent of the Answer-The-Public/Answer-Socrates step. Neither tool name is shown to a client.

## G. Which questions to answer first
- Start from the top 20 % highest-value customers: their traits and problems, even if total views are lower. (How to become rich with social media; Hormozi)
- Prioritize questions that recur on sales calls — the video answers become a sales-asset library sent between calls. (Hormozi)
- Prefer questions that can be answered with a save-worthy artifact: named model, benchmark table, checklist, step-by-step. (LinkedIn 2026 reports)
- Niche specificity over broad overviews (a specific treatment or procedure, not "what is physiotherapy"). (Deep Research Report)

## H. Involving the owner or staff on camera (process question 5)
- "How I / how we" framing from the person who did the work; sharing real operational facts is unfalsifiable and builds rapport. (Hormozi; 1.2M Followers)
- ACCCM loop: do the work in the business → talk on camera about what you just did → do bigger work. Documented outcomes beat theory. (Hormozi)
- Damaging admission up front ("our parking is terrible, but our sessions are the most effective") — owning a real deficit makes every later claim more believable. (14 Years of Marketing Advice)
- SPCL on camera: Status (real results/scarce resources), Power (advice that worked when followed), Credibility (verifiable third-party proof), Likeness (the speaker's own personality, not scripted corporate lines). (If I Wanted To Grow An Audience; My Actual Social Media Strategy)
- Talk-to-camera plus visual amplifiers (props, TV screen, whiteboard, graphic overlays) explain complex points faster than speech alone. (Alex Garcia 4 Formats)
- Keep natural pacing, minor stutters, unscripted pauses — viewers trust real employees and owners far more than faceless brands, AI scripts or avatars. (Social Media Marketing 2027)

## I. Cadence, penalties, testing (for the board's "how to run it" block)
- Cadence: LinkedIn 3–5/week (daily triggers fatigue/quality filters; consistency over 60+ days compounds). Instagram Reels 3–5 high-depth pieces/week, max 1–2/day. TikTok tolerates high frequency (5–10/day when testing volume) but quality rules still apply. YouTube Shorts 3–5/week. (LinkedIn 2026 reports; StoryChief; Deep Research Report)
- Penalties the notebook cites: outbound link in post text −48 % to −68 % reach (LinkedIn, X), up to −80 %; X first-tweet link −8× weight. Unedited generic AI copy −57 % reach on LinkedIn. Under 3 s dwell → clickbait downrank, up to −50 % views. LinkedIn 5+ hashtags −29 %, 10+ hashtags −30 to −50 %; X 3+ hashtags → spam filter. Engagement pods detected at 97 %. (Ghost 360Brew; ZoomSphere; Allgood; X algorithm 2026)
- Testing a format before scaling: the 2-of-3 readiness rule (ops, quality, team skill at 2× volume in 30 days; 2 of 3 → scale now, 1 of 3 → 30–60 days of infrastructure first). Ship 4–6 hook variants of the same body per platform; judge after 24–48 h; the signal is the 3-second hook rate — kill anything under 20–25 %. (Alex Garcia 4 Formats; Deep Research Report; UGC Copilot)

## J. On-camera delivery and speaking (added 2026-10-26)
Source: "give me 7 minutes, I'll change the way you speak on YouTube" (AlexanderTheCreate, youtube.com/watch?v=up1xuwn_euk), source #39 in the Content Marketing Playbook notebook. Five real mistakes, each with the fix — not hook/structure, but how the person on camera actually talks.

- **The coffee-shop rule.** Say the line the way you'd say it to a friend sitting across a coffee-shop table. If it would feel strange said out loud to one specific person, it reads as stiff and scripted on camera too. Check every script line this way before recording.
- **Connecting beats perfecting.** Trying to be flawless on camera reads as stiff and kills connection. Aim to connect with the viewer, not to perform without error — the mindset shift shows up in tone and body language on its own, without forcing gestures.
- **Avoid "emotional spaghetti."** Decide the one feeling the viewer should leave with (inspired, taught something, motivated) before recording. A creator who doesn't know that themselves produces a video the viewer can't follow either, and they click off.
- **Points need paint.** A bare technical statement goes over the viewer's head. Wrapping the same point in an analogy, story, or example the viewer already understands is what makes it land — not just saying it correctly.
- **Don't go in cold.** A few minutes of physical warm-up (shaking out body and voice) plus a short mental visualization of the outcome raises on-camera energy and delivery quality versus starting straight from stillness.

## K. Upload volume and the "algorithm penalty" myth (added 2026-10-27)
Source: 34 new sources Martin added 2026-10-27 (73 total in the notebook), mostly one creator's "daily upload" video series plus one Instagram-growth case. Covers posting volume specifically — a gap the earlier three queries did not cover.

- No algorithm penalty for high-frequency or daily uploads. The sources reject the idea that platforms "steal views" from older videos or suppress reach when a creator posts daily. More frequent posting adds surface area for attention; it does not divide the audience between videos. (Daily-upload volume videos)
- "Quality-quantity" beats a few polished pieces. Moving from 1-2 heavily scripted videos a month to 3-7 videos a week (or daily, at lower effort per video) raised total views, leads and sales well above the slower pace. In one documented case, the view count per video stayed about the same. The weekly total rose by roughly four times. (Daily-upload volume videos)
- Volume finds the "audience key" faster. Each post is a try at a packaging angle, title or topic. A creator posting at high volume finds what resonates in weeks; a creator posting rarely takes years to find the same thing. (Daily-upload volume videos)
- The "warm pool" effect. Frequent posting turns cold, one-time viewers into regular "warm" viewers who watch more and buy more readily, instead of depending on occasional viral spikes from strangers who never return. (Daily-upload volume videos)
- Long-form exposure compounds faster, per piece, than short-form. Two one-hour long-form videos give a viewer roughly the same brand exposure as about 480 individual 15-second Shorts. Long-form is the faster route to deep trust with one viewer; short-form needs many more touches for the same effect. (Daily-upload volume videos)
- Short-form platforms tolerate real daily volume — 5-10 posts a day without fatiguing the audience, because they run on an interest graph, not a social graph. This is well above section I's general cadence guidance, which covers steady posting, not active testing. Newsfeed-style platforms (LinkedIn, Facebook) still cap useful frequency at 1-2 posts a day. (Daily-upload volume videos)
- The "Trial Reels" testing method: treat short-form as a lab, not a gallery. Test 5 different executions of the same core idea on non-followers before committing production budget to one version — the concrete version of section I's "ship variants, judge after 24-48h" rule. One documented case grew from 10K to 800K followers in 78 days this way. (Daily-upload volume videos)
- A valid test changes the spoken hook, the body's main points, AND the text overlay together. Platforms detect and penalize a re-upload of the same video body with only the on-screen text changed. (Daily-upload volume videos)
- Name the series. A one-off viral hit converts far fewer viewers into followers than the same idea released as "Episode 1" of a named, continuing series. In one documented pair, the standalone video out-viewed the series version (3.4M vs 2.4M views) but the series version brought in more than three times the followers (57,000 vs 17,000). Every recurring format on a board should carry a series name, not just a topic. (Daily-upload volume videos)

## L. On-camera speaking without a script — more techniques (added 2026-10-27)
Source: the same new batch as K. Expands section J's five rules with concrete structuring and physical techniques, not just mindset advice.

- Map the territory, don't script sentences. Before recording, sketch the relationships, arrows or a flow diagram between the key ideas, so the speaker holds a mental map of where the talk is going, not memorized lines.
- The 4-beat intro: script only the first 20-30 seconds, then go off-the-cuff. Confirm (validate the title's promise immediately), Context (a fresh fact or frame that opens a curiosity loop), Credibility (one quick, verifiable proof point), Curiosity (tease what staying to the end will give the viewer).
- Destination milestones, not transitions. Fix 3-5 visual or conceptual checkpoints and one punchy closing line per main point. Knowing the destination pulls the delivery toward it without memorized bridges.
- The lens eye-contact muscle, next to J's existing coffee-shop rule: hold continuous eye contact with the camera lens while thinking, instead of looking away or up to search for words. Treat eye contact as a trainable muscle, not a fixed trait.
- "Trying on conviction": when stating a claim with conviction feels fake, treat it as trying on a new piece of clothing for 10 minutes — an experiment, not an identity claim. This lowers the resistance to speaking plainly and directly.
- Clear physical tension before recording: breathwork, shaking out the body, or a short vocal warm-up. This frees up mental capacity and stops presenter stiffness. It is the concrete method behind J's existing "don't go in cold" rule.
- Physical hand gestures replace visual edits for abstract ideas. Bring an abstract idea into physical space between the fingers to signal precision, or give each point in a list its own space in front of the chest (point 1 left, point 2 centre, point 3 right). Both remove viewer confusion without adding graphics in post.
- Name the internal method instead of scripting the advice. A battle-tested, named proprietary technique needs no script, because the speaker already knows it by heart — naming it also makes it quotable and memorable for the viewer.
