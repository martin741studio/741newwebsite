# Outlier content notes — mechanism research for the business-account topic grill

Internal only. Captures what actually makes a piece of content in 741's own niche and adjacent ones (SEO / local-business marketing / AI search / social-platform growth) perform far above normal, by studying real outlier examples — not a generic "best practices" list. Feeds the business-account topic grill (`SKILL.md` §"Topic grill") as a research input, alongside real search-demand signals. Never a source to quote or copy — only a mechanism to adapt honestly into 741's own, verifiable claims (`claims-need-real-data`).

## Confirmed pattern: contrarian-obsolescence + authority stat + replacement checklist

Example: "Your Website Won't Matter in 3 Years (Here's What to Build Instead)", Phlash Consulting (home-service marketing agency channel), `youtube.com/watch?v=xAIeA3ewRbo` — 449,010 views, 12,555 likes (checked 2026-10-27). Flagged by Martin directly as a massive outlier.

Shape, in order:
1. Names the thing the audience has already invested heavily in (their website) and declares it obsolete on a short, concrete timeline ("3 years") — high stakes plus specificity, not a vague trend claim.
2. Opens with one hard, attributed statistic in the first 15 seconds (a named Google executive's quote; "58% of searches end with no click") — credibility before the claim can read as mere opinion.
3. Old-way-vs-new-way framing, two clean phases — the same "what people assume / what's actually true" shape Martin's own `build_personal_mechanism.py` visual already uses for the personal account.
4. Ends on a concrete, numbered checklist (publish pricing content, audit the booking tool, document real customer questions, build a knowledge catalog, optimize for AI citations) — a save-worthy artifact, not just an opinion (`content-format-board/references/playbook-notes.md` §G already ranks checklist-shaped answers highest).
5. A damaging admission against the creator's own industry ("don't pay $1,500/month for AI-slop blog posts") — insider honesty, not a sales pitch.

Pattern-check across the same niche (search run 2026-10-27, confirms this is not a one-off from one creator): "SEO in 2025: How I'd Learn It If I Were Starting Over" (Ahrefs channel) 568,589 views; "What the 'New AI Google' Means for SEO…" (Matt Diggity) 112,387 views; "Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) Explained Like You're 5" (Vendasta) 45,770 views. The "the old way is dying, here is the concrete replacement" framing recurs across multiple channels in this exact niche and consistently outperforms generic how-to content.

## Confirmed pattern #2: algorithm/mechanism-reveal ("how it actually works now")

Confirmed 2026-10-27 while researching content for a broader marketer/creator audience (not just local-business owners). Search for Instagram-algorithm explainer videos returned a remarkably consistent outlier cluster, far above the niche's normal range: "How Social Media Algorithms Actually Work (And How to Beat Them)" (Kallaway) 1,203,288 views; "The NEW Way To Beat Instagram's Algorithm in 2026" (heyDominik) 393,799 views; "What's Actually Working on Instagram in 2026" (Think Media Podcast) 166,452 views; several more channels (Robert Benjamin, Build Your Tribe, Sunny Gala) consistently in the 25K-100K range with the same angle, well above generic how-to-grow content in the same space.

Shape, in order:
1. Names the real, current mechanism behind a platform's ranking/distribution ("how it actually works right now," not a generic tip list) — curiosity plus a promise of real insider understanding.
2. Explicitly debunks a common wrong belief first ("more posting," "better editing") before stating the real lever — the same contrast-with-the-assumed-cause shape as Martin's own `build_personal_mechanism.py` visual and the personal-posts "first-principles" step (5a root mechanism).
3. Grounds the claim in something verifiable now (a platform's own stated change, a leak, an observed pattern), not timeless generic advice — "what changed," dated.
4. Ends with what to actually do differently, tied directly to the mechanism just explained, not a separate generic checklist.

This is a strong match for 741's own voice (`voice-profile-business.md`: direct, mechanism-first, "states the real mechanism, not just the critique") — explaining the real mechanism behind a platform or a market dynamic, rather than listing generic tips, is already how this account is supposed to sound.

## Confirmed pattern #3: teach-the-exact-build ("steal this / build this once")

Confirmed 2026-10-29, Martin's own vidIQ Research outlier list for 741 Studio's niche (`app.vidiq.com/research/explore`, "For You" tab, keywords ai seo / agency / local business marketing). Real data, checked live: "Build a Local SEO Lead Magnet in 15 Minutes (Free Tutorial)" (Merchynt, 24k subs) 167K views, >100x, 3mo old; "Build This Once → Sell It to Every Local Business ($297/Mo)" (Adam Erhart, 732k subs) 181K views, 25x, 4mo old; "How to Build 1,000 AI Microsites and Dominate an Entire Industry" (Jesse Cunningham, 120k subs) 178K views, 27x, 2mo old; "It Should Be ILLEGAL To Build Microsites Like This" (James Ranks, only 1.1k subs) 24K views, 30x — the smallest channel in the set, but the highest relative outlier multiple.

Shape, in order:
1. Names ONE concrete, narrow build (a lead magnet, a microsite, a photo trick), never an abstract strategy — the title promises a replicable artifact, not an idea.
2. A speed or scale claim sits right in the hook ("15 Minutes", "1,000 Microsites", "Build This Once") — makes the payoff feel both fast and disproportionate to the effort.
3. Several titles add a transgressive or taboo framing on top of the build ("It Should Be ILLEGAL") — this is what pushes a small channel (1.1k subs) to a 30x outlier multiple, well past what the build-tutorial framing alone would get.
4. Often ties the build directly to money in the title itself ("$297/Mo", "Sell It to Every Local Business") — the artifact isn't just useful, it's explicitly a sellable unit.

Close cousin, same list: "Chris' Dental Growth Hack: The Google Business Profile Photo Hack" (KickStart Dental Marketing, 26k subs) 45K views, >100x — same build-the-exact-thing shape, scoped to one named person's one specific tactic rather than a general build.

This is the strongest match yet for 741's own "give away the exercise, not just the idea" pillar (`voice-profile-business.md` pillar 1) — it is literally the same move: hand over a concrete, buildable artifact rather than a tip.

## Mandatory step before writing: pull the real transcript

Confirmed 2026-10-29, hard rule (Martin, after a rejected carousel built from an invented summary instead of the real source). Before writing ANY post text from a picked outlier video, pull its real transcript first and build the content from what is actually said, not from a guessed summary of the thumbnail or title.

Steps:
1. Confirm the exact video and URL (`firecrawl_search`, then `firecrawl_scrape` with `alexandria: {provider: "youtube-com", capability: "videos/video_details", options: {video_id: "..."}}` to verify the match).
2. Pull the transcript: `mcp__Apify__call-actor` with `actor: "johnvc/YoutubeTranscripts"`, input `{"youtube_url": "<url>", "languages": ["en"], "output_formats": ["text"]}` (field name is `youtube_url`, not `start_urls`).
3. Read the result with `mcp__Apify__get-dataset-items` (`fields: "text,title,channel_name,view_count"`, `clean: true`).
4. Mine the real transcript for the actual strong material: concrete examples, the clearest one-line framing, named concrete steps. Translate/adapt into German in 741's own voice, grounded in 741's own proof point, never a thin restatement of the title.

This is the same pattern already used for `content-format-library` (see `.claude/skills/content-format-board/SKILL.md`, "the source transcript (internal, never cited to a client)") — it already existed in the codebase before this session, just not written into this file. Never cite the transcript or its channel/creator in any published post (`no-method-sources-in-deliverables`).

## How to use this — never copy the claim itself

Adapt the SHAPE, not the specific claim. Name something 741's own audience already relies on, state only what 741 can actually back with a real, checkable source or its own tracked work (`claims-need-real-data` — no "in 3 years" or similar unless that is a real, sourced forecast), then hand over a concrete checklist instead of leaving the reader anxious with nothing to act on. Never name Ahrefs, Matt Diggity, Vendasta, Phlash Consulting, Kallaway, heyDominik, Merchynt, Adam Erhart, Jesse Cunningham, James Ranks, KickStart Dental Marketing, Chris Heidlebaugh, ProfileTree Digital, or any other creator in a published post (`no-method-sources-in-deliverables`) — this file is internal reference only.
