# Voice profile — Martin's personal posts

Built from one live interview (2026-10-22), not from old posts. Martin has never posted before, so this file is the only source of tone. Treat every rule here as provisional until Martin corrects it on a real draft; the first corrections update this file (house rule: patch the skill, not a one-off reply).

## Core philosophy (Martin's content intelligence brief, 2026-10-22)

Question the narrative. Understand the incentives. Observe the behavior. Think for yourself. The central move across most posts: the gap between a socially accepted narrative and the actual behavior underneath it — explored honestly, without assuming the gap means everyone involved is lying. Full source context in `references/content-intelligence-brief.md`.

## Core stance

- Contempt for corporate-platitude dishonesty. Example target: "we're all a family."
- Belief: competition and direct confrontation produce more real freedom and empathy than performative niceness.
- Prefers an uncomfortable truth over a comfortable narrative.
- Willing to be hard or polarizing. The post can make some readers push back.

## What the voice sounds like

- States the claim first, then the reasoning. Not: build-up, then reveal.
- Names the thing it is against by its real shape ("we're a family" line), not a vague gesture at it.
- Draws a line between the surface claim and what is actually true underneath.
- Short, declarative sentences mixed with one longer sentence that carries the real argument.
- Ends on the stance, not on a summary or a call to engage ("thoughts?", "agree?").
- **"How I" not "How to"** (`references/strategy-notes.md`): speak from what Martin did and saw, never from prescribing what the reader should do. "Here's what I did and what I saw" over "here's what you should do" — the first is unfalsifiable and builds rapport, the second reads as lecturing.
- **Negative/contrarian openings outperform positive framing** and match the voice already — lean into it, do not soften a sharp opening into a positive-sounding one.
- A real, specific number in the opening line beats a vague version of the same claim — another reason the claim-proof pairing rule (C8.4 below) matters beyond just honesty.

## Hard no's (Martin's own limits, do not soften these into "safe")

- No empty motivational-poster lines: "success is a journey", "hustle harder", "trust the process".
- No false-modesty humblebrag framing: "I didn't want to share this, but...", "I almost didn't post this".
- No invented or exaggerated numbers. Every number real and sourced (`claims-need-real-data`).
- No hedge-everything corporate softening ("in my humble opinion", "no offense to anyone, but"). State the view straight.
- No forced engagement bait ("comment YES if you agree", "tag someone who needs this").
- No throat-clearing openers: "the truth is", "to be honest", "honestly". State the claim; do not announce that you are about to be honest about it. (Found in the 4.2 functional test: a first-pass LinkedIn draft scored 4/5 on the slop lint for exactly this.)
- No fake luxury entrepreneurship ("day in the life of a 7-figure founder" posturing) and no forced controversy. Forced controversy is a manufactured hot take with nothing real behind it; the voice's willingness to be polarizing above is earned (a real contradiction, a real stance), never manufactured for engagement.
- No exaggerated certainty. A strong opinion stated plainly is the voice; a claim dressed up as more certain or more universal than Martin actually believes it is not.
- No invented personal stories or experiences, ever. See "Accuracy rules" below — this is the single most important addition from the 2026-10-22 content intelligence brief.

## Style defaults (not a hard rule, applied unless it kills the voice)

- Avoid stacking multiple em-dashes in one post; split into separate sentences instead. Reason: it is both a slop-lint tell and a cadence that reads as written-for-you rather than said-by-you.
- One real example or number per post where the topic allows it, never a stacked list of three.
- Keep contractions and the occasional sentence fragment. A rough edge reads as a person, not a caption generator.
- **No outbound links in the post body, ever.** If a link matters, it goes in the first comment, never the post text itself (`references/strategy-notes.md`; `scripts/check_lengths.py` warns if a draft contains a bare URL). This is unrelated to the no-auto-post rule — it is a drafting rule about where a link lives, not permission to post one.
- A real, owned flaw or limitation stated plainly (not a hedge) can open or anchor a post — it reads as more credible than a claim with no acknowledged edge. Optional, not required on every post.

## Accuracy rules (Martin's content intelligence brief, 2026-10-22 — critical, applies before every draft)

Before drafting from Martin's raw thought or from anything stated about him in a prior conversation, classify it as one of: a **confirmed personal experience** (he lived it, stated as fact), an **explicitly expressed opinion** (he said this is what he thinks), an **interpretation inferred from conversation** (a reasonable read, not his own words), or a **hypothesis for content exploration** (an idea to test, not a claim at all). Never present an inferred opinion or a hypothesis as Martin's confirmed belief or lived experience in a published post. A post built on anything but a confirmed experience or an explicitly expressed opinion needs Martin's confirmation before it ships — fold this into step 3 (claim check): the check covers personal narrative accuracy, not only numbers.

The personal-background and personality-trait notes in `references/content-intelligence-brief.md` are context for calibrating tone, not pre-written claims — never turn an "observed personality characteristic" from that file into a first-person anecdote Martin never actually told you.

## Editorial method (optional technique, strongest fit for pillar-1 "uncomfortable truths" posts)

For a contradiction-style post, work through: the accepted narrative -> the potential contradiction -> the incentive or mechanism behind it -> a real example or personal experience (accuracy rules apply) -> an alternative interpretation or counterargument -> a memorable, honest hook -> a clear takeaway or open question. This is the shape the "we're all a family" test post already follows by instinct; use it deliberately for pillar-1 posts, not as a rigid template for every pillar (a pillar-7 personal story or a pillar-6 discipline post does not need a "narrative vs. contradiction" structure forced onto it).

## Braindumps and the complaint-vs-insight rule (Martin's own words, 2026-10-22)

Martin wants a place to dump raw, unprocessed thinking (`scripts/braindump.py`) without every dump being treated as post-ready. His own limit, stated directly: he does not want to put out posts that just read as complaints.

A real frustration or irritation can be the SEED of a pillar-1 or pillar-2 post, but the post itself has to land on an actual mechanism or insight, not just vent the frustration. The test: strip the emotion out of the raw dump and ask what is left. If what is left is still a real claim about how something works (a pattern, an incentive, a mechanism) -> it is distillable, run it through the editorial method above. If what is left is only "this annoys me" with no underlying claim -> it is not ready; say so plainly rather than dressing a complaint up as an insight.

## First-principles note (Martin's own words, 2026-10-22 — the real purpose of this workflow)

The point of this skill is not only to produce posts. Martin wants the process itself to help him actually understand the topic — first-principles thinking — and for the published post to carry that understanding to readers, not just a hook. His words: "I need you in this workflow to help me to understand better... with putting it online and posting we also help others to learn this."

So every angle-generation step (SKILL.md step 5) opens with a short first-principles note, written out and shown to Martin BEFORE the hook options, not folded invisibly into them:

1. **Root mechanism.** Why does the pattern actually happen — the real incentive or cause underneath it, not just a restatement that it happens.
2. **Cross-domain translation, especially to business.** Where else the same mechanism shows up — business is Martin's own primary meaning-making domain, so this is not optional decoration, it is the point. If the same mechanism already appears in a business-account post or pillar, say so explicitly (it may turn into a second, business-account angle on the same underlying insight).

Only after this note does the usual set of hook angles follow. The note is working material Martin reads and can correct — it is itself part of "distilling an inferred interpretation into a confirmed claim" (Accuracy rules), not a finished claim on its own.

**Workflow for a raw dump (not a single clear thought):** save it first (`scripts/braindump.py add personal "<text>"`, status starts `raw`), then distill it before offering angles — do not skip straight to step 4 (angle generation) on raw, unprocessed text. Confirm the distilled claim with Martin (it is very likely an "interpretation inferred from conversation" at this stage, not yet his "explicitly expressed opinion" — accuracy rules apply in full). Once he confirms the distilled claim, mark the dump `distilled` and proceed to normal angle generation from that confirmed claim, not from the raw text.

## Headline-prompt / copy-rules reuse (Martin's go, 2026-10-22)

Draft the opening line of every post the way the DR hero formula builds a headline, scaled down to one sentence instead of a page hero. Source: `~/.claude/skills/content-writing/references/copy-rules.md` (741 Content-Raster). Read directly, never call `/content-write` — that skill is scoped to client-voice page and blog copy.

- **C2.1 pattern, adapted:** the opening line states the claim plainly, then the post gives the one reason to believe it (not three). Never "here's a thought" or "something I've been thinking about" as an opener.
- **C2.8 no dashes:** stack of em-dashes reads as written-for-you; split into separate sentences instead.
- **C4.1 scanability:** short sentences, 2 sentences per paragraph at most, one idea per paragraph.
- **C4.5 / slop gate:** if it sounds like AI or marketing copy, rewrite in the words Martin actually uses. `slopmonster/tools/deslop.py` is the automated check for this (step 6 of the workflow).
- **C8.4 claim-proof pairing:** a stated number or result sits next to its source in the same post, never floating unattributed.

## Per-platform adaptation (same core take, different shape)

- **LinkedIn:** full argument, 3-6 short paragraphs. The platform people expect reasoning from. No hashtags.
- **X/Twitter:** the sharpest single version of the claim, under 280 characters where possible; if the argument needs more, a short thread (2-4 posts), first post standalone.
- **Instagram caption:** shorter than LinkedIn, pairs with the image; the line that would work as a pull-quote on the image goes first.
- **Facebook caption:** close to the Instagram length and tone, slightly more conversational opening line; same image.

See `platform-specs.md` for the checkable length rules.
