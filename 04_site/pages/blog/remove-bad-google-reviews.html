Understanding Google's Review Policy

Google does not remove reviews simply because they are negative. However, they will permanently delete reviews that violate their **Prohibited & Restricted Content Policy**. If a review contains any of the following, it is eligible for removal:

  • Harassment & Hate Speech: Content targeting specific individuals, employees, or containing offensive language.
  • Conflict of Interest: Reviews written by former employees, direct competitors, or the business owner themselves.
  • Spam & Fake Content: Automated review bombing, repetitive postings, or content that does not represent a genuine customer experience.
  • Off-Topic Content: Comments focusing on political rants, social commentary, or issues unrelated to the business itself.

Step-by-Step Guide to Dispute Fake Reviews

Step 1: Flag the Review inside Your Dashboard

The first step is to officially report the review. Log into your Google Business Profile dashboard, navigate to the reviews section, locate the offensive comment, and click the three dots icon to select "Report Review". Select the specific policy violation that applies.

Step 2: Escalate via the Google Business Profile Help Community

If the automated flag is rejected (which happens frequently), you can submit your case to the GBP Help Community. Here, Product Experts can review your evidence and escalate valid policy violations directly to Google’s engineering support team. Be sure to provide concrete proof, such as evidence that the reviewer name does not exist in your customer database, or that a competitor is behind the account.

Step 3: Submit a Legal Removal Request

In cases of defamation or competitor attacks that damage your business income, you can submit a legal request to Google. In Australia, businesses are protected by strict defamation laws, and Google can be held liable as a publisher if they fail to act after being notified. Submitting a formal **Google Legal Removal Request** is a highly effective way to bypass standard support channels.
