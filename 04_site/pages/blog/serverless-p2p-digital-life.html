Escaping the Big 5: Building a Serverless, Apocalypse-Proof Digital Life

By Martin Drendel • July 7, 2026 • 7 min read

Almost every app you use today lives on rented land. Whether it is AWS, Google Cloud, or Azure, the modern internet is built on a handful of centralized cloud providers that quietly sit between you and your own data. For businesses, that dependency is a recurring line item. For individuals, it is a silent surrender of ownership. And for both, it is a single point of failure waiting to happen.

The good news is that a different architecture has matured to the point of being production-ready. Peer-to-peer (P2P), serverless technologies now let developers ship real applications with no intermediary servers at all — and let people own their digital lives outright. This is not a fringe experiment. It is a practical path to data sovereignty and censorship resistance.

The Flaw at the Center of the Cloud

Centralized infrastructure created enormous convenience, but it also concentrated risk. When your application depends on a single provider, you inherit every weakness of that provider. Data breaches expose the personal information of millions in one incident. Corporate tracking turns your usage patterns into someone else's business model. And because everything funnels through one place, that place becomes a single point of failure — one outage, one policy change, or one subpoena can take a service offline or hand over its contents.

Censorship is the sharpest edge of this problem. If there is a central server to pressure, there is a lever to pull. The architecture itself makes control possible. To escape that, you have to remove the middle entirely.

"Because there are no centralized servers to breach, subpoena, or take offline, a peer-to-peer app gives you something the cloud never could: total ownership. Your data never leaves your device unless you choose to share it."

The P2P Solution: Pear Runtime

Pear Runtime is an open-source, peer-to-peer live-data protocol that lets developers build applications with zero infrastructure costs. Instead of routing traffic through a server you have to rent, provision, and secure, Pear connects users directly to one another. There is no intermediary in the middle of the conversation.

The consequences of that design are significant. Your data never leaves your device unless you explicitly share it. There is no central database to breach, no logs to subpoena, and no server to take offline. Instead of scaling by paying for more machines, Pear apps scale infinitely by leveraging the bandwidth of the peers themselves — every new user adds capacity rather than cost.

Why This Matters for Businesses

For B2B teams, the appeal is direct. Infrastructure is one of the largest and least predictable costs in software. A zero-infrastructure architecture removes an entire category of operational overhead: no servers to patch, no scaling bills that balloon with success, and dramatically less surface area for security incidents. If there is nothing central to breach, the breach never happens. For teams building privacy-first products, that is not just a cost saving — it is a competitive advantage.

Why This Matters for Individuals

For individuals, the value is ownership. Your files, messages, and calls stay on your devices and move directly to the people you trust. Nobody is mining your behavior to sell ads, and nobody can quietly revoke your access. In a world where "apocalypse-proof" is only half a joke, a system that keeps working without any corporate cloud behind it is genuine resilience.

The Core Components of the Ecosystem

Pear Runtime does not stand alone. It sits on top of a small, focused set of tools that make peer-to-peer development practical across every kind of device.

  • ✓ Bare: A lightweight JavaScript runtime built specifically for P2P — an alternative to NodeJS that runs an identical peer-to-peer stack across phones, laptops, and even smartwatches. Write once, and the same code runs everywhere your users are.
  • ✓ Keet: Pear's flagship application — serverless messaging and video calling that requires no signup, exposes no data, and supports secure large-file sharing directly between peers. It is the living proof that P2P is ready for everyday use.
  • ✓ Pear Runtime: The open-source, live-data protocol that ties it together, letting developers build and distribute apps with zero infrastructure and total data sovereignty.

Keet in Practice

Keet is the clearest demonstration of what this architecture makes possible. There is no account to create, because there is no server to hold an account. Your messages and calls travel encrypted, peer to peer, and large files move directly between participants instead of being uploaded to someone else's cloud. It behaves like the messaging apps you already know — but without the data collection, the signup friction, or the central point of failure hiding behind them.

Building Your Serverless Future

The shift away from the Big 5 is not about rejecting the cloud out of principle. It is about matching architecture to what you actually need. If your priority is data sovereignty, censorship resistance, and predictable costs, a serverless P2P stack is now a legitimate — and increasingly attractive — foundation. Because the technology is built on JavaScript and open standards, teams already fluent in the modern web can adopt it without starting over.
